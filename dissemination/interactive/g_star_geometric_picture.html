The Geometry of G* — the Same Z/4 Symmetry, Three Times

G* is the analytic constant that registers the square-ness of one specific elliptic curve.
Three pictures of the same fact: a figure-8 in the plane, a square lattice in ℂ, an arc-length integral.

The lemniscate r² = cos(2θ)
A figure-8 curve in the plane. 4-fold rotational symmetry: rotating by 90° maps the curve to itself, swapping the two lobes.
Arc length of one quarter (highlighted) = ϖ/2 ≈ 1.3110
Total arc length = 2ϖ ≈ 5.2441
The Gaussian-integer lattice ℤ[i] ⊂ ℂ
Integers and i-multiples in the complex plane. The four units {1, i, −1, −i} are the corners of the unit square at the origin. Same 4-fold rotational symmetry: multiplication by i rotates by 90°.
The four units (red dots) are the orbit of 1 under multiplication by i:
1 → i → −1 → −i → 1

Deform the period lattice — only τ = i is square

Every elliptic curve over ℂ has a period parallelogram generated by 1 and a complex number τ with Im(τ) > 0. The shape of this parallelogram determines the curve's symmetry. Almost all τ give a generic curve with only Z/2 symmetry (negation). The single value τ = i gives the unique square parallelogram with full Z/4 symmetry — the lemniscatic case. G* lives only here.

τ = 0.000 + 1.000i Z/4 symmetric (square!)

The rotated copy coincides with the original iff τ = i.

The bridge: arc length = period = Γ(1/4)

The geometry on the left (figure-8 in the plane) and the geometry on the right (square lattice in ℂ) are the same elliptic curve seen two ways. The bridge is an integral.

What G* IS, geometrically:

  • The numerical signature of a torus whose period lattice is a perfect square (rather than a generic rectangle).
  • The arc-length-derived constant of the unique plane curve with full 4-fold rotational symmetry in this analytic family (the figure-8 lemniscate).
  • The ratio of two specific Γ-values Γ(1/4)/Γ(3/4), which by the reflection identity equals Γ(1/4)² / (π√2) — i.e., a square (the Γ(1/4)²) divided by a diagonal-times-side (the π√2).

Why this is not a Möbius strip

A Möbius strip has Z/2 monodromy: walk around once, come back flipped. The geometric object underlying G* has Z/4 monodromy: walk around once, you've rotated by 90°; four trips bring you back. Z/4 contains Z/2 as a subgroup, so the Möbius intuition catches the parity-flip piece (even ↔ odd Dirichlet character) but misses the 90°-rotation full structure.

Topologically the relevant manifold is a torus T² (orientable, two-sided), not a Möbius strip (non-orientable, one-sided). What's special is not the topology — every elliptic curve is a torus — but the geometry of which torus: this one has a square period lattice with a 90° rotational automorphism. That square-ness is what G* is the analytic shadow of.
