\begin{abstract}
Galileo founded timekeeping on isochronism: a harmonic clock's period ignores
its amplitude, and the constant governing it is $\pi$. Exactly at a symmetric
instability threshold --- a column at critical load, a mode at a supercritical
pitchfork --- the quadratic restoring term vanishes and the generic surviving
dynamics is the \emph{pure quartic} oscillator: the anti-pendulum, whose
frequency is proportional to its own amplitude, with period constant
$\Gstar=\Gamma(\tfrac14)/\Gamma(\tfrac34)=2.9586751\ldots$, the lemniscate's
analogue of the circle constant. We present the timekeeping of marginal
stability as an exactly solvable, experimentally ready subject. Beyond the
classical period law $T=\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gstar\sqrt{m/2\lambda}/A$, we show that
the harmonic--quartic--double-well crossover collapses, for every symmetric
orbit at detunings of either sign, onto one universal curve
$T\,A\sqrt{2\lambda/m}=4kK(k)$, with the elliptic modulus
$k^{2}=2\lambda A^{2}/(\mu+4\lambda A^{2})$ as the scaling variable and the
quartic point at exactly $k^{2}=\tfrac12$; and we derive a closed form for a
calibration-free waveform functional along the same crossover,
$\mathcal{B}(k)$, which descends from the sinusoid value $2$ through the
lemniscatic value $48\pi/\Gstar^{4}$ with zero adjustable parameters. The
pair $(4kK,\mathcal B)$ turns amplitude-dependent timing --- a defect for a
clock --- into a precision meter of the distance to threshold. The same
structure governs the edge in \emph{equilibrium}: all thermodynamics
collapses onto one thermal detuning variable $\gamma=\mu\sqrt{\beta/\lambda}$,
with $\langle x^{2}\rangle=\sqrt{k_BT/\lambda}\,/\Gstar$ at threshold, a
$\tfrac34 k_B$ equipartition deficit, and a thermal noise lineshape whose
peak obeys $f_{\mathrm{peak}}\propto T^{1/4}$ --- a third, dynamics-free
measurement channel. Quantum mechanically the normal form reduces once more
to a single detuning $g=\mu(2m)^{1/3}/(\lambda\hbar)^{2/3}$: the pitchfork
is rounded within an $\hbar$-width, the spectrum follows a WKB $\Gstar$-law
$E_n\propto(n+\tfrac12)^{4/3}$ with a widening-ladder spacing ratio
$(E_2-E_1)/(E_1-E_0)=1.335$ measurable in quarton-class circuits, and
thermal freeze-out lands exactly on the classical edge values. A two-spring
tabletop realization forbids every \emph{odd} term by symmetry, leaving a
single micrometer tuning to cancel the quadratic, and end-to-end
simulations of that apparatus, including its
dominant systematics, recover $\Gstar$ to $0.3\%$ with no fitted scale and
trace both universal curves. An interactive in-browser laboratory
accompanies the paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{The anti-pendulum}
\label{sec:intro}

Every introductory course teaches one clock. The harmonic oscillator's period
$T=2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$ is independent of amplitude, and that isochronism ---
noticed by Galileo for small swings, and engineered into exactness by Huygens,
who knew the circular pendulum's isochronism is only approximate and built the
cycloidal pendulum to repair it \cite{huygens} --- is why pendulums, balance
wheels, and $LC$ circuits keep time. The constant that converts the system
scales into a duration is $\pi$: the circle constant, because the phase-space
orbit of a harmonic oscillator is an ellipse traversed uniformly.

This paper is about the complementary clock, the one that lives where the
harmonic term \emph{vanishes}. Its restoring force is $F=-4\lambda x^{3}$, its
potential the flat-bottomed quartic $V=\lambda x^{4}$, and its period is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;T \;=\; \sqrt{\pi}\;\Gstar\,\sqrt{\frac{m}{2\lambda}}\;\frac{1}{A}\;,
\qquad
\Gstar \;=\; \frac{\Gamma(\tfrac14)}{\Gamma(\tfrac34)} \;=\; 2.958675119188639\ldots}
\label{eq:period}
\end{equation}
where $A$ is the turning-point amplitude. Two features deserve immediate
emphasis. First, the period \emph{depends on amplitude} --- frequency grows
linearly with $A$. This clock counts how hard it swings; it is the
anti-pendulum. Second, the role of $\pi$ has been taken over by a different
classical constant, $\Gstar$, which is to the \emph{lemniscate} what $\pi$ is
to the circle: writing $\varpi=2.622057\ldots$ for the lemniscate constant
(half the arc length of the unit lemniscate $r^{2}=\cos2\theta$), one has
$\Gstar = 2\varpi/\sqrt{\pi}$, so that $\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gstar$ \emph{is} the
total arc length of the unit lemniscate, precisely as $2\pi$ is the
circumference of the unit circle. The solution of the quartic oscillator is
the lemniscatic sine of Gauss and Abel, as the harmonic solution is the
circular sine; the mathematics descends directly from Fagnano's
eighteenth-century division of the lemniscatic arc and Gauss's
arithmetic--geometric mean \cite{ww,siegel}.

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.} None of the ingredients is new in
isolation, and we make no such claim: the quartic period integral is
classical \cite{ww,landau}; the Duffing oscillator's Jacobi-elliptic
solutions are textbook material \cite{byrd,nayfeh,kovacic}; intrinsically
nonlinear oscillators with amplitude-dependent periods have a pedagogical
literature of their own \cite{mohazzabi}, including exact Beta-function
periods for the $|x|^{p}$ family \cite{cveticanin} and the Fourier content
of the exact waveforms \cite{belendez}; and the very two-spring geometry
used below has had its \emph{statics} --- the pitchfork, its imperfections,
and its hysteresis --- analyzed in this journal \cite{ong,bissell}. Its
\emph{timekeeping}, to our knowledge, has not. What we believe is new here
is the \emph{composition}: the
identification of the elliptic modulus as the scaling variable that collapses
the entire threshold crossover onto one universal period curve
(Sec.~\ref{sec:exact}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:collapse}); the closed-form waveform
functional $\mathcal{B}(k)$ along that crossover
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:Bk}), giving a second, calibration-free universal curve; the
inversion of the clock's defect into a threshold-proximity meter
(Sec.~\ref{sec:sensor}); and a symmetric tabletop realization with a
simulation-validated, gate-declared measurement chain
(Secs.~\ref{sec:apparatus}--\ref{sec:validation}). We would welcome earlier
references for any of these and will gladly cite them.

Why should a physicist care about a clock nobody builds on purpose? Because
nature builds it at every \emph{symmetric instability threshold}, and
thresholds are everywhere.

\section{The threshold theorem}
\label{sec:threshold}

Consider any one-degree-of-freedom system with a reflection symmetry
$x\to-x$, smoothly tuned by a parameter $\mu$ through a symmetric (pitchfork)
instability at $\mu=0$:
\begin{equation}
V(x;\mu) \;=\; \tfrac12\,\mu\,x^{2} \;+\; \lambda\,x^{4} \;+\; O(x^{6}),
\qquad \lambda>0 .
\label{eq:normalform}
\end{equation}
For $\mu>0$ the well is harmonic; for $\mu<0$ it splits into a double well.
\emph{Exactly at threshold} the quadratic term is gone and the generic leading
behavior --- generic because the cubic is forbidden by symmetry, not by
tuning --- is the pure quartic. Equation~\eqref{eq:normalform} is the normal
form of a perfect Euler column at critical load (a supercritical,
stable-symmetric buckling instability) and of any order parameter at a
continuous symmetry-breaking transition treated at mean-field level
\cite{landau}. One caveat bounds the claim: the story requires
$\lambda>0$. In the \emph{subcritical} class ($\lambda<0$, stabilization
only at sixth order or by contact), the edge is a cliff rather than a
clock, and nothing below applies; ``universal'' here means universal within
the supercritical class.

The quartic oscillator is therefore not an exotic special case: it is
\emph{the clock of supercritical marginal stability}, the timekeeping that
remains when a mode has gone soft symmetrically.

\section{The crossover is exactly solvable}
\label{sec:exact}

The full period law across the threshold follows from one quadrature. With
turning point $A$ and energy $E=\tfrac12\mu A^{2}+\lambda A^{4}$,
\begin{equation}
T(A;\mu)
\;=\;
4\sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}\;
\frac{K(\kappa)}{\sqrt{\tfrac{\mu}{2}+2\lambda A^{2}}},
\qquad
\kappa^{2} \;=\; \frac{\lambda A^{2}}{\tfrac{\mu}{2}+2\lambda A^{2}},
\label{eq:elliptic}
\end{equation}
where $K$ is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind (modulus
convention). The limits close exactly: at $\mu=0$, $\kappa^{2}=\tfrac12$ and
$K(1/\sqrt2)=\Gamma(\tfrac14)^{2}/4\sqrt{\pi}$ reproduce
Eq.~\eqref{eq:period}; at $\lambda=0$, $K(0)=\pi/2$ gives $2\pi\sqrt{m/\mu}$.
The value $\kappa=1/\sqrt2$ is the \emph{self-dual modulus} --- the fixed
point of the exchange $k\leftrightarrow k'$ between a modulus and its
complement, where $K'=K$ --- and it is where every appearance of $\Gstar$ in
classical analysis originates; the reader may recognize it from the
$90^{\circ}$ pendulum, whose exact period is $4\sqrt{L/g}\,K(1/\sqrt2)$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:elliptic} is standard. The two statements we now extract
from it appear not to be, and they are the paper's central results.

\paragraph{Result 1: one curve carries the whole crossover.}
The exact orbit of Eq.~\eqref{eq:normalform} is a Jacobi elliptic cosine,
$x(t)=A\,\mathrm{cn}(\Omega t;k)$ with $\Omega^{2}=(\mu+4\lambda A^{2})/m$
and modulus
\begin{equation}
k^{2} \;=\; \frac{2\lambda A^{2}}{\mu+4\lambda A^{2}}
\label{eq:modulus}
\end{equation}
(the same $\kappa$ as above) \cite{byrd,nayfeh}. Rewriting
Eq.~\eqref{eq:elliptic} in terms of $k$ eliminates $\mu$ and $A$ separately
and leaves
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;T\,A\,\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{m}} \;=\; 4\,k\,K(k)\;}
\label{eq:collapse}
\end{equation}
--- a \emph{single universal curve} onto which every oscillation of every
detuning collapses: harmonic side ($k^{2}\!\to\!0$), the quartic point
(exactly $k^{2}=\tfrac12$, where $4kK=\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gstar$, recovering
Eq.~\eqref{eq:period}), and the double-well side ($\tfrac12<k^{2}<1$,
oscillations over the barrier), all on one axis. The modulus is the order
parameter of marginal timekeeping: measuring $(T,A)$ and knowing
$(\lambda,m)$ places a data point on Fig.~\ref{fig:crossover}a, and its
abscissa \emph{is} the dimensionless distance to threshold. The collapse in
Fig.~\ref{fig:crossover}a is demonstrated with full-geometry simulated
ringdowns at five micrometer settings spanning both sides of the edge; the
small departures visible at the largest $k^{2}$ are the apparatus's real
$x^{6}$ term, not a failure of Eq.~\eqref{eq:collapse}.

\paragraph{Result 2: the waveform functional in closed form.}
Parameterize one cycle by uniform phase $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$ and define the
scale-, amplitude-, and unit-invariant ratio
\begin{equation}
\mathcal B \;=\; \frac{2\,\langle x^{2}\rangle}{\langle (dx/d\theta)^{2}\rangle}.
\label{eq:B}
\end{equation}
A sinusoid gives $\mathcal B=2$ exactly. Averaging the cn orbit over a
period (Appendix~\ref{app:Bk}) gives the exact crossover curve
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\mathcal B(k) \;=\; \frac{3\pi^{2}}{2K^{2}}\;
\frac{E-(1-k^{2})K}{(1-k^{2})K+(2k^{2}-1)E}\;}
\label{eq:Bk}
\end{equation}
with $E=E(k)$ the complete elliptic integral of the second kind. Its limits:
$\mathcal B(0)=2$ (harmonic); at the quartic point,
$\mathcal B(1/\sqrt2)=48\pi/\Gstar^{4}=1.9678953151\ldots$, a pure number
$1.6\%$ below the sinusoid value. Equation~\eqref{eq:Bk} is a
\emph{zero-parameter} prediction: no scale, no calibration, no fit enters
either axis of Fig.~\ref{fig:crossover}b. The same construction on the
one-parameter family $V\propto|x|^{p}$ gives
$\mathcal B_{p}=p(p+2)\pi^{2}B(\tfrac3p,\tfrac12)/2B(\tfrac1p,\tfrac12)^{3}$
(Appendix~\ref{app:B}), so $\mathcal B$ also discriminates \emph{which}
power law a flat-bottomed well has: $\mathcal B_{4}=1.96790$ against
$\mathcal B_{6}=1.92399$ --- the sextic member is what a tricritical-like
threshold would show, and the quartic--sextic separation exceeds the entire
quartic--sinusoid gap by some $40\%$, so the discrimination is easier than
the detection.

\section{Signatures with no fitted parameters}
\label{sec:signatures}

The quartic clock offers unusually clean tests because its key signatures are
dimensionless or free of fitted scales. (We reserve ``calibration-free'' for
quantities like $\mathcal B$, where genuinely nothing must be measured
beforehand; the $\Gstar$ recovery below requires \emph{calibrated} $\lambda$
and $m$ but fits nothing.)

\paragraph{S1 --- the clock law.} On the quartic branch $T\cdot A$ is constant
and
\begin{equation}
\Gstar_{\mathrm{exp}} \;=\; T\,A\,\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{\pi m}}
\label{eq:recovery}
\end{equation}
recovers the lemniscatic constant with \emph{no fitted scale}: $\lambda$ comes
from a static force--displacement measurement, $m$ from a scale. In log--log
form the signature is a frequency--amplitude slope of exactly $+1$
(a harmonic clock gives $0$).

\paragraph{S2 --- the crossover pair.} Equations \eqref{eq:collapse} and
\eqref{eq:Bk} upgrade S1 from a point test to two curve tests: all cycles of
all runs at all detunings must fall on $4kK(k)$ and $\mathcal B(k)$
simultaneously, with the \emph{same} $k$ assigned to each cycle. A
conspiracy that fakes one curve does not fake the other: the period curve
tests the energy--frequency relation, the waveform curve tests the orbit
shape, and they share only the modulus.

\paragraph{S3 --- quantum level scaling.} The phase-space area at energy $E$
is $J(E) = \tfrac{2}{3}\,\Gstar\sqrt{2\pi m}\;\lambda^{-1/4}E^{3/4}$, so
Bohr--Sommerfeld quantization gives $E_n \propto n^{4/3}$ --- against the
harmonic $E_n\propto n$. Anharmonic superconducting circuits tuned to a
quartic point \cite{quarton}, or particles in quartic optical traps, would
realize this rung.

\section{A bad clock is a good sensor}
\label{sec:sensor}

Horology chose $n=2$ for a reason, and the reason is noise. A clock whose
frequency is proportional to its amplitude converts every amplitude
perturbation into a frequency perturbation: near threshold the mode is soft,
susceptibilities grow, relaxation slows, and the very sensitivity that makes
critical points interesting makes them terrible timekeepers. Isochronism is
not an aesthetic preference; it is noise immunity, and the pendulum's
$\partial T/\partial A\approx0$ is the whole reason clocks work. The
anti-pendulum maximizes exactly the coupling the pendulum was chosen to
suppress.

But a defect of a clock is, read backwards, a virtue of a sensor: \emph{the
quantity this oscillator's timing is exquisitely sensitive to is the distance
to its own threshold.} Equation~\eqref{eq:collapse} makes the inversion
quantitative. From a single ringdown --- per-cycle periods and amplitudes,
nothing else --- one assigns each cycle its $4kK$ value, reads off
$k^{2}(A)$, and inverts Eq.~\eqref{eq:modulus}:
$\mu = 2\lambda A^{2}\,(1-2k^{2})/k^{2}$. The detuning $\mu$ is the
integrated proximity to instability: residual pretension in an assembly,
axial load short of buckling in a beam, prestress in a MEMS resonator. The
protocol of Sec.~\ref{sec:apparatus} measures it \emph{without touching the
threshold}: in the validation of Sec.~\ref{sec:validation} the five
micrometer settings are recovered from timing data alone with the correct
linear knob response ($R^{2}=0.999994$) and the suspension's known
gravitational contribution recovered as the intercept to $3\%$. In this
reading the apparatus is not a curiosity but a prototype: anharmonic
ringdown timing as a nondestructive meter of how far a structure sits from
its own edge --- with the exact, parameter-free curves
\eqref{eq:collapse} and \eqref{eq:Bk} as its calibration.

\section{A tabletop realization: odd terms forbidden by symmetry}
\label{sec:apparatus}

The experimental difficulty at any critical point is staying there:
imperfections detune $\mu$ away from zero. The following geometry sidesteps
half of the problem by making the \emph{odd} terms vanish identically rather
than approximately. A mass $m$ sits midway between two anchors separated by
$2L$, held by two identical springs (stiffness $k_s$, natural length $L_0$).
For transverse displacement $x$ each spring has length $\sqrt{L^{2}+x^{2}}$;
with the springs exactly at natural length ($L=L_0$) the extension is
second order in $x$, and the total potential is
\begin{equation}
V(x) \;=\; \frac{k_s}{4L^{2}}\,x^{4}\;+\;O(x^{6}),
\qquad \lambda = \frac{k_s}{4L^{2}},
\label{eq:springquartic}
\end{equation}
with the reflection symmetry exact by construction --- no cubic term is
possible, tuned or not. The \emph{quadratic} term, by contrast, is not
symmetry-protected: cancelling it is a genuine tuning, and that is precisely
the micrometer's job. Pretension $\delta=L-L_0$ reintroduces
$\mu = 2k_s\delta/L$, so a single micrometer on one anchor walks the system
through the full pitchfork of Fig.~\ref{fig:edge}a. At a representative
design point ($k_s=500$\,N/m, $L=12$\,cm, $m=200$\,g) the periods in the
working amplitude window of $1.0$--$2.4$\,cm run from $0.74$ to $1.78$\,s in
the $\mu=0$ idealization (from $0.71$ to $1.43$\,s once the suspension term
of Sec.~\ref{sec:validation} is included) --- comfortable territory for
camera tracking.

Because the frequency tracks its own amplitude, a \emph{single ringdown
sweeps the entire} $T(A)$ \emph{curve}: per-cycle periods from successive
zero crossings, per-cycle amplitudes from the geometric mean of bracketing
peaks. Ten ringdowns constitute a complete dataset for
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:collapse}--\eqref{eq:recovery}.

\section{Simulated validation and error budget}
\label{sec:validation}

Before any hardware, the full measurement chain was validated against a
synthetic apparatus that includes what the idealization omits: the exact
two-spring geometry (whose $x^{6}$ softening shifts the period by $+0.3\%$ at
$A=0.1L$ and $+1.2\%$ at $A=0.2L$, setting the window edge), a
bifilar-suspension gravitational term $\mu_g = mg/\ell$ (which is \emph{not}
small --- at $\ell=1.5$\,m it shifts periods by $\sim20\%$ at the window's
small-amplitude edge, and more below it, and must be modeled, not ignored),
and linear damping. The
declared analysis --- Eq.~\eqref{eq:elliptic} with $\mu$ pinned at its
independently known value --- recovers (Fig.~\ref{fig:recovery}a) a
frequency--amplitude slope of $0.988$ and $\Gstar$ to $+0.28\%$, robust to a
deliberate $\pm5\%$ mis-pin of $\mu$ ($+0.70\%/-0.12\%$). The residual bias
is the booked $x^{6}$ term. The waveform functional
(Fig.~\ref{fig:recovery}b) requires one procedural rule discovered in
simulation: because this clock chirps, each cycle must be mapped onto its own
$2\pi$ of phase before averaging --- resampling several cycles against a
single fixed period smears phase and spuriously pushes $\mathcal B$
\emph{above} $2$. With per-cycle mapping the extractor is unbiased at the
$3\times10^{-5}$ level on exact controls, the simulated ringdown traverses
$\mathcal B = 1.994\to1.974$ along the exact curve of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Bk}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:crossover}b), and the five-detuning dataset collapses onto
Eq.~\eqref{eq:collapse} (Fig.~\ref{fig:crossover}a).

The projected error budget for $\Gstar_{\mathrm{exp}}$ --- static $\lambda$
calibration ($1\%$), effective mass ($0.8\%$), amplitude calibration
($0.5\%$), residual-$\mu$ modeling ($0.5\%$), geometric $x^{6}$ ($0.5\%$) ---
combines, through the square roots in Eq.~\eqref{eq:recovery}, to
$\approx1.1\%$.

\section{The edge in equilibrium: exact thermodynamics}
\label{sec:thermo}

Everything so far concerned isolated dynamics. Put the mode in contact with
a bath at temperature $T$ instead, and the entire equilibrium theory turns
out to mirror the dynamical one --- with its own detuning variable, its own
universal curves, and $\Gstar$ again at the critical point. (All results in
this section are verified numerically in the accompanying code to
$10^{-10}$ or better; Appendix~\ref{app:thermo} has the derivations.)

\paragraph{One thermal variable.} The momentum integral is Gaussian always
(kinetic equipartition, $\langle p^{2}/2m\rangle=\tfrac12 k_BT$, is exact
regardless of the potential). Rescaling $x$ by $(\beta\lambda)^{1/4}$ in the
configurational integral leaves a single dimensionless group
\begin{equation}
\gamma \;=\; \mu\sqrt{\beta/\lambda}
\qquad (\beta = 1/k_BT)
\label{eq:gamma}
\end{equation}
--- the \emph{thermal} detuning, the equilibrium twin of the dynamic modulus
$k$. Every dimensionless equilibrium quantity is a universal function of
$\gamma$ alone (Fig.~\ref{fig:thermo}).

\paragraph{The critical values.} At threshold ($\gamma=0$) the moments
evaluate in Gamma functions:
\begin{equation}
\langle x^{2}\rangle
=\sqrt{\frac{k_BT}{\lambda}}\;\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac34)}{\Gamma(\tfrac14)}
=\frac{1}{\Gstar}\sqrt{\frac{k_BT}{\lambda}},
\qquad
U\equiv\frac{\langle x^{4}\rangle}{\langle x^{2}\rangle^{2}}
=\frac{\Gstar^{2}}{4}=\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac14)^{4}}{8\pi^{2}}
=2.188440\ldots
\label{eq:thermalcrit}
\end{equation}
The variance result means the constant that rules the edge's
\emph{timekeeping} also rules its \emph{thermal fluctuations} --- with no
oscillation measured at all: a static, dynamics-free recovery of $\Gstar$
from thermomechanical noise. The dimensionless ratio $U$ interpolates from
$3$ (Gaussian, taut) through $\Gstar^{2}/4$ at the edge to $1$ (two-state,
deep double well); readers from statistical mechanics will recognize it as
the Binder cumulant ratio, whose mean-field critical value is exactly this
pure-quartic number \cite{binder,brezin} --- here realized in a single
mechanical mode with a micrometer, rather than in the finite-size scaling
limit of a lattice model.

\paragraph{Equipartition deficit.} Generalized equipartition
($\langle x V'(x)\rangle=k_BT$) gives the critical mode
$\langle V\rangle=\tfrac14 k_BT$, hence
\begin{equation}
\langle E\rangle_{\gamma=0}=\tfrac34\,k_BT,
\qquad
C_{\gamma=0}=\tfrac34\,k_B
\label{eq:heatcap}
\end{equation}
--- a mode at its threshold carries three-quarters of a harmonic mode's heat
capacity. The full universal curve $C(\gamma)$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:thermo}b) dips
\emph{below} $\tfrac34 k_B$ on the slightly-slack side (minimum
$\approx0.67\,k_B$ near $\gamma\approx-2.5$) before recovering through a
Schottky-like excess in the double-well regime: heat capacity alone is a
threshold detector.

\paragraph{The Laplace bridge.} The two halves of this paper are one
object: since the phase-space area between energy shells of a single orbit
family is $T(E)\,dE$, the classical partition function of that family is
\begin{equation}
Z_{\mathrm{osc}}(\beta)\;=\;\int T(E)\,e^{-\beta E}\,dE .
\label{eq:laplace}
\end{equation}
For $\mu\ge0$ there is one family, Eq.~\eqref{eq:collapse} covers it, and
the identity is verified to ten digits in the code: \emph{the
thermodynamics is the Laplace transform of the clock curve}, with the
dynamical modulus $k$ and the thermal detuning $\gamma$ as conjugate
descriptions of the same crossover. For $\mu<0$ the phase space carries a
\emph{second} class of motion --- the two sub-barrier intra-well orbit
families on $E_{\min}=-\mu^{2}/16\lambda \le E<0$, themselves
Jacobi-elliptic --- and the bridge completes to
$Z=\int_{E_{\min}}^{0}2\,T_{\mathrm{well}}(E)\,e^{-\beta E}dE
+\int_{0}^{\infty}T(E)\,e^{-\beta E}dE$: verified to $4\times10^{-9}$ at
$(\mu,\beta)=(-2,1.3)$, where the over-barrier family alone would omit
$36\%$ of $Z$. The $\gamma<0$ curves in this section are computed by direct
quadrature and do not rely on the bridge.

\paragraph{The thermal lineshape and the $T^{1/4}$ law.} An undamped
thermal ensemble of critical-mode orbits has frequency distribution
(Appendix~\ref{app:thermo})
\begin{equation}
P(f)\;\propto\;f^{2}\,e^{-(f/f_T)^{4}},
\qquad
f_T=\frac{(k_BT/\lambda)^{1/4}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gstar}\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{m}}
\;\propto\;T^{1/4},
\label{eq:lineshape}
\end{equation}
peaked at $f_{\mathrm{peak}}=(1/2)^{1/4}f_T$. A harmonic mode's thermal
spectrum sits at a temperature-independent frequency; a marginal mode's
spectral peak \emph{climbs as the fourth root of temperature}
(Monte-Carlo check: median frequency ratio $1.998$ between baths at
$16{:}1$ temperature ratio, against the predicted $2$;
Fig.~\ref{fig:thermo}c). For nanomechanical resonators parked at buckling,
this is a signature measurable in the thermomechanical noise floor with no
drive at all. The finite-$Q$ and driven versions of this lineshape connect
to the established theory of fluctuations in nonlinear oscillators, and a
careful reconciliation with that literature is required before this
subsection's novelty is asserted beyond the closed forms themselves.

\section{The quantum rung}
\label{sec:quantum}

The same normal form has a third one-parameter reduction. Rescaling
$\hat x = a\hat u$ with $a=(\hbar^{2}/2m\lambda)^{1/6}$ and measuring energy
in $\varepsilon=\lambda a^{4}=(\lambda\hbar^{4}/4m^{2})^{1/3}$ turns
$\hat H = \hat p^{2}/2m+\tfrac12\mu\hat x^{2}+\lambda\hat x^{4}$ into
\begin{equation}
\hat h \;=\; \hat v^{2} \;+\; \tfrac{g}{2}\,\hat u^{2} \;+\; \hat u^{4},
\qquad
g \;=\; \frac{\mu\,(2m)^{1/3}}{(\lambda\hbar)^{2/3}}
\label{eq:qdetuning}
\end{equation}
--- a single \emph{quantum} detuning, the third sibling of the dynamical
modulus $k$ and the thermal $\gamma$ (universality verified to machine
precision in the code: two different $(m,\mu,\lambda,\hbar)$ at equal $g$
give identical reduced spectra to $10^{-16}$). Three deformations of one
structure: $k$ indexes the orbit, $\gamma$ the ensemble, $g$ the spectrum.

\paragraph{The edge has an $\hbar$-width.} Equation~\eqref{eq:qdetuning}
says quantum mechanics rounds the pitchfork on the scale $|g|\lesssim1$,
i.e.\ $|\mu|\lesssim\mu_{\hbar}=(\lambda\hbar)^{2/3}/(2m)^{1/3}$. Inside
that window there is no transition at all: the ground state is symmetric for
\emph{every} $g$, the would-be broken doublets are split by tunneling, and
the splitting closes smoothly (exponentially) as $g\to-\infty$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:quantum}b) --- the classical edge's sharpness is an
$\hbar\to0$ idealization. For the tabletop of Sec.~\ref{sec:apparatus},
$\mu_{\hbar}\sim10^{-20}$\,N/m --- twenty orders below the micrometer's
reach, which is why the classical treatment is exact there; a quarton-class
circuit lives \emph{inside} its $\hbar$-width by design.

\paragraph{The spectrum carries the $G^{*}$-law.} At $g=0$ the
Bohr--Sommerfeld action of Sec.~\ref{sec:signatures} gives
\begin{equation}
E_{n} \;\simeq\; \Bigl[\tfrac{3\sqrt{\pi}\,(n+\tfrac12)}{\Gstar}\Bigr]^{4/3}
\varepsilon,
\label{eq:qwkb}
\end{equation}
exact to $0.05\%$ by $n=40$ and to $1\%$ by $n=2$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:quantum}a); the numerically exact ground state is
$E_{0}=1.06036\,\varepsilon$, the classic strong-coupling benchmark of the
quartic-oscillator literature \cite{benderwu,hioe} --- none of the spectral
theory here is new, and we claim only the composition. The device-facing
number is the spacing ratio: the pure quartic ladder \emph{widens} with $n$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{E_{2}-E_{1}}{E_{1}-E_{0}} \;=\; 1.3346,
\qquad
\frac{E_{3}-E_{2}}{E_{2}-E_{1}} \;=\; 1.1458,
\label{eq:qratios}
\end{equation}
fixed, dimensionless, and opposite in sign of anharmonicity to a transmon's
narrowing ladder --- a two-tone spectroscopy test of whether a circuit mode
truly sits at its quartic point.

\paragraph{Freeze-out welds the rungs together.} The quantum thermal state
at $g=0$ interpolates the paper's sections: the heat capacity rises from the
quantum $0$ to the \emph{classical edge value} $\tfrac34 k_B$ of
Sec.~\ref{sec:thermo} (reaching $0.749\,k_B$ by $k_BT=30\,\varepsilon$), and
the moment ratio $U$ descends from its ground-state value
\begin{equation}
U_{0} \;=\; \frac{\langle0|\hat u^{4}|0\rangle}{\langle0|\hat u^{2}|0\rangle^{2}}
\;=\; 2.6969
\label{eq:U0}
\end{equation}
--- a universal pure number of the quantum edge, sitting between the
Gaussian $3$ and the classical $\Gstar^{2}/4$ --- down to the classical
value as $T$ grows (Fig.~\ref{fig:quantum}c). Temperature and $\hbar$ are
conjugate dials moving the same system between the $\gamma$-theory and the
$g$-theory; the classical edge of Secs.~\ref{sec:exact}--\ref{sec:thermo}
is the high-$T$, small-$\hbar$ face of one object.

\section{Outlook: from simulation to measurement, and beyond}
\label{sec:platforms}

The natural next step is the measurement itself, and a preregistered
protocol --- hypotheses, quantitative pass/fail gates, kill conditions, and
the analysis code frozen before data --- accompanies this paper; the
apparatus costs a few hundred dollars and the primary dataset is ten
ringdowns. Beyond the tabletop: buckled micro- and nanomechanical beams at
critical load realize the same normal form at kHz--MHz frequencies with
quality factors in the thousands, where the $\mathcal B(k)$ curve becomes
easy and the threshold-proximity metrology of Sec.~\ref{sec:sensor} has an
engineering constituency --- resonant sensors deliberately operated near
the buckling bifurcation for enhanced responsivity already exist
\cite{liu} --- with prestress and buckling margin as the natural
measurands;
superconducting circuits in which the Josephson cosine's quadratic term is
cancelled --- the ``quarton'' element, demonstrated to date as a purely
nonlinear \emph{coupler} between qubits \cite{quarton} --- point at a
\emph{quantum} quartic mode where the $E_n\propto n^{4/3}$ rung becomes
measurable; and cold atoms have already been held in quartic-plus-quadratic
traps --- built, historically, to confine fast-rotating Bose--Einstein
condensates and their vortex lattices \cite{bretin} --- so the many-body
breathing dynamics of a pure-quartic trap is within reach of existing
technique. In each case the offer is the same: at the supercritical edge of
stability, timekeeping is lemniscatic, its constant is $\Gstar$, its entire
crossover is two exact parameter-free curves, and what ruins it as a clock
is precisely what qualifies it as a sensor.

\section{The period law}
\label{app:period}

For $H=p^{2}/2m+\lambda q^{4}$ with turning point $A$ ($E=\lambda A^{4}$),
\[
T=4\!\int_0^A\!\frac{dq}{\sqrt{2(E-\lambda q^4)/m}}
 =4\sqrt{\frac{m}{2\lambda}}\,\frac{1}{A}\int_0^1\!\frac{du}{\sqrt{1-u^4}},
\qquad
\int_0^1\!\frac{du}{\sqrt{1-u^4}}=\frac{B(\tfrac14,\tfrac12)}{4}
=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gstar}{4},
\]
which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:period}; the Beta-function evaluation uses
$\Gamma(\tfrac14)\Gamma(\tfrac34)=\pi\sqrt2$ (reflection). For the detuned
potential $V=\tfrac12\mu q^{2}+\lambda q^{4}$, the factorization
$E-V=(A^{2}-q^{2})\bigl(\tfrac{\mu}{2}+\lambda(A^{2}+q^{2})\bigr)$ and the
substitution $q=A\sin\theta$ give
\[
T=4\sqrt{\frac m2}\int_0^{\pi/2}
\frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{\tfrac{\mu}{2}+\lambda A^{2}+\lambda A^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}}
=4\sqrt{\frac m2}\,
\frac{K(\kappa)}{\sqrt{\tfrac{\mu}{2}+2\lambda A^{2}}},
\]
with $\kappa^{2}=\lambda A^{2}/(\tfrac{\mu}{2}+2\lambda A^{2})$ ---
Eq.~\eqref{eq:elliptic}. The collapse Eq.~\eqref{eq:collapse} follows by
eliminating: from Eq.~\eqref{eq:modulus},
$\tfrac{\mu}{2}+2\lambda A^{2}=\lambda A^{2}/k^{2}$, so
$T=4\sqrt{m/2}\;K\,k/(A\sqrt{\lambda})$, i.e.
$T\,A\sqrt{2\lambda/m}=4kK(k)$, valid on both sides of threshold
($0<k^{2}<1$; $k^{2}>\tfrac12$ is the double-well side, oscillations above
the barrier).

\section{The waveform functional on the $|x|^{p}$ family}
\label{app:B}

Let $x(\theta)$ be one cycle of the normalized ($|x|\le1$) waveform in
uniform phase. For $V\propto|x|^{p}$, energy conservation gives the
phase-speed law $(dx/d\theta)^{2}=\tfrac{4B_{p0}^{2}}{p^{2}\pi^{2}}
(1-|x|^{p})$ with $B_{p0}=B(\tfrac1p,\tfrac12)$, whence
\[
\langle x^{2}\rangle=\frac{B(\tfrac3p,\tfrac12)}{B_{p0}},
\qquad
\langle x'^{2}\rangle=\frac{4B_{p0}^{2}}{p(p+2)\pi^{2}},
\qquad
\mathcal B_p=\frac{2\langle x^{2}\rangle}{\langle x'^{2}\rangle}
=\frac{p(p+2)\pi^{2}B(\tfrac3p,\tfrac12)}{2B_{p0}^{3}} .
\]
For $p=2$ this is $2$ exactly. For $p=4$, $B(\tfrac34,\tfrac12)=
4\sqrt{\pi}/\Gstar$ and $B(\tfrac14,\tfrac12)=\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gstar$ give
$\langle x^{2}\rangle=4/\Gstar^{2}$, $\langle x'^{2}\rangle=\Gstar^{2}/6\pi$,
and $\mathcal B_4=48\pi/\Gstar^{4}$.

\section{The waveform functional along the crossover}
\label{app:Bk}

With $x=A\,\mathrm{cn}(u;k)$, $u=\Omega t$, one period is $4K$, and uniform
phase is $\theta=\pi u/2K$, so $dx/d\theta=(2K/\pi)\,dx/du
=-(2K/\pi)A\,\mathrm{sn}\,\mathrm{dn}$. The period averages of the Jacobi
functions are standard \cite{byrd}:
\[
\langle \mathrm{cn}^{2}\rangle=\frac{E-(1-k^{2})K}{k^{2}K},
\qquad
\langle \mathrm{sn}^{2}\rangle=\frac{K-E}{k^{2}K},
\qquad
\langle \mathrm{sn}^{4}\rangle=\frac{(2+k^{2})K-2(1+k^{2})E}{3k^{4}K},
\]
and $\mathrm{sn}^{2}\mathrm{dn}^{2}=\mathrm{sn}^{2}-k^{2}\mathrm{sn}^{4}$
gives
\[
\langle \mathrm{sn}^{2}\mathrm{dn}^{2}\rangle
=\frac{(1-k^{2})K+(2k^{2}-1)E}{3k^{2}K}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathcal B(k)
=\frac{2A^{2}\langle \mathrm{cn}^{2}\rangle}
      {(2K/\pi)^{2}A^{2}\langle \mathrm{sn}^{2}\mathrm{dn}^{2}\rangle}
=\frac{3\pi^{2}}{2K^{2}}\,
\frac{E-(1-k^{2})K}{(1-k^{2})K+(2k^{2}-1)E},
\]
which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:Bk}. Limits: $k\to0$ gives $\mathcal B\to2$
(expanding $E,K$ to $O(k^{2})$); $k^{2}=\tfrac12$ gives, with
$K(1/\sqrt2)=\Gamma(\tfrac14)^{2}/4\sqrt{\pi}$ and the Legendre relation,
$\mathcal B=48\pi/\Gstar^{4}$. Both limits, and a generic-modulus spot
check against direct waveform simulation, are verified numerically in the
accompanying code.

\section{The thermodynamics of the edge: derivations}
\label{app:thermo}

\paragraph{Moments.} With $x=(\beta\lambda)^{-1/4}u$ the configurational
weight becomes $e^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}u^{2}-u^{4}}$ with $\gamma$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gamma}. At $\gamma=0$,
$\langle u^{2n}\rangle=\Gamma(\tfrac{2n+1}{4})/\Gamma(\tfrac14)$, giving
$\langle u^{2}\rangle=\Gamma(\tfrac34)/\Gamma(\tfrac14)=1/\Gstar$ (via the
reflection formula, as in Appendix~\ref{app:period}) and
$\langle u^{4}\rangle=\Gamma(\tfrac54)/\Gamma(\tfrac14)=\tfrac14$, whence
Eq.~\eqref{eq:thermalcrit}: $U(0)=\tfrac14\Gstar^{2}$. The Gaussian limit
$\gamma\to+\infty$ gives Wick's $U=3$; the deep double well
$\gamma\to-\infty$ concentrates on $\pm x_{\min}$, giving $U=1$.

\paragraph{Energy and heat capacity.} Generalized equipartition
$\langle xV'(x)\rangle=k_BT$ applied to $V=\lambda x^{4}$ gives
$4\langle V\rangle=k_BT$; adding the kinetic $\tfrac12 k_BT$ yields
Eq.~\eqref{eq:heatcap}. For general $\gamma$, $C(\gamma)$ follows from
differentiating $\langle E\rangle$; the curve in Fig.~\ref{fig:thermo}b is
computed by direct quadrature.

\paragraph{Laplace bridge.} The phase-space measure between energy shells
of a one-degree-of-freedom orbit family is $dJ = T(E)\,dE$ (the period is
the derivative of the action), so summing over families,
$Z_{\mathrm{osc}}=\int e^{-\beta H}\,dx\,dp=\sum_{\mathrm{families}}\int
T_{i}(E)\,e^{-\beta E}dE$ --- Eq.~\eqref{eq:laplace}. For $\mu\ge0$ the sum
has one term; for $\mu<0$ it has three (two congruent intra-well families
below the barrier, one symmetric family above). This is elementary but
load-bearing: it is the exact sense in which the equilibrium theory of
Sec.~\ref{sec:thermo} is the integral transform of the dynamical periods,
of which Eq.~\eqref{eq:collapse} describes the symmetric family.

\paragraph{Lineshape.} For the critical mode, $E=\lambda A^{4}$ and
$f(E)=(E/\lambda)^{1/4}\sqrt{2\lambda/m}\,/(\sqrt{\pi}\Gstar)$. The thermal
weight of orbits is $T(E)e^{-\beta E}dE\propto E^{-1/4}e^{-\beta E}dE$;
transforming to $f$ with $E\propto f^{4}$ (so $dE\propto f^{3}df$ and
$E^{-1/4}\propto f^{-1}$) gives $P(f)\propto f^{2}e^{-(f/f_T)^4}$ with
$f_T$ as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:lineshape}, peaked at $(1/2)^{1/4}f_T$.
