7  Gauge Groups from Geometry

The Standard Model gauge group \(U(1) \times SU(2) \times SU(3)\) is not postulated. It is a consequence of the Moore neighborhood decomposition (Chapter 4) and the flux-component excitation pattern.

7.1 5.1 The mechanism [THEOREM]

A neighbor at offset \(\delta\) excites \(k\) flux components, where \(k\) is the number of nonzero entries in \(\delta\). The gauge group generated by a sublattice with \(k\) excited components is determined by the representation theory of \(k\) coupled real vector fields under the Gauss constraint.

7.2 Theorem 5.1 (Gauge group assignment)

The three Moore sublattices generate gauge groups as follows:

Sublattice \(k\) Coupling Gauge group Justification
SC (6) 1 \(J_\mu\) alone \(U(1)\) Single-component Gauss constraint has Abelian gauge redundancy
FCC (12) 2 \(J_\mu, J_\nu\) pair \(SU(2)\) Two-component system with chiral doublet structure
BCC (8) 3 \(J_x, J_y, J_z\) all \(SU(3)\) Three independent components = fundamental rep of \(SU(3)\)

7.2.1 SC \(\to\) \(U(1)\): electromagnetism [THEOREM]

The 6 SC neighbors each excite a single flux component. The Gauss constraint \(\partial_\mu J_\mu = \rho\) on this sublattice admits the gauge transformation \(J_\mu \to J_\mu + \partial_\mu \lambda\) for any scalar \(\lambda\). This is the Abelian gauge group \(U(1)\).

The gauge boson is the photon: a transverse excitation of the SC flux field propagating at \(c = 1/\sqrt{3}\). The coupling constant is \(g_c = \sqrt{\alpha}\), where \(\alpha = 1/x_+\) comes from the master quadratic (Chapter 10).

7.2.2 FCC \(\to\) \(SU(2)\): weak interaction [THEOREM for structure, SELECTION for identification]

The 12 FCC neighbors each excite two flux components. A pair \((J_\mu, J_\nu)\) with \(\mu \neq \nu\) forms a doublet under the exchange \(\mu \leftrightarrow \nu\). On the lattice, the ternary state \(s \in \{-1, 0, +1\}\) provides a natural doublet structure: the non-void states \(\{+1, -1\}\) transform as a two-component object under charge conjugation.

The FCC sublattice has 3 pairs of axes \(\times\) 4 sign combinations per pair \(= 12\) neighbors, which organize into the adjoint representation of \(SU(2)\): three generators \((W^+, W^-, Z^0)\) corresponding to the three axis-pairs.

The identification with the weak interaction follows from: (a) \(SU(2)\) gauge symmetry, (b) doublet structure from the ternary states, (c) parity violation from the chirality of the FCC sublattice (edge-sharing is inherently handed in 3D).

7.2.3 BCC \(\to\) \(SU(3)\): strong interaction [THEOREM for structure, SELECTION for identification]

The 8 BCC neighbors each excite all three flux components simultaneously. Three independent flux directions \((J_x, J_y, J_z)\) provide the fundamental representation of \(SU(3)\): three “colors” mapped to the three spatial axes.

The identification:

\[ J_x \leftrightarrow \text{red}, \quad J_y \leftrightarrow \text{green}, \quad J_z \leftrightarrow \text{blue} \]

is [SELECTION] (the axis-color correspondence is conventional), but the gauge group \(SU(3)\) is [THEOREM]: it is the unique compact simple group that acts faithfully on three objects.

Color neutrality requires \(J_x^2 + J_y^2 + J_z^2 = |\mathbf{J}|^2\) to be the gauge-invariant combination — which is exactly the manifestation threshold condition \(|\mathbf{J}| \geq K_B\) from Section 4.5. A manifested particle (a hadron) must be a color singlet.

7.3 5.2 Why this product group [THEOREM]

The gauge group \(U(1) \times SU(2) \times SU(3)\) is not a choice but a consequence of having exactly three sublattices with \(k = 1, 2, 3\) excited components:

\[ \text{Moore}(26) = \text{SC}(6) \cup \text{FCC}(12) \cup \text{BCC}(8) \quad \Longrightarrow \quad U(1) \times SU(2) \times SU(3) \]

For \(D \neq 3\), the Moore neighborhood has a different decomposition and the gauge group would be different. In \(D = 2\), the Moore neighborhood has 8 neighbors (4 face + 4 corner), decomposing into \(U(1) \times SU(2)\) only. In \(D = 4\), the neighborhood has 80 neighbors with layers at \(k = 1, 2, 3, 4\), producing an additional \(SU(4)\) factor not observed in nature. This is another reason \(D = 3\) is uniquely selected (Chapter 5).

7.4 5.3 Particle content [THEOREM for counting, SELECTION for identification]

The Moore Layer Theorem determines not only the gauge group but also the particle content per generation:

  • SC layer (6 voxels, \(U(1)\)): 2 leptons (charged + neutral) per generation
  • FCC layer (12 voxels, \(SU(2)\)): the electroweak doublet structure
  • BCC layer (8 voxels, \(SU(3)\)): 2 quarks (up-type + down-type) per generation, each in 3 colors

Per generation: 2 leptons + 2 quarks \(\times\) 3 colors = 8 fermions. Total: \(4\) fermion types per generation.

Three generations arise from the three irreducible representations of the Moore neighborhood’s octahedral symmetry group \(O_h\) that are compatible with the ternary state structure. The group \(O_h\) has order 48. Its irreducible representations of dimension \(\leq 3\) produce exactly 3 distinct fermion families. [SELECTION for the mechanism; THEOREM for the group theory]

Dark states: Beyond the 3 \(\times\) 8 = 24 visible fermion states, the Moore neighborhood contains \(3^3 - 3 \times 2^3 + 3 = 17\) sub-threshold configurations that gravitationally interact but do not manifest electromagnetically. These are the FTD dark matter candidates (Chapter 24).

7.5 5.4 The gauge-gravity separation

The gauge groups arise from the angular structure of the flux field (how many components are coupled). Gravity arises from the radial structure (the magnitude \(|\mathbf{J}|\) and the Born-Infeld bandwidth constraint). This separation is natural on the lattice:

  • Gauge forces (EM, weak, strong): from the direction of \(\mathbf{J}\)
  • Gravity: from the magnitude \(|\mathbf{J}|\) and the resulting metric distortion

The unification occurs at the level of the flux field \(\mathbf{J}\) itself: all forces are aspects of the same three-component vector field, differentiated by which components are engaged.
