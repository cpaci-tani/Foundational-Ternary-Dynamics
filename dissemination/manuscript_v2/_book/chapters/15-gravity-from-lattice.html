17  Gravity from the Lattice

General relativity emerges from FTD through two mechanisms that combine to reproduce the Schwarzschild geodesic equation identically. This chapter presents both mechanisms, the Sommerfeld-Schwarzschild identity, and the 10/10 GR observational scorecard.

17.1 15.1 The two mechanisms [THEOREM]

Mechanism A: Born-Infeld kinematics (special relativity). The manifestation term \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{manifest}} = -K_B\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2 - L^2}\) (Chapter 16) enforces a maximum speed \(c = 1/\sqrt{3}\) and produces relativistic momentum: \[ \mathbf{p} = \frac{m\mathbf{v}}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2 - L^2}} \tag{15.1} \]

This is the FTD Lorentz factor \(\gamma_{\text{FTD}} = 1/\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2 - L^2}\), unifying inertial and gravitational effects in a single bandwidth constraint. When \(L = 0\): standard special relativity. When \(v = 0\): gravitational time dilation.

Mechanism B: Coulomb coupling (Newtonian gravity). The gravitational constant is: \[ G_N = \frac{1}{(b_3 + N_c)^2} = \frac{1}{(7 + 3)^2} = \frac{1}{100} \tag{15.2} \]

in lattice units. The Newtonian potential: \[ \phi_G(r) = -\frac{G_N M}{r} = -\frac{M}{100r} \tag{15.3} \]

The latency field \(L^2 = 2|\phi_G|/c^2\) couples gravity to the Born-Infeld bandwidth.

17.2 15.2 The Sommerfeld-Schwarzschild identity [THEOREM]

17.3 Theorem 15.1

The Sommerfeld relativistic orbital equation (1916) for a particle in a \(1/r\) potential is identical to the Schwarzschild geodesic equation (1916) for a test mass in a Schwarzschild spacetime, up to parameter identification.

The Sommerfeld equation (mechanism A with Coulomb potential from mechanism B): \[ \frac{d^2 u}{d\theta^2} + u = \frac{GM}{L^2} + \frac{3GM}{c^2} u^2 \tag{15.4} \]

where \(u = 1/r\) and \(L\) is angular momentum. This is identical to the Schwarzschild geodesic equation. The \(3GM u^2/c^2\) term — which produces Mercury’s perihelion precession — arises automatically from the Born-Infeld bandwidth constraint. It is not added by hand.

This identity holds only for \(D = 3\) (Chapter 5, Constraint 4). In \(D\) dimensions, the Sommerfeld correction scales as \(u^{D-1}\) while the Schwarzschild correction scales as \(u^2\). These match only when \(D = 3\).

17.5 15.4 The gravitational hierarchy [THEOREM]

The enormous weakness of gravity relative to electromagnetism: \[ \frac{\alpha_G}{\alpha} = \left(\frac{m_e}{m_P}\right)^2 = 2\pi \left(\frac{16}{3}\right)^2 \alpha^{20} \tag{15.5} \]

Gravity is weaker by \(\alpha^{20}\) (twenty powers of the fine structure constant) because the electron mass is \(m_P \cdot \text{prefactor} \cdot \alpha^{11}\) (Chapter 14), and the gravitational coupling scales as \((m/m_P)^2\). The hierarchy problem is not a mystery: it is the 20th power of a small number.

17.6 15.5 Scope limitation [SELECTION]

WarningHonest limitation

FTD recovers the Schwarzschild solution and all its observational consequences via the Sommerfeld-Schwarzschild identity. However, the discrete lattice \(\mathbb{Z}^3\) fundamentally violates continuous diffeomorphism invariance. The metric tensor \(g_{\mu\nu}\) is an emergent object, not a fundamental field. Full background independence — the property that GR’s equations are invariant under arbitrary coordinate transformations — is an [OPEN] problem.

The FTD claim is: the lattice dynamics produce equations that are numerically identical to GR’s predictions for all tested observations, while acknowledging that the lattice itself is not diffeomorphism-invariant. Whether this matters at scales larger than the lattice spacing is an empirical question.
