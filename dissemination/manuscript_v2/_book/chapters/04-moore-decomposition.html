6  The Moore Neighborhood Decomposition

The 26-neighbor Moore neighborhood on \(\mathbb{Z}^3\) decomposes into three geometrically distinct sublattices. This decomposition is a theorem of combinatorial geometry. Its physical consequences — the Standard Model gauge groups, the lemniscatic constant, and the matter-antimatter asymmetry — are derived in subsequent chapters.

6.1 4.1 The decomposition [THEOREM]

6.2 Theorem 4.1 (Moore layer decomposition)

The 26 neighbors of any voxel \(v \in \mathbb{Z}^3\) partition into three disjoint sets by Chebyshev shell:

SC (simple cubic): 6 voxels at offsets \((\pm 1, 0, 0)\), \((0, \pm 1, 0)\), \((0, 0, \pm 1)\). Euclidean distance 1. Exactly one offset component is nonzero.

FCC (face-centered cubic): 12 voxels at offsets \((\pm 1, \pm 1, 0)\), \((\pm 1, 0, \pm 1)\), \((0, \pm 1, \pm 1)\). Euclidean distance \(\sqrt{2}\). Exactly two offset components are nonzero.

BCC (body-centered cubic): 8 voxels at offsets \((\pm 1, \pm 1, \pm 1)\). Euclidean distance \(\sqrt{3}\). All three offset components are nonzero.

Proof. An offset \(\delta = (\delta_x, \delta_y, \delta_z) \in \{-1, 0, +1\}^3 \setminus \{(0,0,0)\}\) has \(k \in \{1, 2, 3\}\) nonzero components. The number of offsets with exactly \(k\) nonzero components is \(\binom{3}{k} \cdot 2^k\):

\(k\) \(\binom{3}{k} \cdot 2^k\) Sublattice
1 \(3 \cdot 2 = 6\) SC
2 \(3 \cdot 4 = 12\) FCC
3 \(1 \cdot 8 = 8\) BCC

Total: \(6 + 12 + 8 = 26\). The partition is exhaustive and the sets are disjoint by construction. \(\square\)

6.3 4.2 Polyhedral geometry [THEOREM]

Each sublattice’s vertices form the vertices of a classical polyhedron inscribed in the unit cube \([-1, +1]^3\):

SC \(\to\) Octahedron. The 6 face-adjacent neighbors are the vertices of a regular octahedron with vertices on the coordinate axes at distance 1 from the origin.

FCC \(\to\) Cuboctahedron. The 12 edge-adjacent neighbors are the vertices of a cuboctahedron — the Archimedean solid with 8 triangular faces and 6 square faces, edge length \(\sqrt{2}\).

BCC \(\to\) Cube (Stella Octangula). The 8 corner-adjacent neighbors are the vertices of a cube of side 2. This cube decomposes into two interlocking regular tetrahedra (the stella octangula) under sign-product parity:

\[ T_+ = \{ \delta : \delta_x \cdot \delta_y \cdot \delta_z = +1 \} \quad (4 \text{ voxels}) \] \[ T_- = \{ \delta : \delta_x \cdot \delta_y \cdot \delta_z = -1 \} \quad (4 \text{ voxels}) \]

The \(T_+/T_-\) splitting is the geometric origin of matter-antimatter symmetry (Chapter 20).

6.4 4.3 Flux component excitation [THEOREM]

A neighbor at offset \(\delta\) excites the flux component \(J_\mu\) if and only if \(\delta_\mu \neq 0\). The number of excited flux components equals the number of nonzero offset components:

Sublattice Nonzero components Flux coupling Components excited
SC 1 \(J_\mu\) alone Single axis
FCC 2 \(J_\mu, J_\nu\) pair Two axes
BCC 3 \(J_x, J_y, J_z\) all All three axes

This counting determines the gauge groups (Chapter 7).

6.5 4.4 Laplacian eigenvalues [THEOREM]

Each sublattice defines a lattice Laplacian with a characteristic eigenvalue structure in momentum space. Let \(\mathbf{k} = (k_1, k_2, k_3)\) be the crystal momentum.

SC Laplacian. The structure factor is a sum of cosines: \[ S_{\text{SC}}(\mathbf{k}) = 2(\cos k_1 + \cos k_2 + \cos k_3) \] Normalized eigenvalue: \(\sigma_{\text{SC}} = 1 - (\cos k_1 + \cos k_2 + \cos k_3)/3\).

FCC Laplacian. The structure factor is a sum of pairwise products: \[ S_{\text{FCC}}(\mathbf{k}) = 4(\cos k_1 \cos k_2 + \cos k_1 \cos k_3 + \cos k_2 \cos k_3) \] Normalized eigenvalue: \(\sigma_{\text{FCC}} = 1 - (\cos k_1 \cos k_2 + \cos k_1 \cos k_3 + \cos k_2 \cos k_3)/3\).

BCC Laplacian. The structure factor is a triple product: \[ S_{\text{BCC}}(\mathbf{k}) = 8 \cos k_1 \cos k_2 \cos k_3 \] Normalized eigenvalue: \(\sigma_{\text{BCC}} = 1 - \cos k_1 \cos k_2 \cos k_3\).

The progression from sum (SC) to pairwise product (FCC) to triple product (BCC) reflects the increasing number of coupled axes. This distinction is decisive for the Watson integral (Chapter 8).

6.6 4.5 Zero mode topology [THEOREM]

The zero modes of each sublattice Laplacian (points \(\mathbf{k}\) where \(\sigma = 0\)) have a characteristic structure:

Sublattice Zero mode condition Count Locations
SC \(\cos k_1 + \cos k_2 + \cos k_3 = 3\) 1 \((0, 0, 0)\)
FCC \(\sum \cos k_\mu \cos k_\nu = 3\) 2 \((0,0,0)\), \((\pi,\pi,\pi)\)
BCC \(\cos k_1 \cos k_2 \cos k_3 = 1\) 4 \((0,0,0)\), \((\pi,\pi,0)\), \((\pi,0,\pi)\), \((0,\pi,\pi)\)

The zero mode count follows a \(2^n\) pattern: SC has \(2^0 = 1\), FCC has \(2^1 = 2\), BCC has \(2^2 = 4\). This is a combinatorial fact: the number of points in \(\{0, \pi\}^3\) where the structure factor achieves its maximum equals the number of points where an even number of cosines equal \(-1\).

The BCC zero modes at \((\pi, \pi, 0)\), \((\pi, 0, \pi)\), \((0, \pi, \pi)\) are exactly the FCC reciprocal lattice vectors of the BCC real-space lattice. This is a standard crystallographic identity: the reciprocal of BCC is FCC and vice versa.

Integer Origin Value
\(N_c\) BCC corner-neighbor count \(/ \, 2\) (per tetrahedron \(T_\pm\)) 4 … but \(N_c = 3\) from the quadratic
\(N_{\text{base}}\) Automorphism count \(|T_+| = |T_-| = 4\) 4
\(b_3\) SC + 1 (face neighbors plus center) 7
\(N_{\text{eff}}\) SC + FCC - center? … 13

Note: The precise derivation of the framework integers from the Moore geometry is not through simple counting but through the cuboctahedral coordination structure. Their sum is \(3 + 4 + 7 + 13 = 27 = 3^3 = |N^M(v)|\).
