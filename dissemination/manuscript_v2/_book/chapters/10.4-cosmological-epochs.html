58  Cosmological Epochs

“The history of the universe”

NoteKey Revelation

The universe has evolved through distinct eras, each with its own physics. From the Planck era to the present, we can trace 13.8 billion years of cosmic history.

58.1 Timeline Overview

The key events span 60 orders of magnitude in time: from the Planck era (\(10^{-43}\) s) through inflation, particle physics transitions, nucleosynthesis, recombination, the dark ages, cosmic dawn, and galaxy assembly to the present day (13.8 billion years).

58.2 The Planck Era (0 to \(10^{-43}\) s)

58.2.1 Conditions

  • Temperature: >\(10^{32}\) K
  • Size: <\(10^{-35}\) m (Planck length)
  • All forces unified?

58.2.2 Physics

  • Quantum gravity regime
  • No known physics applies
  • Requires theory of quantum gravity

58.3 Inflation (\(10^{-36}\) to \(10^{-32}\) s)

58.3.1 What Happened

Exponential expansion by factor ~\(10^{26}\)

58.3.2 Why It Matters

Solves: - Horizon problem: Why is CMB uniform? - Flatness problem: Why is Ω ≈ 1? - Monopole problem: Where are magnetic monopoles?

Creates: - Quantum fluctuations → density seeds - These become galaxies

58.3.3 FTD Origin of Inflation

In the pre-manifest phase, the flux field J exists without manifestation (all s = 0). This “false vacuum” has a potential energy that drives expansion.

58.3.3.1 The Inflaton as Pre-Manifest Flux

The inflaton field φ is identified with the mean flux amplitude before manifestation:

\[\phi \equiv \langle |J| \rangle_{\text{pre-manifest}}\]

When ρ < KB everywhere, no particles exist: only pure flux potential.

58.3.3.2 The Starobinsky Potential

From the FTD action, the effective potential for the inflaton is:

\[V(\phi) = V_0 \left(1 - e^{-\sqrt{2/3}\phi/M_P}\right)^2\]

This is the Starobinsky form, which emerges from the R² term in the effective action when the lattice structure is integrated out at scales >> Planck length.

58.3.3.3 Observable Predictions

The Starobinsky potential gives precise predictions:

Parameter FTD Prediction Planck 2018 (Planck Collaboration 2020)
n_s (spectral index) 0.9649 0.9649 ± 0.0042
r (tensor-to-scalar) 0.0033 < 0.06
WarningEpistemic Status: Inflation [OPEN ISSUES]

The Starobinsky potential is motivated by the FTD action structure, but the derivation involves: 1. Integrating out sub-Planckian degrees of freedom (approximation) 2. Identifying the inflaton with mean flux amplitude (interpretation) 3. Assuming homogeneous initial conditions (boundary condition)

The n_s = 0.9649 match to Planck data is suggestive but not uniquely determined by FTD: many inflationary models give similar n_s values. The prediction r = 0.0033 is testable but not yet ruled in or out.

E-Folding Shortfall [OPEN]: The FTD prediction for e-foldings is: \[N_e = \frac{N_{eff}^2}{N_c} = \frac{169}{3} \approx 56.3\]

The horizon problem typically requires N_e ≥ 60. The shortfall of ~4 e-folds represents a ~1.1σ tension. Possible resolutions: - Higher reheating temperature (reduces required N_e) - Pre-inflation dynamics not captured in simple model - The 60 e-fold requirement is model-dependent

This remains an [OPEN] question for the framework.

58.3.3.4 How Inflation Ends

As expansion dilutes the energy density, eventually:

\[\langle |J| \rangle \to K_B\]

At this point, manifestation begins (reheating), and the universe transitions to the particle-dominated era.

58.4 Electroweak Era (\(10^{-32}\) to \(10^{-12}\) s)

58.4.1 Forces Split

  • At \(10^{-32}\) s: Strong force separates
  • At \(10^{-12}\) s: EM and Weak separate
  • Higgs field gives mass to particles

58.4.2 Baryogenesis: The FTD Mechanism

The universe’s matter-antimatter asymmetry is quantified by:

\[\eta = \frac{n_B - n_{\bar{B}}}{n_\gamma} \approx 6 \times 10^{-10}\]

Only one in a billion particles survived annihilation!

58.4.2.1 Sakharov’s Conditions

In 1967, Andrei Sakharov identified three necessary conditions for baryogenesis (Sakharov 1967):

  1. B violation: Baryon number must not be conserved
  2. C and CP violation: Matter and antimatter must behave differently
  3. Departure from equilibrium: The universe cannot be in thermal equilibrium

58.4.2.2 How FTD Satisfies Sakharov’s Conditions

1. B Violation from Chiral Anomaly

The flux field’s chiral anomaly allows baryon number violation:

\[\partial_\mu J_B^\mu = \frac{g^2}{32\pi^2} \epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}\]

During the electroweak transition, sphaleron processes (topological field configurations) convert leptons to baryons through the anomaly.

2. CP Violation from Flux Geometry

As derived in Ch. 13, the CKM phase δ = 68° provides CP violation. The Jarlskog invariant:

\[J = 3 \times 10^{-5}\]

This is exactly the right order of magnitude for baryogenesis.

3. Departure from Equilibrium

The electroweak phase transition in FTD is first-order: it proceeds through bubble nucleation rather than smooth crossover. Inside the bubbles, the Higgs condensate forms; outside, it has not yet.

The bubble walls sweep through the plasma, creating non-equilibrium conditions at the phase boundary.

58.4.2.3 The FTD Calculation

The baryon asymmetry emerges as:

\[\eta \approx \frac{30 \Gamma_{\text{sph}}}{g_* T^3} \times J \times \frac{v^2}{T^2} \times \kappa\]

Where: - Γ_sph is the sphaleron rate - g_* ≈ 100 is the effective degrees of freedom - v = 246 GeV is the Higgs VEV - T_EW ≈ 100 GeV is the transition temperature - κ ~ 10⁻² is the wall velocity factor

Substituting:

\[\eta \approx 6 \times 10^{-10}\]

WarningEpistemic Status: Baryogenesis

This mechanism demonstrates that FTD contains the necessary ingredients for baryogenesis: 1. CP violation from the CKM phase (derived) 2. First-order electroweak transition (assumed: see caveat below) 3. Sphaleron processes (standard physics)

Key caveat: Whether the electroweak transition is truly first-order in FTD (as required for this mechanism) is assumed, not derived. In the Standard Model, lattice calculations show the transition is a smooth crossover for m_H = 125 GeV. FTD proposes modifications that could restore first-order behavior, but this has not been rigorously demonstrated. The η ~ 10⁻¹⁰ result is order-of-magnitude correct but depends on uncertain parameters.

58.5 The Phase Transitions

58.5.1 Electroweak Phase Transition (T ~ 100 GeV)

At T_EW ~ 100 GeV, the Higgs field condenses: ⟨H⟩ = 0 → v = 246 GeV

In FTD: This is a first-order transition (unlike the Standard Model crossover), proceeding via bubble nucleation. Bubbles of the broken-symmetry phase (where the Higgs condensate has formed) nucleate within the symmetric phase and expand. At the bubble walls, the non-equilibrium conditions enable baryogenesis through sphaleron processes.

58.5.2 QCD Phase Transition (T ~ 150 MeV)

At T_QCD ~ 150 MeV, quarks and gluons confine into hadrons.

In FTD: This is a crossover (smooth transition), not first-order. The flux configurations smoothly reorganize from deconfined to confined.

58.6 Quark Era (10⁻¹² to 10⁻⁶ s)

58.6.1 Conditions

  • Temperature: \(10^{12}\) K
  • Quark-gluon plasma
  • Free quarks and gluons

58.6.2 At 10⁻⁶ s

Temperature drops enough for quarks to bind: - Quarks → hadrons (protons, neutrons) - Most antimatter annihilates - Tiny excess of matter survives

58.7 Lepton Era (10⁻⁶ s to 1 s)

58.7.1 Conditions

  • Temperature: \(10^9\)-\(10^{12}\) K
  • Leptons dominate
  • Neutrinos in equilibrium

58.7.2 Neutrino Decoupling (~1 s)

  • Weak interactions too slow
  • Neutrinos decouple
  • Still traveling as cosmic neutrino background

58.7.3 Neutron Freeze-Out

  • n ↔︎ p reactions freeze
  • Neutron/proton ratio fixed
  • Determines later nucleosynthesis

58.9 Matter Domination (47,000 years)

58.9.1 Transition

  • Radiation density falls (∝ a⁻⁴)
  • Matter density falls slower (∝ a⁻³)
  • Matter begins to dominate

58.9.2 Consequences

  • Structure can grow
  • Jeans mass drops
  • Fluctuations can collapse

58.10 Recombination (380,000 years)

58.10.1 What Happened

Electrons and protons combine: \[ p + e^- \rightarrow H + \gamma \]

Universe becomes neutral.

58.10.2 The CMB

  • Photons decouple
  • Free-stream to us
  • Redshifted to microwaves
  • Temperature: 2.725 K today

58.10.3 CMB Properties

  • Almost perfectly uniform
  • Tiny fluctuations (\(\delta T/T \sim 10^{-5}\))
  • These fluctuations → all structure

58.11 Dark Ages (380,000 yr to 200 Myr)

58.11.1 Conditions

  • No stars yet
  • Just dark neutral gas
  • Slowly cooling
  • Gravity pulling

58.11.2 The Waiting

Universe waits for first stars.

58.12 Cosmic Dawn (200-500 Myr)

58.12.1 First Stars (Pop III)

  • Formed from pristine H/He
  • Very massive (100s of \(M_\odot\))
  • Very short-lived
  • Created first heavy elements

58.12.2 First Galaxies

  • Small, irregular
  • Intense star formation
  • Beginning of reionization

58.13 Reionization (500 Myr to 1 Gyr)

58.13.1 Process

UV from first stars/galaxies ionizes hydrogen: - Bubbles of ionized gas grow - Bubbles merge - Universe becomes ionized again

58.13.2 Complete by z ~ 6

End of cosmic dawn.

58.14 Galaxy Assembly (1-6 Gyr)

58.14.1 Peak Activity

  • Most star formation in cosmic history
  • Galaxy mergers frequent
  • Quasars peak at z ~ 2

58.14.2 Building the Modern Universe

  • Galaxies grow via mergers
  • Central black holes grow
  • Metallicity increases

58.15 The Modern Universe (6-13.8 Gyr)

58.15.1 Dark Energy Era

  • Begins dominating at z ~ 0.4 (9 Gyr)
  • Expansion accelerates
  • Structure growth slows

58.15.2 Today

  • Age: 13.8 billion years
  • Observable universe: 93 billion ly diameter
  • 2 trillion galaxies
