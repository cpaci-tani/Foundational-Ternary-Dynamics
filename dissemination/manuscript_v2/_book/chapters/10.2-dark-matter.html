56  Dark Matter

“The invisible scaffold”

NoteKey Revelation

Most of the matter in the universe is invisible. We know it exists because we see its gravitational effects, but we have never detected it directly. Dark matter is the skeleton on which galaxies are built.

WarningEpistemic Status

Dark matter in FTD remains [OPEN]. The mechanisms proposed in this chapter are speculative interpretations, not derivations from the framework axioms. Multiple dark matter candidates remain under investigation. (For the FTD mechanism, see Ch. 22.)

56.1 Evidence for Dark Matter

56.1.1 Galaxy Rotation Curves

Expected (Keplerian): \[ v(r) \propto r^{-1/2} \]

Observed: \[ v(r) \approx \text{constant at large } r \]

This discrepancy between expected and observed rotation curves requires additional unseen mass.

56.1.2 Galaxy Cluster Masses

Multiple methods give consistent results: - Virial theorem: \(M = \frac{3\sigma^2 R}{G}\) - X-ray gas: Hydrostatic equilibrium - Gravitational lensing: Light bending

All give: \(M_{\text{total}} >> M_{\text{visible}}\)

56.1.3 Gravitational Lensing

Mass bends light: - Strong lensing: Multiple images, arcs - Weak lensing: Statistical distortions - Maps invisible mass directly

56.1.4 The Bullet Cluster

Two clusters collided, providing smoking-gun evidence for dark matter. The galaxies passed through each other largely unaffected. The hot gas collided and was left behind at the collision point. But gravitational lensing reveals that most of the mass (dark matter) passed through like the galaxies, now separated from the gas. This proves dark matter is a distinct component from ordinary matter.

56.1.5 CMB Anisotropies

Pattern of fluctuations requires dark matter: - Baryons alone do not match - Need ~5× more non-baryonic matter - Confirmed by Planck satellite

56.3 Candidates

56.3.1 WIMPs (Weakly Interacting Massive Particles)

  • Mass: 10-1000 GeV
  • Weak-scale interactions
  • Thermal relic from early universe
  • Not found (so far)

56.3.2 Axions

  • Mass: \(10^{-6}\)-\(10^{-3}\) eV
  • Very light, very cold
  • Solve strong CP problem
  • Active searches ongoing

56.3.3 Sterile Neutrinos

  • Mass: keV scale
  • Right-handed neutrinos
  • Warm dark matter
  • X-ray signatures?

56.3.4 Primordial Black Holes

  • Formed in early universe
  • Various mass ranges
  • Most constrained but not ruled out

56.3.5 MACHOs (Massive Compact Halo Objects)

  • Normal matter, just dark
  • Brown dwarfs, black holes
  • Microlensing surveys
  • Not enough to explain observations

Dark matter is the far-field tail of the self-field halo:

ORDINARY MATTER (the seed):
  Manifested particle (s = +/-1) at one lattice site
  Interacts via all forces (Coulomb, gravity, color)
  Emits/absorbs light

DARK MATTER (the halo):
  Self-field envelope (s = 0, |J| > 0) extending ~15-23 voxels
  Only gravitates (F = G_N * grad(rho), rho = |J|)
  No electromagnetic interaction (F_em = -alpha * s * grad(phi), s = 0)

The key insight: every particle carries its own dark matter halo. The self-field is gravitationally active but electromagnetically invisible because void sites (s = 0) do not couple to the Coulomb potential.

56.5.3 Stability: Why Dark Matter Persists

The FTD engine uses selective damping (default behavior): energy dissipation only occurs within 1 lattice hop of a manifested particle. Far-field flux — everything beyond the immediate particle neighborhood — propagates losslessly through the vacuum.

This means the self-field halo, once established, persists indefinitely. The dark matter does not decay because there is no damping channel in the vacuum. This is verified by engine test DS-1 (sub-threshold flux stability under selective damping).

Ordinary matter consists of manifested particles with states s = +/-1. These interact via all forces and emit or absorb light. Dark matter consists of their self-field halos where s = 0 everywhere. These only gravitate and have no electromagnetic interaction.

The gravitational coupling in FTD acts on the density gradient ∇ρ̄, where ρ = |J|. The flux amplitude contributes regardless of manifestation state:

\[F_{\text{gravity}} = G_{\text{bias}} \cdot \nabla\bar{\rho}(x)\]

Coherent flux patterns in the void create density inhomogeneities → gravitational effects → dark matter behavior.

56.5.5 Why It Does Not Shine

Dark matter does not interact electromagnetically because: - EM forces require charge (q ≠ 0) - Void fluctuations have no charge - Only manifested particles (s = ±1) carry charge

The flux correlations are uncharged vacuum modes.

56.7 Direct Detection

56.7.1 Principle

Dark matter particles scatter off nuclei:

DM particle → [nucleus] → DM (deflected) + nucleus (recoils)

Detect nuclear recoil.

56.7.2 The Migdal Effect: A New Detection Channel

In January 2026, the first direct observation of the Migdal effect was reported in Nature (Yi et al. 2026), achieving 5σ statistical significance with 6 candidate events from ~\(10^6\) recorded.

The Migdal effect occurs when a nucleus experiences sudden acceleration (from collision with a neutral particle), causing electrons to be ejected:

Neutral particle → [nucleus accelerates suddenly]
                           ↓
                   [electrons cannot follow]
                           ↓
                   [electron ejected = SIGNAL]

Why This Matters for Dark Matter:

Light dark matter (MeV-GeV scale) produces nuclear recoils too small to detect directly. But the Migdal effect provides an amplification mechanism:

Direct Recoil Migdal Enhancement
Sub-threshold signal Electron ejection detectable
Requires heavy DM Works for light DM
Limited by detector threshold Extends sensitivity

FTD Interpretation:

In FTD terms, the Migdal effect demonstrates flux perturbation propagation:

  1. Dark matter (|J| < κ_B) collides with nucleus
  2. Creates sharp flux gradient ∇J at collision site
  3. Gradient propagates through void substrate
  4. Electron shell experiences threshold crossing
  5. Electron manifests as free particle (detectable)

This is precisely the mechanism FTD predicts: flux disturbances in the void can trigger manifestation transitions at distant locations from the primary interaction.

NoteValidation of FTD Detection Mechanism

The Migdal effect confirmation validates the core FTD prediction that sub-threshold interactions can produce observable signatures through flux propagation and non-local threshold crossing.

56.7.3 Experiments

  • XENON, LUX-ZEPLIN (liquid xenon)
  • SuperCDMS (cryogenic)

56.7.4 Status

No confirmed dark matter detection yet. However, the Migdal effect confirmation opens new sensitivity windows for light dark matter searches.
