## 1. The Lattice Hodge Decomposition

$$ C^0 \xrightarrow{d_0} C^1 \xrightarrow{d_1} C^2 \xrightarrow{d_2} C^3, \qquad d_{k+1}d_k = 0 $$

$$ \delta_k = d_{k-1}^{\dagger}, \qquad \Delta_k = d_{k-1}\delta_k + \delta_{k+1}d_k $$

Under this calculus, any smooth flux vector field $J \in C^1$ can be uniquely decomposed into three mutually orthogonal components:

$$ J = d\phi + \delta B + H $$

*   $d\phi$: The **longitudinal / exact sector**. This is the curl-free gradient generated by the scalar potential $\phi$ (e.g., the pure electrostatic response of the Gauss solver: $J_{\infty} \leftarrow J_{\infty} - \nabla\phi$).
*   $\delta B$: The **transverse / coexact sector**. This is the divergence-free circulation generated by a vector potential $B$ (e.g., pure rotational/magnetic response).
*   $H$: The **harmonic / topological sector**. This represents fields that are simultaneously curl-free and divergence-free ($dH = 0$ and $\delta H = 0$).
