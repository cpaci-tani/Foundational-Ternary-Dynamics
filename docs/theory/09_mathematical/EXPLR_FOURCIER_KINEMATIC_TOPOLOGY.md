# Part I: The Critical Deltoid

## 1.1 The Deltoid Family [THEOREM: FKT-1]

Consider the two-parameter family of plane curves generated by counter-rotating complex exponentials:

$$z(t) = A \, e^{it} + B \, e^{-2it}, \quad t \in [0, 2\pi],$$

where A, B > 0 are real amplitudes. This is the family of **deltoids** (hypocycloids of three cusps). The curve traces a path on the plane where the outer circle (radius A, counterclockwise) and inner circle (radius B, clockwise at triple frequency) interact.

The topology of these curves depends on the amplitude ratio:

| Ratio |A|/|B| | Topology | Cusps | Enclosed regions |
|--------|----------|-------|----------------|
| > 2 | Smooth convex loop | 0 | 1 |
| **= 2** | **Critical deltoid (cusp transition)** | **3** | **1 (pinched)** |
| < 2 | Self-intersecting, 3 enclosed lobes | 3 crossings | 3 |

The critical condition is:

$$|A| = 2|B| \quad \text{(cusp-smooth boundary)}.$$

At this precise ratio, the curve has three **cusps** — points where the tangent vector vanishes. For |A| > 2|B|, the cusps open into smooth bulges. For |A| < 2|B|, the cusps become self-intersection crossings enclosing finite regions.

## 1.2 The FTD Coefficient [THEOREM]

The canonical Fourcier curve (FOURCIER_CURVE_ANALYSIS.md) has:

- Frequency-1 (x-channel): a₁ = 1.0
- Frequency-2 (x-channel): a₂ = 1/2 = 2/N_base

The complex representation of the first two harmonics is:

$$z_{1,2}(t) = 1 \cdot e^{it} + \frac{1}{2} \cdot e^{-2it},$$

where the counter-rotation arises from the sign alternation in the y-channel (b₂ = −1/2).

Check: |A|/|B| = 1.0 / 0.5 = 2.0. **Exactly the critical ratio.**

The Fourcier curve, restricted to its first two harmonics, sits precisely on the cusp-smooth boundary of the deltoid family. This is not a numerical coincidence — the coefficient 2/N_base = 1/2 is determined by the framework integer N_base = 4, and N_base = 4 is selected by self-referential closure (FOURCIER_CURVE_ANALYSIS.md §2.5).

**Physical interpretation:** The Fourcier lives on topology's phase boundary. It is simultaneously:
- The last smooth curve (no self-intersections from {1,2} alone)
- The first structured curve (cusps mark where lobes would form)

The deltoid cusps are the **geometric precondition** for the lobe closure that occurs at the next harmonic level.

**Claim FKT-1 [THEOREM]:** The FTD one-loop coefficient 2/N_base = 1/2 places the first two Fourcier harmonics at the critical cusp-smooth transition |A| = 2|B| of the deltoid family. The Fourcier sits on the phase boundary of planar curve topology.

# Part II: Lobe Genesis at the Quaternionic Level

## 2.1 Self-Intersection Through the Origin [THEOREM: FKT-2]

Adding the frequency-4 harmonic (a₄ = 1/2, b₄ = +1/2) converts the critical deltoid into a self-intersecting curve with three enclosed lobes. The mechanism is precise: the quaternionic harmonic drives the curve through the origin at three equally spaced parameter values.

**Theorem.** For the three-harmonic Fourcier z(t) = (x(t), y(t)) with harmonics {1, 2, 4}:

$$x(t) = \cos t + \tfrac{1}{2}\cos 2t + \tfrac{1}{2}\cos 4t,$$
$$y(t) = \sin t - \tfrac{1}{2}\sin 2t + \tfrac{1}{2}\sin 4t,$$

the curve passes through the origin at t = π/3, π, 5π/3.

**Proof** (for t = π/3):

$$x(\pi/3) = \cos(\pi/3) + \tfrac{1}{2}\cos(2\pi/3) + \tfrac{1}{2}\cos(4\pi/3) = \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2}(-\tfrac{1}{2}) + \tfrac{1}{2}(-\tfrac{1}{2}) = \tfrac{1}{2} - \tfrac{1}{4} - \tfrac{1}{4} = 0.$$

$$y(\pi/3) = \sin(\pi/3) - \tfrac{1}{2}\sin(2\pi/3) + \tfrac{1}{2}\sin(4\pi/3) = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2}(-\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}) = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} - \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} = 0.$$

The proofs for t = π and t = 5π/3 are analogous (verified numerically to machine precision). The three crossing points are separated by 2π/3, giving exact **trigonal symmetry** — consistent with the 3-fold deep-minimum structure verified in FOUND_FOURCIER_ONTIC_TOOL.md (OT-3).

## 2.2 What the Theorem Means

The frequency-4 harmonic creates **topology** — it converts the cusped boundary curve (from §1) into a self-intersecting curve with three enclosed regions. Before freq-4, the curve had structure (cusps) but no enclosure. After freq-4, it has three lobes.

This is the geometric content of OT-5 ("Non-commutativity creates topology"): the quaternionic level ℍ is where commutativity is lost in the Cayley-Dickson tower, and it is precisely where the Fourcier curve first encloses area.

## 2.3 Residual from Higher Harmonics

The 5-harmonic Fourcier does NOT pass through the origin at t = π/3. The freq-8 and freq-16 contributions produce a residual:

| Harmonic | x-contribution at π/3 | y-contribution at π/3 |
|----------|---------------------|---------------------|
| freq 8 | −0.200 | −0.303 |
| freq 16 | −0.031 | −0.054 |
| **Total residual** | **−0.231** | **−0.357** |

The octonionic and sedenionic harmonics **shift** the crossing point away from the origin but do not destroy the topological enclosure (verified by the persistence of 3 lobes in the full 5-harmonic curve). This is consistent with the "fine structure" interpretation: higher harmonics refine the shape without changing the topology.

**Claim FKT-2 [THEOREM]:** The frequency-4 harmonic of the Fourcier curve creates three self-intersections through the origin at t = π/3, π, 5π/3 (exact trigonal symmetry), converting cusps into enclosed lobes. Topology (enclosure) is born at the quaternionic level.

# Part III: Rotation Parity and the Cayley-Dickson Conjugation

## 3.1 Complex Decomposition [THEOREM: FKT-3]

Each Fourcier harmonic at frequency f can be decomposed into positive-frequency (counterclockwise, CCW) and negative-frequency (clockwise, CW) complex exponentials:

$$x_f \cos(ft) + y_f \sin(ft) = c_+ \, e^{+ift} + c_- \, e^{-ift},$$

where

$$c_+ = \frac{a_f + b_f}{2}, \qquad c_- = \frac{a_f - b_f}{2}.$$

Here a_f and b_f are the x- and y-coefficients at frequency f from the canonical Fourcier definition. If c₊ dominates, the harmonic rotates CCW; if c₋ dominates, it rotates CW.

## 3.2 The Rotation Table [THEOREM]

Applied to the canonical coefficients:

| Freq | a_f | b_f | c₊ | c₋ | Purity | CD Level | Rotation |
|------|------|------|------|------|--------|----------|----------|
| 1 | 1.0 | 1.0 | 1.0 | 0 | 100% | ℝ | **CCW** (pure) |
| 2 | 0.5 | −0.5 | 0 | 0.5 | 100% | ℂ | **CW** (pure) |
| 4 | 0.5 | 0.5 | 0.5 | 0 | 100% | ℍ | **CCW** (pure) |
| 8 | 0.4 | −0.35 | 0.025 | 0.375 | 93.75% | 𝕆 | **CW** (impure) |
| 16 | 0.0625 | 0.0625 | 0.0625 | 0 | 100% | 𝕊 | **CCW** (pure) |

The pattern is unambiguously **alternating**: CCW / CW / CCW / CW / CCW.

## 3.3 Origin of the Alternation

The sign alternation in the y-coefficients follows sign(b_f) = (−1)^k for f = 2^k (FOURCIER_CURVE_ANALYSIS.md §2.4). This is the **Cayley-Dickson conjugation signature**: each doubling step introduces a conjugation (sign flip) in the imaginary part.

When b_f has the same sign as a_f (k even: f = 1, 4, 16), the harmonic is purely CCW (c₋ = 0). When b_f has opposite sign to a_f (k odd: f = 2, 8), the harmonic is predominantly CW (c₊ ≈ 0). The alternation is exact at ℝ, ℂ, ℍ, and 𝕊 levels.

## 3.4 The Octonionic Impurity

At the octonion level (freq 8), the rotation is not pure CW. The minority component is:

$$c_{+,\text{oct}} = \frac{a_8 + b_8}{2} = \frac{0.4 + (-0.35)}{2} = \frac{0.05}{2} = 0.025 = \frac{1}{40}.$$

This impurity arises because |a₈| ≠ |b₈| (the octonionic coefficients are asymmetric: a₈ = 2/5, b₈ = −7/20). The asymmetry is:

$$|a_8| - |b_8| = 0.4 - 0.35 = 0.05 = \frac{1}{20} = \frac{1}{N_\text{base} \times (N_\text{eff} - 2N_\text{base})}.$$

This is the coefficient gap 1/(N_base × two_loop_integer), already established in the generating function (FOURCIER_CURVE_ANALYSIS.md §4). Its kinematic consequence is that the octonionic harmonic has a residual CCW component that prevents perfect counter-rotation.

**Interpretation:** In the Cayley-Dickson tower, associativity is lost at 𝕆. A perfectly counter-rotating harmonic (c₊ = 0) would imply perfect conjugation — the algebra acting cleanly as an involution. The non-zero c₊ at the octonion level is the kinematic manifestation of associativity loss: the conjugation operation is no longer a clean involution, because (ab)c ≠ a(bc) means the composition of rotations is path-dependent.

**Claim FKT-3 [THEOREM]:** The Cayley-Dickson conjugation sign pattern produces a strict CCW/CW/CCW/CW/CCW alternating rotation across the five Fourcier harmonics. Purity is 100% at levels ℝ, ℂ, ℍ, 𝕊 and 93.75% at level 𝕆. The octonionic impurity c₊ = 1/40 is the kinematic signature of associativity loss.

# Part VI: The 3-from-2 Composition Law
