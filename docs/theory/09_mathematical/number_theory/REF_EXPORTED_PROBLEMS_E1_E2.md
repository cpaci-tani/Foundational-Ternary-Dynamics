## Problem 1 — Joint independence of the cubic-lattice Watson constants

**Setting.** For the three classical cubic lattices, Watson's triple integrals (1939) give the return Green's-function constants. The BCC constant is exactly Γ(1/4)⁴/(4π³) (Watson 1939). The SC and FCC constants have closed forms involving Γ-values at 24th- and 3rd-division points respectively (Glasser–Zucker 1977, with the known erratum history for the SC form; see also Borwein–Zucker and Guttmann's 2010 survey).

**Problem.** Determine the transcendence degree over ℚ of the field generated by
$$\{\pi,\ \Gamma(1/4)\} \cup \{\text{the SC and FCC Watson constants}\}.$$
In particular: are the SC and FCC constants algebraically independent of {π, Γ(1/4)}?

**What is known (state of the art).** The proven **floor is trdeg 2**: π and Γ(1/4) are algebraically independent (Chudnovsky 1976), likewise π and Γ(1/3). Nesterenko (1996) reaches trdeg 3 only by adjoining a *same-field* exponential — {π, Γ(1/4), eᵖ} and {π, Γ(1/3), e^{π√3}} — so the two triples share only π and do **not** combine to give the joint {π, Γ(1/4), Γ(1/3)}. That joint statement (trdeg 3) is itself **open** (Waldschmidt Conjecture 5.23), and the SC (Γ(1/24)-class) values are **not even individually proven transcendental** (Fermat-curve genus > 1 / higher-dimensional CM abelian-variety obstruction, as with Γ(1/5)). The **conjectural value is trdeg 4** (the SC/FCC/BCC constants are periods of CM points of *distinct* imaginary-quadratic fields, disc −4/−3/the 24-content); full independence follows from the **Rohrlich–Lang conjecture** and the **Grothendieck period conjecture** (André §24.6), both open — Lang's conjecture `trdeg = 1+φ(n)/2` is proven only for n = 3, 4, 6, and GPC for CM abelian varieties is "still widely open" (Gao–Ullmo 2024). **So the entire gap 2 → 4 coincides with Rohrlich–Lang / GPC; no unconditional bound above 2 is currently available.** A sharper sub-question: does the SC constant's ℚ(ζ₂₄) CM abelian variety admit a reduction under a *more tractable* hypothesis than full Rohrlich–Lang?

**Why it matters mathematically.** It is the smallest concrete instance of "periods of distinct CM points don't talk to each other" that arises from a single classical family (Watson's three integrals), and any progress sharpens the arithmetic of lattice Green's functions generally.

## Problem 2 — Independence of exponential lattice periods

**Setting.** Let σ(k) be a cubic-lattice symbol (a trigonometric polynomial, e.g. 1 − cos k₁ cos k₂ cos k₃), and for rational τ > 0 consider the heat-kernel-type torus integrals
$$H_\sigma(\tau) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\int_{[0,2\pi]^3} e^{-\tau\,\sigma(k)}\, d^3k$$
— exponential periods with theta-type content at *transcendental* nome (these arise as diffusive-scaling limits of lattice heat kernels).

**Problem.** Are the values H_σ(τ) (τ ∈ ℚ₊, classical σ) algebraically independent of {π, Γ(1/4)}? More modestly: is any single H_σ(τ) transcendental over ℚ(π, Γ(1/4))?

**What is known (state of the art).** Essentially nothing beyond expectation: these are exponential periods (e-content mixed with Γ/π-periods), Schanuel-adjacent, and Nesterenko-type methods reach only *same-field* exponentials (eᵖ jointly with Γ(1/4)), not general lattice exponential periods. **Even the transcendence of a single value over ℚ(π, Γ(1/4)) is open.** Independence would follow from the **exponential period conjecture** (Fresán–Jossen, Conj. 8.2.6: `trdeg = dim G_M` for an exponential motive M — the exponential-motives analogue of the Grothendieck period conjecture), which is open. A concrete sub-question with a possible *unconditional* handle: identify the exponential motive M whose periods are the H_σ(τ), so that `dim G_M` (an unconditional **upper** bound on the trdeg) can be computed — it may separate H_σ(τ) from {π, Γ(1/4)} without the conjecture. Whether Schanuel's conjecture alone suffices (strictly weaker than Conj. 8.2.6 here) is also open.

**Why it matters mathematically.** A minimal, integral-defined test family for independence questions *between* the exponential-period and classical-period worlds — more concrete than generic Schanuel instances, with a lattice combinatorial handle.

## Problem 3 — The mixed-coupling cubic lattice Green's function

**Setting.** The Green's function at the origin of the "18-point" cubic lattice with equal nearest- and next-nearest-neighbor mixture,
$$\sigma_{18}(k) = 1 - \tfrac{1}{6}\textstyle\sum_i \cos k_i \;-\; \tfrac{1}{6}\sum_{i<j} \cos k_i \cos k_j, \qquad W_{18} = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\int_{[0,2\pi]^3} \frac{d^3k}{\sigma_{18}(k)} \approx 1.2679\ldots$$

**Problem.** (a) *[Resolved below.]* The moment generating function satisfies a minimal linear ODE of **order 4**, degree 12. (b) *[Resolved below.]* The operator is **irreducible** over ℚ̄(z) and is **not** a symmetric power of a second-order operator — so W₁₈ is *not* a Γ-quotient of the classical elliptic (SC/FCC/BCC) type; it is the simplest *named* cubic-lattice mixture whose Green's constant is a genuinely irreducible order-4 period, leaving the classical Γ-world. **The remaining open question:** what is the arithmetic nature of this order-4 period? The operator's local system is **not self-dual** — at the finite singular points z = 1, −2, −3 its exponent multiset {0, ½, 1, 2} is symmetric about no center (0+2 ≠ ½+1), so it carries no nondegenerate monodromy-invariant bilinear form — which rules out the rigid-Calabi–Yau / weight-4-modular / Sym^k-elliptic / K3 types (all requiring a pure-weight self-dual variation of Hodge structure). Its natural home is the theory of **diagonals of rational functions** (W₁₈ is the diagonal of 1/(1 − z·σ₁₈)); the open problem is a Hadamard-type factorization, or a quadratic pullback rationalizing the ½ℤ-exponent lattice, giving its closed form.

**What is known.** The three pure lattices are all CM-evaluable (Watson 1939; Glasser–Zucker 1977; Joyce 1973), with LGF ODEs of order 3 (SC) and order 6 (FCC, Koutschan 2013). The moment generating function m_n = CT[σ₁₈ⁿ] is holonomic (Lipshitz 1988). Reconstructing from 171 exact moments by modular-rank detection (cross-checked on two 61-bit primes; the same code recovers the known order-3 SC operator) gives the **minimal annihilating operator: order 4, degree 12**, with leading coefficient
$$p_4(z) = 4\,z^3\,(z-1)(z+2)(z+3)(z+6)(z+8)\,(3z^4+16z^3+24z^2-24z+16),$$
and exact local exponents $\{0,0,0,\tfrac12\}$ at $z=0$, $\{0,\tfrac12,1,2\}$ at each of $z=1,-2,-3,-6,-8$, and $\{1,\tfrac32,\tfrac52,3\}$ at $z=\infty$ — **every exponent in $\tfrac12\mathbb{Z}$, and no maximally-unipotent (MUM) point**. A Sage/ore_algebra factorization then shows the operator is **irreducible over ℚ̄(z)** (`right_factor()` = None; the routine self-validates by finding a factor of a Fuchsian reducible operator and returning None on the irreducible elliptic-K operator), with true singular locus $\{0,1,-2,-3,\infty\}$ (the $z=-6,-8$ and the complex roots of the leading coefficient are apparent). Independently, it is **not a symmetric cube** of a second-order operator: the $z=0$ exponents $\{0,0,0,\tfrac12\}$ are incompatible with any $\mathrm{Sym}^3$ pattern $\{3a,2a+b,a+2b,3b\}$ (three equal values force $a=b$, hence all four equal). So although the $\tfrac12\mathbb{Z}$-exponent lattice *resembles* a symmetric power of an elliptic operator (as the SC order-3 operator genuinely is), here that structure is **absent** — W₁₈ is a new higher period, not an elliptic Γ-quotient.

**Why it matters mathematically.** Either outcome is clean: a new CM evaluation extends the classical Watson family; a non-CM verdict gives the minimal natural example of a cubic-lattice mixture escaping it — directly relevant to the arithmetic classification of LGF operators.
