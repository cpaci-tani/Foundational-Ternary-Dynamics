## Problem 1 — Joint independence of the cubic-lattice Watson constants

**Setting.** For the three classical cubic lattices, Watson's triple integrals (1939) give the return Green's-function constants. The BCC constant is exactly Γ(1/4)⁴/(4π³) (Watson 1939). The SC and FCC constants have closed forms involving Γ-values at 24th- and 3rd-division points respectively (Glasser–Zucker 1977, with the known erratum history for the SC form; see also Borwein–Zucker and Guttmann's 2010 survey).

**Problem.** Determine the transcendence degree over ℚ of the field generated by
$$\{\pi,\ \Gamma(1/4)\} \cup \{\text{the SC and FCC Watson constants}\}.$$
In particular: are the SC and FCC constants algebraically independent of {π, Γ(1/4)}?

**What is known.** π and Γ(1/4) are algebraically independent (Chudnovsky 1976); π, Γ(1/4) *and* e^π are (Nesterenko 1996). Neither result covers Γ(1/24)- or Γ(1/3)-class values jointly with Γ(1/4). The Γ-values at different CM division points correspond to periods of non-isogenous CM elliptic curves (discriminants −4 vs −3 vs the class-field content at 24), for which joint independence is expected (Grothendieck-period-conjecture heuristics) but unproven.

**Why it matters mathematically.** It is the smallest concrete instance of "periods of distinct CM points don't talk to each other" that arises from a single classical family (Watson's three integrals), and any progress sharpens the arithmetic of lattice Green's functions generally.

## Problem 2 — Independence of exponential lattice periods

**Setting.** Let σ(k) be a cubic-lattice symbol (a trigonometric polynomial, e.g. 1 − cos k₁ cos k₂ cos k₃), and for rational τ > 0 consider the heat-kernel-type torus integrals
$$H_\sigma(\tau) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\int_{[0,2\pi]^3} e^{-\tau\,\sigma(k)}\, d^3k$$
— exponential periods with theta-type content at *transcendental* nome (these arise as diffusive-scaling limits of lattice heat kernels).

**Problem.** Are the values H_σ(τ) (τ ∈ ℚ₊, classical σ) algebraically independent of {π, Γ(1/4)}? More modestly: is any single H_σ(τ) transcendental over ℚ(π, Γ(1/4))?

**What is known.** Essentially nothing beyond expectation: the values are Schanuel-adjacent (mixing e-content with Γ/π-periods), and no Nesterenko-type result reaches them. Even the transcendence of individual values appears open.

**Why it matters mathematically.** A minimal, integral-defined test family for independence questions *between* the exponential-period and classical-period worlds — more concrete than generic Schanuel instances, with a lattice combinatorial handle.

## Problem 3 — The mixed-coupling cubic lattice Green's function

**Setting.** The Green's function at the origin of the "18-point" cubic lattice with equal nearest- and next-nearest-neighbor mixture,
$$\sigma_{18}(k) = 1 - \tfrac{1}{6}\textstyle\sum_i \cos k_i \;-\; \tfrac{1}{6}\sum_{i<j} \cos k_i \cos k_j, \qquad W_{18} = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\int_{[0,2\pi]^3} \frac{d^3k}{\sigma_{18}(k)} \approx 1.2679\ldots$$

**Problem.** (a) Derive the linear ODE satisfied by the generating function of the associated structure-function moments (creative telescoping applies; the operator is expected to be of Calabi–Yau type per Guttmann's LGF/ODE program). (b) Classify: is the operator of modular/CM type — giving W₁₈ a Γ-quotient closed form like the three classical lattices — or genuinely non-CM, making this the simplest *named* cubic-lattice mixture whose Green's constant leaves the classical Γ-world?

**What is known.** The three pure lattices are all CM-evaluable (Watson 1939; Glasser–Zucker 1977; Joyce's exact-evaluation program; Joyce–Delves methods handle anisotropic and NNN couplings). No evaluation or classification of the equal-mixture σ₁₈ appears in the literature we could locate (Guttmann 2010 and the LGF/Calabi–Yau papers give the method, not this case).

**Why it matters mathematically.** Either outcome is clean: a new CM evaluation extends the classical Watson family; a non-CM verdict gives the minimal natural example of a cubic-lattice mixture escaping it — directly relevant to the arithmetic classification of LGF operators.
