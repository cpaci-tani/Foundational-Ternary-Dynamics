## 1. Definition

For every integer q >= 2, define the **q-th race constant**:

$$R(1/q) = \lim_{N \to \infty} (N+1)^{-(q-2)/q} \prod_{k=0}^{N} \frac{qk + q - 1}{qk + 1}$$

**Theorem (Closed Form).** [THEOREM]

$$R(1/q) = \frac{\Gamma(1/q)}{\Gamma(1 - 1/q)}$$

*Proof.* The product is the Pochhammer ratio $((q-1)/q)_{N+1} / (1/q)_{N+1}$. By Stirling's approximation, $(a)_N \sim N^a \cdot \Gamma(N)/\Gamma(a)$, so the ratio grows as $N^{(q-2)/q} \cdot \Gamma(1/q)/\Gamma((q-1)/q)$. Dividing by $N^{(q-2)/q}$ gives the result. $\square$

## 3. The Reflection Pairing

Each race has a **product** and a **ratio**:

$$\text{Product:} \quad \Gamma(1/q) \cdot \Gamma(1-1/q) = \frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi/q)}$$

$$\text{Ratio:} \quad R(1/q) = \frac{\Gamma(1/q)}{\Gamma(1-1/q)}$$

The product is fixed by the reflection formula — it encodes the angle pi/q.
The ratio is the remaining degree of freedom — it encodes the arithmetic of the number field Q(e^{2pi*i/q}).

At each q, the reflection formula factors into:
- An angular piece (sin(pi/q), geometric)
- An arithmetic piece (R(1/q), number-theoretic)

## 4. The Duplication Law

The Legendre duplication formula connects races at composite levels to products of races at prime-power levels, up to algebraic factors (powers of 2).

**Theorem (Duplication).** [THEOREM]

$$R(1/6) = 2^{1/3} \cdot R(1/3)^2$$

$$R(1/10) = 2^{3/5} \cdot R(1/5) \cdot R(2/5)$$

$$R(1/8) = \sqrt{2} \cdot R(1/4) \cdot R(3/8)$$

More generally, R at composite denominators factors through R at prime-power denominators. The hierarchy is not flat: level 2q factors through level q.

**Consequence:** The "irreducible" races are at prime q. Composite q always decompose. The primes p = 3, 5, 7, 11, ... generate the full family.

## 6. Shared Zeta Values: The Critical Observation

### The Tower at Each q

At each rational point s = 1/q, the Gamma function has a Laurent expansion whose coefficients involve:

- **Even derivatives:** L(n, chi) where chi is a Dirichlet character mod q. These are **particular** — they change with q.
- **Odd derivatives:** (1 - q^{-n}) * zeta(n). These are **universal** — they are the same at every q.

### The Universality of Odd Zeta Values

zeta(3) appears in the tower at s = 1/4 (constrained by chi_{-4}), at s = 1/3 (constrained by chi_{-3}), at s = 1/5 (constrained by chi_{-5}), and at every other rational point simultaneously.

The even L-values (Catalan's constant G = L(2, chi_{-4}), L(2, chi_{-3}), L(2, chi_{-5}), ...) are **particular**: each belongs to one tower and one number field.

The odd zeta values (zeta(3), zeta(5), zeta(7), ...) are **universal**: they belong to all towers and all number fields simultaneously.

### Why Odd Zeta Values Are Hard

Particular constants live in one house. Universal constants live in every house at once. That is why no single house can determine them.

- zeta(2) = pi^2/6 is "easy" because it is determined by the q=2 tower alone.
- zeta(3) is "hard" because it appears in every tower but is determined by none.

Apery proved zeta(3) is irrational (1978), but its algebraic nature remains unknown precisely because it is not the invariant of any single number field — it is the invariant of the entire family.
