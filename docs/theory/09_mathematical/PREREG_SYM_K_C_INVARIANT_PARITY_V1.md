## Hypothesis (pre-registered BEFORE numerical or PSLQ verification)

**H1 (Z[i]-eigenline decomposition):** For the rank-2 Z[i]-module H¹ := H¹_dR(E_lemn) ⊗ Q[i], the symmetric power Sym^k(H¹) ⊗ C decomposes as a direct sum of (k+1) one-dimensional Z[i]-eigenlines, indexed by i^k, i^{k−2}, ..., i^{−k}. Each eigenline is generated by a monomial ω^a · η^b with a+b=k and Z[i]-eigenvalue i^{a−b}.

**H2 (c-action parity swap):** The complex-conjugation involution c on Sym^k(H¹) ⊗ C maps the i^{a−b}-eigenline to the i^{b−a}-eigenline, scaled by an explicit Legendre factor.

**H3 (Sym² c-invariant dimension):** Sym²(H¹)^c (the c-fixed subspace of Sym²) is exactly 1-dimensional over Q[i].

**H4 (joint-root assembly constraint, the load-bearing hypothesis):** Among monic quadratics P_{(a,b)} = x² − 16·Φ(b')·x + 16·Φ(c') with b' ∈ Sym^a(H¹) and c' ∈ Sym^b(H¹), the requirement that (x_+, x_−) be real and that b', c' lie in compatible c-invariant subspaces of their symmetric powers will single out (a,b) = (2,3) as the unique minimal pair (in the sense of Paper A Theorem 17.5) for a ≤ 5, b ≤ 6.

If H4 falsifies, the entire Tier-A bridge framing collapses to "(2,3) is one of several minimal pairs" and the FTD identification weakens to [PARAMETRIC].
