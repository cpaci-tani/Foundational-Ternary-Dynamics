## 1 · Statement

> **No-Go Theorem (a_phys).** No quantity with SI dimension of length is derivable from Axiom Zero alone. Consequently, the lattice-to-physical length conversion `a_phys` must be supplied as an external calibration input.

The theorem extends (Corollary 3.1) to every non-trivial SI dimension: mass, time, energy, temperature, charge. Axiom Zero cannot set any physical scale.

## 2 · Definitions

- **Axiom Zero** = the five postulates plus the derivable position property (`FOUND_AXIOM_ZERO.md`): D = 3 discrete cubic lattice, discrete time, ternary states `{−1, 0, +1}`, 26-Moore locality, determinism.
- **Axiom-Zero invariants** = any quantity definable purely from Axiom Zero without importing an external dimensional constant. These include the integers `{N_base, N_eff, b_3, N_c, D}`, the algebraic constants `{G*, ϖ, π, x_+, x_−}`, and the dimensionless CFL constant `c_lat = 1/√3`.
- **Axiom-Zero ring `R`** = the smallest sub-ring of `ℝ` containing all Axiom-Zero invariants and closed under `+, −, ×, ÷` (on non-zero elements), `exp`, `log`, and `Γ`, `θ`-functions, Watson integrals.
- **SI dimension** = the usual assignment mapping a physical quantity to a point in the free ℤ-module generated by `{M, L, T, Θ, I, …}`.

## 3 · Proof

**Claim A.** Every Axiom-Zero invariant is an element of `R`, and every element of `R` has SI dimension `1` (the zero element of the SI dimension module).

*Argument.* By construction, the Axiom-Zero postulates refer to (i) an abstract discrete lattice (coordinates are integers, not meters), (ii) abstract tick indices (integers, not seconds), (iii) an abstract alphabet `{−1, 0, +1}`, (iv) neighborhood adjacency (a combinatorial predicate), (v) an update rule (a function on finite state space). None of these introduces an SI unit. The CFL speed `c_lat = 1/√3` is the ratio (voxels per tick) of two Axiom-Zero unit-labels that carry no SI meaning on their own — it is dimensionless in SI. Ring operations preserve SI dimension `1`. Transcendental closures (exp, log, Γ, θ, Watson) map `R → R`. Hence every element of `R` has SI dimension `1`.

**Claim B.** A length `a_phys` has SI dimension `L¹ ≠ 1`.

*Argument.* By the definition of `a_phys` in `SPEC_FTD.md`: "one voxel, expressed in a physical length unit." The target unit is meters, i.e., `L¹`.

**Claim C.** No function of Axiom-Zero invariants alone has SI dimension `L¹`.

*Argument.* Suppose for contradiction that `a_phys = f(r_1, …, r_n)` with each `r_i ∈ R`. By Claim A, every `r_i` has SI dimension `1`. SI dimensions multiply under ordinary algebraic operations, so for any rational expression in dimensionless quantities the output also has dimension `1`. For transcendental `f` (exp, log, Γ, θ, Watson), under the standard dimensional-analysis axioms of physics the argument must itself be dimensionless (a transcendental of a quantity carrying non-trivial SI dimensions has no dimensionally-consistent reading), and the output is dimensionless. Hence `dim(a_phys) = 1`, contradicting Claim B. Therefore no such `f` exists.

> **Convention note.** Claim C is a theorem under the dimensional-analysis axioms of physics, not a pure-mathematics theorem. A mathematician may object that `log(a_phys/a_phys) = 0` is defined regardless; the physics convention is that transcendental arguments must be dimensionless ratios, and this convention is what the claim relies on. Under that convention the argument is rigorous.

**Conclusion.** `a_phys` cannot be written as a function of Axiom-Zero invariants alone. Any expression producing `a_phys` in meters must contain at least one dimensional generator outside `R`. That generator is, by definition, a calibration. `∎`

## 4 · Corollaries

### 4.1 · No mass, time, energy, temperature, or charge from Axiom Zero

*Proof.* Replace "L¹" in Claim B with `M¹`, `T¹`, `M L² T⁻²`, `Θ¹`, or `I T`. Claims A and C apply verbatim. `∎`

### 4.2 · Calibration dimension count

Every dimensional FTD prediction is conditional on exactly **one calibration per independent SI dimension** spanned by the prediction. With `c_phys` as the dimensional bridge linking length ↔ time and `c² = E/M` linking energy ↔ mass, fixing two calibrations (one length, one mass) closes the set {L, T, M, E}:

- `a_phys ≡ ℓ_P` fixes length.
- `K_B = m_e` fixes mass.
- `c_lat = 1/√3 ↔ c_phys` promotes the length fix to a time fix.
- `c² = E/M` promotes the mass fix to an energy fix.

This is the calibration basis declared in `docs/SPEC_FTD.md` "LATTICE ↔ PHYSICAL CALIBRATION." Adding a third independent dimensional anchor (e.g., a separately-calibrated `ℏ`) would over-determine the system and must be checked for consistency with the two primary anchors — the retracted `archive/retracted/DERIV_A_PHYS_MECHANISM_GAMMA_SUCCESS.md` failed this consistency check.

Dimensionless observables (α, N_c, mass ratios m_p/m_e, mixing angles sin²θ_W, anomalous magnetic moments a_e, any cross-section-to-cross-section ratio, any frequency ratio) are invariant under the calibration choice: they belong to `R` directly. They are the calibration-independent content of FTD and the only predictions that falsify the framework without a matching convention debate.

## 5 · Epistemic reframe — this is a strength of FTD

The no-go theorem reframes `a_phys` from an awkward open problem into a structural feature:

- **FTD is a purely dimensionless theory.** Its entire substantive content lives in `R`: dimensionless ratios, integer counts, transcendental constants, and algebraic identities among them.
- **FTD has a clean calibration interface.** Exactly two real-number calibrations (`a_phys`, `K_B`) are needed to promote the dimensionless skeleton to a dimensional model compatible with SI units. Every other dimensional prediction derives from these two by algebra involving Axiom-Zero invariants and `c_phys`.
- **Compare the Standard Model.** The SM has ~20 dimensional parameters (quark masses, lepton masses, CKM matrix, Higgs VEV, QCD scale, G_F, α, etc.) fit independently to experiment. FTD declares two calibration numbers and computes the rest. The theorem proves this cannot be further reduced in principle — which means the two declared calibrations are the irreducible minimum, not an accident of current development.
- **The calibration choice is documented, not hidden.** `docs/SPEC_FTD.md` names the two calibrations and their matching observables. Every dimensional prediction is tagged "conditional on `a_phys ≡ ℓ_P` and `K_B = m_e`." Every dimensionless prediction is tagged as calibration-independent.

This is the same epistemic position occupied by every effective field theory at its matching scale, sharpened by the observation that FTD's matching interface is exactly two numbers wide — the narrowest possible for a theory intended to produce SI-unit predictions.

## 6 · What this theorem does **not** claim

- It does not claim that FTD is complete or correct in all dimensionless predictions. Those predictions remain open to direct experimental falsification.
- It does not forbid future extensions of Axiom Zero (e.g., an additional postulate that introduces a dimensional generator). Such extensions would change the ring `R`, and the theorem would have to be re-examined. The theorem is conditional on the current Axiom Zero.
- It does not claim `a_phys = ℓ_P` is the unique natural calibration. Other calibrations (atomic, QCD-scale, cosmological) are logically admissible; the spec chooses `ℓ_P` on Occam-of-smallest-natural-scale grounds.
- It does not resolve `g_c` (`OPEN_GC_FROM_FIRST_PRINCIPLES.md`) — `g_c` is dimensionless; its open status is about a different kind of first-principles derivation.
