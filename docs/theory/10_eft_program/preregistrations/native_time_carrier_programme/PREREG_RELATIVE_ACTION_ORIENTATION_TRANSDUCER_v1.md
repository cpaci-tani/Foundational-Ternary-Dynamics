## 1. Question

Can the target-blind production event energy identified by FTD-0855 be moved
into a nonzero relative canonical carrier without first clearing that carrier
to zero? If so, what information is retained by the minimum action pump, what
fails at the empty carrier, and can one unlabelled canonical pair faithfully
retain an arbitrary prior carrier state together with the erased event sign and
energy?

The registered distinction is load-bearing:

- a **lossy deterministic transducer** need only close the declared energy and
  apply a definite orientation response; and
- a **faithful history transducer** must also permit recovery of the erased
  signed event and the arbitrary prior receiver state.

FTD postulate P5 requires a unique next state. It does not by itself require
the update to be invertible. The certificate must not silently upgrade
determinism to reversibility.

## 3. Frozen canonical receiver

Use one real canonical pair

\[
 z=\binom q p,\qquad
 I(z)=\frac{q^2+p^2}{2},\qquad
 J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.       \tag{1}
\]

Here `J` is the real quarter-turn/complex-structure operator. It is not a
Hilbert-space state and no Hilbert-space recovery claim is permitted.

Let a locally accepted event supply only

\[
 s\in\{-1,+1\},\qquad B>0.                       \tag{2}
\]

For a nonzero carrier `I>0`, freeze the selected signed-quarter-turn action
pump

\[
 \rho_B(I)=\sqrt{\frac{I+B}{I}},\qquad
 F_{s,B}(z)=\rho_B(I)\,sJz.                       \tag{3}
\]

Equivalently, in action-angle coordinates,

\[
 I'=I+B,\qquad \theta'=\theta+s\frac{\pi}{2}.    \tag{4}
\]

The quarter-turn in (3)--(4) is a `[SELECTION]`: it is the minimum nontrivial
orientation operation generated by the already-declared complex structure
`J`, but logic alone does not exclude another fixed phase response.

## 4. Frozen exact claims

### 4.1 Nonzero-carrier action pump

The certificate must prove exactly that:

1. `J^2=-1`, `J^T J=1`, and `det J=1`;
2. equation (3) gives `I(F_{s,B}(z))=I(z)+B`;
3. among positive radial gains with the frozen action increment and phase
   response, `rho_B(I)` is unique;
4. the Jacobian determinant and Poisson bracket of (3) equal one on `I>0`;
5. for known `(s,B)`, the inverse on `I'>B` is

   \[
    z=-sJ\sqrt{\frac{I'-B}{I'}}\,z';             \tag{5}
   \]

6. with `K=diag(1,-1)` as canonical time reversal,
   `K(sJ)K=(-s)J`; and
7. the map is rotation equivariant because every planar rotation commutes
   with `J` and the gain depends only on `I`.

These statements license an exact, target-blind, deterministic energy pump on
a **pre-existing nonzero carrier**. They do not establish a localized
production mode or a controller that maintains `I>0`.

### 4.2 Empty-carrier obstruction

No rotation-equivariant extension of (3) can assign positive event energy at
`z=0`. Since zero is fixed by every planar rotation, equivariance would require
`F(0)` to be fixed by every rotation. The only such vector is zero, whereas
energy closure requires

\[
 \lVert F(0)\rVert^2=2B>0.                       \tag{6}
\]

The same obstruction appears as path dependence: approaching zero along
different phases gives distinct radius-`sqrt(2B)` limits. A deposit into an
empty isotropic pair therefore needs a phase anchor, a selected symmetry
breaking direction, or a separate directional receiver.

### 4.3 Lossy versus faithful output

Equation (3) is not faithful to the erased event on an arbitrary background.
It has the exact collision

\[
 F_{+,B}(z)=F_{-,B}(-z),                         \tag{7}
\]

and output action records only the sum `I+B`. For example, two inputs with
the same phase and

\[
 (I_1,B_1)=(I,B),\qquad
 (I_2,B_2)=(I+B/2,B/2)                           \tag{8}
\]

have the same final action. Within the frozen action-pump class, the prior
carrier action and event energy cannot be separated from the output pair
alone.

More generally, for each fixed `I>0` an exact-energy continuous sign-faithful
map would have to embed two disjoint input circles into the single output
circle at action `I+B`. A continuous injection of one circle into a circle is
onto, so the two images must intersect. One unlabelled pair cannot retain both
an arbitrary phase and an additional erased binary branch on the same exact
energy shell.

If loss of `(s,B)` is declared, equation (3) remains a valid deterministic
reduced transducer. If faithful history is required, an additional signed
receiver coordinate is necessary in the registered continuous class. The
single real amplitude

\[
 a=s\sqrt{2B}                                    \tag{9}
\]

is sufficient to carry both sign and energy, because
`s=sign(a)` and `B=a^2/2`. But assigning its quadratic energy already deposits
`B`; adding both (3) and an energetic (9) would double count. The faithful
branch is therefore the FTD-0852/0855 reserved signed rail, while (3) is a
distinct lossy energy-mixing branch.
