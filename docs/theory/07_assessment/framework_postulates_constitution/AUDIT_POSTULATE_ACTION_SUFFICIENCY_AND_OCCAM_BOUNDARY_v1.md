## 1. Audit standard: Occam without assumption laundering

This audit applies five rules.

### O1. Adequacy before simplicity

A simpler model that does not produce propagation, matter, records, clocks, or
observed correlations is not preferred to a more complex model that does.
Occam ranks surviving explanations; it does not replace physical evidence.

### O2. Count independent clauses, not headings

One sentence may contain several logically independent choices. Calling the
contents of P3 one postulate does not make `R^3`, vector character, J-primary
ontology, the ternary codomain, and the manifestation projection one
assumption.

### O3. Do not count a price twice

If `D=3` is part of P1, its entry as a priced selected type is metadata about
that choice, not a second physical input. A unified register must not present
the same content as independent membership at two layers without an explicit
alias.

### O4. Derived representations are cheaper than primitive duplicates

A continuum field, action, Hilbert scaffold, probability law, or metric is
ontologically cheap only if a map from the finite ontology to that object is
defined and its required properties are proved. Calling it "emergent" without
that map does not lower its price.

### O5. A selection principle must close countermodels

"Symmetric," "minimal," "local," and "reversible" count as selectors only
when competing models satisfying those words have been excluded. The exact
countermodels in this audit prevent those adjectives from selecting the live
FTD rule by themselves.

## 3. Exact underdetermination theorem

Let a valid configuration obey the P3 readout relation

\[
 s_x=R(J_x),\qquad J_x\in\mathbb R^3,
 \quad s_x\in\{-1,0,+1\}.
\]

Consider these one-tick maps:

\[
 I: J'_x=J_x,
\]

\[
 N: J'_x=-J_x,
\]

\[
 S: J'_x=J_{x-\hat e_1},
\]

with `s'=R(J')` in each case. All three are deterministic. Their dependencies
are respectively onsite, onsite, and one axis neighbor, hence each lies inside
the Moore causal bound. All three are bijective, with inverses `I`, `N`, and
the opposite shift. They are plainly distinct.

A fourth map,

\[
 E:J'_x=0,\qquad s'_x=0,
\]

is deterministic and local but many-to-one.

Therefore:

> **Theorem 1.** P1--P5 admit multiple inequivalent update maps.

> **Corollary 1.** Adding global reversibility still admits multiple
> inequivalent maps.

> **Corollary 2.** P1--P5 do not determine an arrow, propagation law,
> interaction, clock, conserved energy, action, or probability ensemble.

These are logical countermodels, not proposed physics. Their role is to prove
that the postulates are insufficient to select the live model.

### 3.1 Why “there exists an action” is not the missing theorem

For any already selected deterministic map `F`, define the history constraint

\[
 S_F[X]=\sum_n \mathbf 1[X_{n+1}\ne F(X_n)].
\]

Its zero-action histories are exactly the trajectories of `F`. This construction
works for identity, shift, collapse, and any other rule. It merely rewrites the
rule and generates no Noether content, pole, coupling, or continuum dynamics.

Thus the open problem is not to attach the word “action” to FTD. It is to find
a **compressed, non-target-coded local rule** whose derived history functional
has the needed physical sectors.

### 3.2 What counting invariance does and does not do

On a finite alphabet, every bijection preserves uniform counting measure. The
identity and C4 rotation on the nine-state ternary square are distinct examples.
Moreover, identity preserves every probability distribution, including both
the uniform distribution and a delta distribution. Invariance therefore does
not select the physical ensemble.

For the canonical real flux field there is an additional obstruction: no
normalized translation-invariant uniform probability measure exists on
`R^(3N)`. If one unit interval has positive mass, sufficiently many disjoint
translates exceed total mass one; if it has zero mass, countable additivity
gives total real-line mass zero.

The recent uniform-C18 counting/Born seam is consequently a result about the
**finite strict-discrete candidate**, not a native measure theorem for current
J-primary P3. A physical Born theorem still requires the common dynamics to
form the prepared records and push its physically generated ensemble through
the detector event map.

## 10. Final assessment

The audit strengthens the ontology by making its real choice visible.

FTD currently has two conceptually different foundations:

1. a **canonical hybrid foundation** with primitive continuous `J`, ternary
   manifestation, later dynamics posits, and v1/v2 observer branches; and

permit declaring it successful before that law exists.

bright/recovery permutation carries event delta `(8,1,0,-9)`, exact charged

count. Under Occam this is preferable to adding separate sector axioms. It
still counts as a conditional transaction witness because its material frame,
bank, work reserve, detector, and gravity kit are prepared resources and its
long-range poles are not generated by the one homogeneous law.

The precise foundational gap is therefore:

> **FTD now has one exact selected finite reference law and many local
> transaction witnesses, but it does not yet have a physically sufficient
> extension of that law from which action normalization, physical measure,
> stable matter, coupling, clocks, and gravity are all controlled block
> consequences.**

That is the correct target for the next phase of work.
