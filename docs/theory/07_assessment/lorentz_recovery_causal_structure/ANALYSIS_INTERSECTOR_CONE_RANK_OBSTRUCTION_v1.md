## 1. Why this is the load-bearing problem

One sector can always be given unit speed by choosing the time unit. That
removes one normalisation and no more: if two propagating sectors have
slopes $c_A$ and $c_B$, the ratio $c_A/c_B$ survives every common unit
change and **is physical preferred-frame data**. The engine currently
carries $c^2 = 1/3$ (production flux), $1/7$ (BCC-time prototype) and raw
$1$ (standalone Wilson matter) — an *order-unity* mismatch, against which
the intra-sector, mass-dependent effect computed elsewhere
(`ANALYSIS_MASSIVE_CONE_AND_DILATION_v1.md`) is $10^{-40}$ and irrelevant.

## 2. Exact all-orders no-go against the production flux

FTD-0412 established that a scalar Wilson $r$ cannot match the
*quartic-free BCC-time* pole at $q^4$. The production sector is the M18
flux, and there the statement can be made **exact and at all orders**.

Writing $u_i = \cos q_i$, the two spatial symbols are polynomials:

$$-L_{18} = 4 - \tfrac23\textstyle\sum_i u_i - \tfrac23\sum_{i<j}u_iu_j,
\qquad
E_W^2 = c_s^2\Big[3 - \sum_i u_i^2 + r^2\big(3-\sum_i u_i\big)^2\Big].$$

Demanding $E_W^2 = \kappa\,(-L_{18})$ *identically* gives ten coefficient
equations. Two of them already close the case:

| monomial | equation | forces |
|---|---|---|
| $u_1u_2$ | $2c_s^2r^2 + \tfrac23\kappa = 0$ | $\kappa = -3c_s^2r^2$ |
| $u_1$ | $-6c_s^2r^2 + \tfrac23\kappa = 0$ | $\kappa = +9c_s^2r^2$ |

Together they force $c_s^2r^2 = 0$. **No real $(c_s, r)$ matches the
production flux symbol at any order**, and the full solve returns only
$c_s^2 = \kappa = 0$. This is stronger than FTD-0412's statement in two
respects: it is exact rather than order-$q^4$, and it is against the
sector the engine actually runs.

## 3. The conditions grow without bound

The $O_h$-invariant polynomials in $q$ are generated by
$p_1 = \sum q_i^2$, $p_2 = \sum_{i<j}q_i^2q_j^2$, $p_3 = q_1^2q_2^2q_3^2$,
so the number of invariants at order $q^{2n}$ is the number of ways to
write $n$ from parts $\{1,2,3\}$. Isotropy requires every invariant except
$p_1^n$ to vanish:

| order | $q^2$ | $q^4$ | $q^6$ | $q^8$ | $q^{10}$ | $q^{12}$ | $q^{14}$ | $q^{16}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| invariants | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 7 | 8 | 10 |
| new conditions | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 7 | 9 |
| cumulative | 0 | 1 | 3 | 6 | 10 | 16 | 23 | 32 |

There is no order at which the count closes. Matching is therefore an
order-by-order improvement programme — the Symanzik situation — and the
coefficient bill is unbounded, not a one-off purchase.

## 4. The q⁶ obstruction is a **rank** violation

This is the substantive finding, and it is not what the counting alone
predicts.

FTD-0413 buys $q^4$ by adding a face-diagonal kinetic weight: one extra
knob for the one $q^4$ condition. Enrich *both* structures with that shell,

$$K_i = a_1\sin q_i + a_2\sin q_i(\cos q_j + \cos q_k),\qquad
M = b_1\!\sum_i(1-\cos q_i) + b_2\!\sum_{i<j}(1-\cos q_i\cos q_j),$$

with $E^2 = \sum_i K_i^2 + M^2$, and match to $\kappa(-L_{18})$ through
$q^6$. That is **6 distinct equations** against **5 parameters**
$(a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2,\kappa)$ — apparently one short.

But $E^2$ is *quadratic* in $(a,b)$ with no $a$–$b$ cross terms, so the
system is **linear** in the six monomials
$A_{11}=a_1^2,\,A_{12}=a_1a_2,\,A_{22}=a_2^2,\,B_{11},\,B_{12},\,B_{22}$.
In those variables it is 6 equations in 7 unknowns and **it solves**. The
counting is fine.

What fails is realisability. For real $(a_1,a_2)$ the matrix
$A_{st} = a_sa_t$ is an outer product and must be **rank one**:
$A_{11}A_{22} - A_{12}^2 = 0$. The solution instead forces

$$\boxed{\,A_{11}A_{22} - A_{12}^{2} \;=\; \tfrac{2}{9}\kappa^{2}\,}$$

which vanishes only for $\kappa = 0$. The required kinetic Gram matrix is
**rank two**, and no real single-vector $K$ realises it. The residual is
proportional to $\kappa^2$, so no rescaling escapes it.

**Two escapes were tested, and both fail.**

| enrichment | result at $q^6$ |
|---|---|
| add a $\gamma^5$ / twisted-mass scalar (the fifth Clifford structure available to 4-component spinors) | residual **unchanged** at $2\kappa^2/9$ — it adds a squared *scalar* while the obstruction is in the *kinetic* Gram |
| add a third kinetic shape $\sin 2q_i$ | the rank-1 conditions on the $3\times3$ block become a nontrivial system with **no solutions** |

> ⚠ **Correction to an earlier draft of this section.** It argued that
> shells can *never* lift a rank obstruction, because
> $K_i = \sum_s a_s f_{s,i}$ gives coefficient matrix $a\otimes a$, rank
> one by construction. That reasoning is incomplete: with more shapes the
> *required* matrix acquires free entries, so rank one can in principle
> become satisfiable. It does not at three shapes — the minors solve to the
> empty set — but that is a computed fact at $n_K = 2, 3$, **not a theorem
> for all $n_K$**. The general claim is withdrawn.

What is established is therefore: the obstruction is structural rather than
arithmetic (the linear system solves; realisability fails), it is immune to
the one extra Clifford structure that 4-component spinors permit, and it
survives a third kinetic shell.

## 4a. The decisive general question, and its shape

The right way to settle all shell counts at once is to recognise what
$E^2 = \sum_i K_i^2 + M^2$ is: a **sum of squares** of trigonometric
polynomials. The matching question is then exactly

> is $\kappa\,(-L_{18})$ in the cone of sums of squares generated by the
> available shapes?

That is a semidefinite feasibility problem. Two regimes:

- **Rank-constrained** (a single kinetic vector, i.e. one Clifford
  structure): the Gram must be rank one. This is what fails above.
- **Termwise Clifford** (several independent anticommuting structures,
  whose squares add without cross terms): a plain SDP supplies a direct
  construction. Its square count prices that ansatz; it is not a universal
  matrix-factorization lower bound because factor identities could allow
  collective cross-term cancellation.

So the open question has a sharp form and a physically meaningful price:
*how many independent Clifford structures does a common cone cost, and is
that number finite?* If $-L_{18}$ is not a sum of squares in the available
shapes at any rank, no Dirac-type operator of that range can match it and
the no-go is complete. If it is, the price is a spinor doubling — visible,
heavy, and checkable against the doubling problem.

That is the next computation, and it decides the matter for all shell
counts and all spinor dimensions simultaneously.

## 4b. Resolved, constructively: exact decompositions on two placement classes

Regroup the symbol as
$-L_{18} = \tfrac23\sum_i(1-\cos q_i) + \tfrac23\sum_{i<j}(1-\cos q_i\cos q_j)$
and apply two elementary identities,
$1-\cos A = 2\sin^2\!\tfrac A2$ and
$1-\cos A\cos B = \sin^2\!\tfrac{A-B}{2} + \sin^2\!\tfrac{A+B}{2}$:

$$\boxed{\;-L_{18} \;=\; \tfrac43\sum_i \sin^2\!\frac{q_i}{2}
\;+\; \tfrac23\sum_{i<j}\Big[\sin^2\!\frac{q_i-q_j}{2}
+ \sin^2\!\frac{q_i+q_j}{2}\Big]\;}$$

Verified symbolically and numerically ($\max$ residual $2.2\times10^{-15}$
over 2000 random momenta). **Three face squares plus six edge squares:
nine.** This is an economical half-angle construction, but it is not an
endpoint-location or necessity theorem.

**(i) This termwise-Clifford construction uses spinor dimension 16.** For
$H = \sum_\mu\Gamma^\mu\phi_\mu$, imposing cancellation separately for
each cross term makes the $\Gamma^\mu$ mutually anticommute. Dimension
$2^k$ carries $2k+1$:

| spinor dim | 2 | 4 (Dirac) | 8 | **16** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| structures | 3 | 5 | 7 | **9** |

Nine squares therefore use **16-component spinors** in that ansatz. Section
4c lowers this half-angle count to seven.

**(ii) Integer displacements also give an exact construction.** For every
integer hop $d$ with graph weight $w$,

$$2w[1-\cos(q\!\cdot\!d)]
=w\sin^2(q\!\cdot\!d)+w[1-\cos(q\!\cdot\!d)]^2.$$

Taking the three face-pair hops at $w=1/3$ and six edge-pair hops at
$w=1/6$ yields an exact **eighteen-square integer-frequency** decomposition
of $-L_{18}$. The identity
$e^{iq}-1=2ie^{iq/2}\sin(q/2)$ makes the conceptual point: a half-angle
magnitude does not by itself locate endpoints at half sites. Naive
termwise Cliffordization of the integer construction uses dimension $512$
($2k+1\ge18$), so the half-angle basis is much more economical; it is not
forced by existence. The scalar-Wilson no-go of §2 and the limited Gram
obstructions of §4 remain valid only for their stated ansätze.

### What this settles, and what it does not

**Settles.** The common spatial symbol is not obstructed in principle.
There are explicit exact integer-hop and half-angle decompositions, with
different known termwise-Clifford construction prices.

**Does not settle.** Whether the resulting operator is an acceptable
fermion: doubling, chirality, and the locality of the induced interactions
are all untested here. Nor is the time discretisation matched (§5). The
claim is existence-with-a-price, not a working sector. A physical
half-offset ontology is optional and separately open.

## 4c. Correction: nine is **not** minimal, and the price is not 16

Same day, on the question §4b left open. Write $x = q/2$ and let $V$ be the
nearest-neighbour class on the half-offset lattice — trigonometric
polynomials of degree $\le 1$ per variable in $x$, i.e. the span of the 27
monomials $\prod_i g_i$ with $g_i \in \{1, \cos x_i, \sin x_i\}$. Three
results, in increasing strength.

**(i) The ambient space is 19-dimensional, not 27.** $p$ vanishes exactly
on $\{0,\pi\}^3$, squares are non-negative, so every $f_a$ vanishes there;
the 8 $s$-free monomials evaluate on those points to an invertible $8\times
8$ Hadamard matrix, so their coefficients are forced to zero. Only the 19
monomials carrying at least one $\sin$ survive.

**(ii) An exact seven-square decomposition exists, and it is cubic-covariant.**

$$\boxed{\;-L_{18} \;=\; 4\sum_i \sin^2\!x_i\cos^2\!x_j\cos^2\!x_k
\;+\; \tfrac{16}{3}\sum_i \sin^2\!x_j\sin^2\!x_k\cos^2\!x_i
\;+\; 4\,\sin^2\!x_1\sin^2\!x_2\sin^2\!x_3\;}$$

Solved symbolically from a six-parameter orbit ansatz (sympy returns eight
solutions, all sign variants of this one) and checkable by hand: with
$A = \sin^2 x_1$ etc. and $S_1 = A+B+C$, $S_2 = AB+BC+CA$, the three groups
contribute $4[S_1 - 2S_2 + 3ABC]$, $\tfrac{16}{3}[S_2 - 3ABC]$ and $4ABC$.
**The triple products cancel exactly** — $12 - 16 + 4 = 0$ — leaving
$4S_1 - \tfrac83 S_2 = \tfrac43[3S_1 - 2S_2] = -L_{18}$. The singlet is not
decoration; it is what kills $ABC$.

In the termwise Clifford ansatz, seven squares use seven anticommuting
structures, so **this cubic-covariant construction uses spinor dimension 8,
not 16.**

**(iii) Unrestricted, the minimum is five — ordinary Dirac.** A
Burer–Monteiro search over $F \in \mathbb{R}^{19\times n}$ (residuals on a
$5^3$ grid, which resolves every frequency in $[-2,2]$ exactly, so grid
agreement *is* functional agreement) finds decompositions at
$n = 9,8,7,6,5$ and none at $n = 4$ or $3$ from 400 restarts each. The
$n=5$ solution re-checks to $5.3\times10^{-15}$ on 4000 fresh random
momenta. **Five squares fit spinor dimension 4** — plain Dirac, with no
multiplication at all.

The five is genuinely non-covariant: $\max|PGP^{\mathsf T} - G| = 4.0$ over
the 48 group elements, not a rounding artefact.

| squares | anticommuting structures | spinor dim | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 (§4b) | 9 | 16 | exact, covariant — **loose** |
| **7** | 7 | **8** | **exact, covariant** |
| **5** | 5 | **4** | numerical residual; **not an exact certificate**, not covariant |
| 4 | — | — | none found, 400 restarts |

> ⚠ **A retracted sub-argument.** A first draft of this section asserted
> that no covariant decomposition of length 5 can exist, on the grounds
> that covariant lengths are sums of orbit sizes $\{1, 3\}$. That is right
> only for $f_a$ lying in a *single* sign-character sector, where the sign
> group acts by $\pm1$ and $f_a^2$ is sign-invariant. An $f_a$ mixing
> sectors — $s_1 + s_2s_3$, say — has a larger orbit, of any size dividing
> $|\mathbb{Z}_2^3\rtimes S_3| = 48$. Five is not a divisor of 48, so no
> *single* orbit has size five; but $5 = 2+3 = 1+4 = 1+2+2$ are all
> arithmetically available. **The covariant minimum is therefore bounded
> above by 7 and below by nothing better than 3** — the pure-sector search
> reported here does not settle it.

**What §4b's claim becomes.** "The price is spinor dimension 16 —
recognisably staggered taste multiplication" was an upper bound presented
as the price. The honest termwise-Clifford construction price is **at most 8** if
cubic covariance is required; the numerical four candidate suggests
dimension 4 if it can be certified exactly. The taste-multiplication
reading does not survive — a fourfold multiplication was an
artefact of a non-minimal decomposition, not a feature of the symbol.

## 4d. Second correction: a numerical four candidate — not an exact minimum

An adversarial pass produced a rank-4 numerical candidate, but did not
publish an exact or interval existence certificate.
§4c's search drew all 400 restarts from one fixed initial scale, which
samples a single basin 400 times rather than 400 basins once. With the
scale dispersed, a **four-square** least-squares candidate is found with
residual `5.3e-15` on 4000 fresh momenta (Gram spectrum
`{16/3, 16/3, 16/3, 4}`,
not covariant). Its basin is rare — hit after 13, 52 and 146 starts on
three runs and missed once at 200 — which is itself the lesson: restart
counts bound nothing unless the starts are independent.

The bounds are now honest: `n ≥ 3` by the Hessian rank at a zero, while
the exact seven gives `n ≤ 7`. The statements that `n = 3` was not found
and that four fits numerically are search evidence only.
Restricted to the body-centre sector the search reaches **five and stalls**
(2000 starts), so `FTD-0819`'s "same minimum" reading is corrected there.

**The mass-ladder consequence is conditional.** Saturation alternates —
3, 5, 7 squares saturate their spinor dimension; 4, 6, 8 leave one
structure spare. Thus an exactly certified four-square carrier would admit
a mass at ordinary Dirac dimension 4. §4c's "necessarily massless"
prediction is **withdrawn**, but the replacement is not theorem-grade.
Covariance, not mass, is what multiplies the spinor: the covariant seven
saturates dimension 8, so covariance plus a mass forces 16.
