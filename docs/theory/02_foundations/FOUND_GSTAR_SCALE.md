## Abstract

## 2. The Master Quadratic as Graded Period Relation

The master quadratic

$$x^2 - 16G^{*2} x + 16G^{*3} = 0$$

has coefficients drawn from two different weight-graded pieces:

- **Trace** = 16G\*² ∈ Sym² (weight 2)
- **Determinant** = 16G\*³ ∈ Sym³ (weight 3)

### 2.1 Why This Is Not a Characteristic Polynomial [THEOREM]

A characteristic polynomial of a 2×2 matrix M has trace linear in the entries and determinant quadratic. If M depends on G\* with entries of degree d, then Tr(M) ∝ G\*ᵈ and Det(M) ∝ G\*²ᵈ. The ratio of exponents is always 1:2.

For the master quadratic, the ratio of exponents is 2:3, not 1:2. No endomorphism of a 2-dimensional space can produce this. (Proved by Grothendieck, Round 4.)

### 2.2 Why This IS a Graded Period Relation [CONJECTURE]

In the category of mixed motives, the symmetric algebra Sym\*(h¹(E_i)) is a graded ring whose degree-n piece has periods proportional to G\*ⁿ. The master quadratic is a relation of degree 2 in the polynomial ring over this graded ring:

$$x^2 - (16 \cdot \text{per}(\text{Sym}^2)) \cdot x + (16 \cdot \text{per}(\text{Sym}^3)) = 0$$

The coefficient 16 = |Aut(E_i)|² appears as the arithmetic invariant that normalizes the periods across the weight filtration.

The asymmetry between Sym² and Sym³ is not a defect — it is the signature of the weight filtration. Characteristic polynomials are spectral (same object, one matrix). This quadratic is hierarchical (adjacent weights, the scale itself).

## 3. The Power Sum Convergence [THEOREM]

The Newton power sums pₖ = x₊ᵏ + x₋ᵏ satisfy the recurrence

$$p_k = (x_+ + x_-) \cdot p_{k-1} - x_+ x_- \cdot p_{k-2}$$

with p₁ = 16G\*², p₀ = 2. Since x₊ >> x₋ (137 >> 3), the ratio pₖ/pₖ₋₁ converges geometrically to x₊:

| k | pₖ/pₖ₋₁ | Deviation from x₊ |

| 2 | 134.14 | 2.1% |
| 3 | 136.97 | 0.048% |

**The fine structure constant α⁻¹ is the fixed point of the G\* hierarchy.** It is the number to which the symmetric power ratio converges. Climbing the levels of Sym\*(h¹(E_i)) and taking successive power-sum ratios asymptotically produces 1/α.

This is a theorem of algebra (it holds for any quadratic with well-separated roots). But the physical content is that α⁻¹ is not "put in" — it is the asymptotic attractor of the hierarchy generated by G\*.

## 4. The Discriminant Between Levels

The discriminant of the master quadratic is

$$\Delta = (16G^{*2})^2 - 4 \cdot 16G^{*3} = 64G^{*3}(4G^* - 1)$$

This involves:
- G\*³ from Sym³ (the action level)
- The factor (4G\* − 1) = (4 × 2.9587 − 1) = 10.835

The threshold 4G\* − 1 > 0 is the condition for two real roots — two physical phases. It is satisfied because G\* > 1/4. The discriminant lives "between" Sym³ and Sym¹, combining the action level with the first-comparison level.

The EFT mass m² = x₊ − x₋ = 8G\*^{3/2}√(4G\* − 1) = 134.012 involves a half-integer power G\*^{3/2}, which in the motivic language corresponds to the CM character splitting h¹(E_i) into rank-1 pieces of "CM-weight ½." The mass lives between levels, at the staircase.
