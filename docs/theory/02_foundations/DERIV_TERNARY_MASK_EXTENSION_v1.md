## 1. The adoption

The registered pairwise candidate law is extended from binary to the
substrate's own state set:

> **σ_a ∈ {−1, 0, +1}**, with the mask formula unchanged:
> `A_ab = (1 − σ_a σ_b)/2 ∈ {0, ½, 1}`, and the pair energy
> `U = Σ_{a<b} A_ab V(q_ab)` with the registered `V(q)` unchanged.

Forbidden pairs are now exactly the equal-**nonzero** pairs (`A = 0` for
`++` and `−−`). A void constituent bonds to everything — at half stiffness
to charged partners and to other voids (`A = ½`), leaving the bond geometry
untouched (`V`'s minimum stays at `q = 1` under any positive prefactor).

This is an **adoption, not a derivation**: the binary domain was itself a
selected restriction (the parent doc's §2 declares its own pairwise
additivity "selected, not forced"); extending the domain to the ternary
state set is a second selection of the same rank — arguably the more native
one, since `{−1, 0, +1}` is the substrate's P3 state set and the formula
requires no modification to accept it. Consumption-programme position:
a Front-B (P6C-class) candidate purchase, registered by owner decision;
the FTD-0387 pricing rules are DRAFT and are not bypassed by this record —
the adoption is priced informally as **one domain extension, zero new

## 2. Structural consequences (exact; verifier cells in the v3 campaign)

1. **The mod-2 obstruction algebra dissolves.** Bipartiteness,
   triangle-freeness, strut parity transport, odd-cycle kills, and the
   `n`-even ring condition — the entire weapons rack of FTD-1004/1005/1006 —
   are properties of the binary restriction. Under the ternary mask every
   finite graph admits a legal coloring (all-void suffices), so the polarity
   gate ceases to constrain topology. The FTD-1006 closure of the strutted
   axial class stands as a theorem **about the binary sector**.
2. **Clearances shift.** Same-nonzero pairs keep the capacity floor
   (`q ≥ 4/25`, no interaction). All other non-bonded pairs — opposite,
   void–charged, void–void — now interact and must clear the support
   (`q ≥ 3/2`). Void sites lose the same-polarity proximity privilege.
3. **The rigidity machinery is unchanged.** Stresses live in `coker(R)`
   (geometry only); the blocking form acquires weights `A_e ∈ {½, 1}`,
   which cannot change positive-definiteness. The FTD-0789 criterion, the
   buckling rule (compressed members single bonds), and the per-coordinate
   stress identities carry over verbatim.
4. **The distilled native-C3 question of the ternary sector** is coloring-
   free classical rigidity: *does a unit-bar framework in ℝ³ with minimum
   non-bonded distance ≥ √(3/2) exist that is prestress-stable but not
   first-order rigid?* (First negative certificates: both full antiprisms

   coker 0 by exact rank 24 over ℚ(√2,√3) — so the object the triangle

## 3. Liabilities (named at adoption, unpriced)

- **Masslessness:** manifestation carries the inertial calibration
  (`M_INERTIAL = K_B`); a void joint carries none. Structural roles are
  unaffected (massless joints equilibrate as constraints); any clock
  dynamics must put its oscillating inertia on charged joints.
- **Genesis fragility:** a void site bonded into a flux-rich cluster sits
  under the manifestation threshold machinery (`K_GENESIS`); one crossing
  manifests it and changes the structure it supports. Persistence of
  void-jointed structures is `[OPEN]` and engine-testable.
- **Not implemented:** like its parent, this extension is registered
  algebra, not engine dynamics. No engine, toggle, or production change
  accompanies this record.

## 4. Cute and possibly meaningful

The octahedron graph `K_{2,2,2}` is exactly 3-chromatic, and its three
color classes are its three antipodal pairs: the ternary state set colors
the octahedron perfectly — every one of its 12 unit bonds legal — where the
binary set could not color it at all. The first fully-braced convex solid
the matter law can hold together becomes legal precisely when the law is
allowed to use all three of the substrate's states. (It is rigid, hence a
harmonic body, not a clock — but it is the sector's first legal solid.)
