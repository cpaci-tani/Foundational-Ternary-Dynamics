## Part II: The Emergence of {0, 1}

### 2.1 The First Distinction (Level -1)

The pregnant void admits exactly one primordial operation: **drawing a distinction**.

Following Spencer-Brown's *Laws of Form*, we recognize that:

> A distinction creates two sides: an **inside** and an **outside**.

The simplest distinction is binary: **{0, 1}** or equivalently **{nothing, something}** or **{unmarked, marked}**.

```
PREGNANT VOID                   FIRST DISTINCTION
     null*          -->              {0, 1}
  (capacity)                    (binary realized)
```

### 2.2 Why {0, 1} and Not {-1, +1}?

The ternary states {-1, 0, +1} come later. The **first** distinction must be:

| Requirement | Reason | Form |
|-------------|--------|------|
| Binary | Simplest non-trivial | Two elements |
| Ordered | Creates boundary | 0 < 1 |
| Positive definite | No negation yet | {0, 1} not {-1, +1} |

Negation requires a reference point. Before the first distinction, there is no reference. Thus the first distinction must be **positive definite**: the difference between nothing (0) and something (1).

The ternary {-1, 0, +1} emerges later when the distinction can reflect upon itself, creating positive and negative poles around a neutral center.

### 2.3 The Integration Bounds

The integers {0, 1} are discrete. But the void is continuous - it has no preferred granularity.

The **continuous realization** of the first distinction is the **interval [0, 1]**:

- **Endpoint 0**: The unmarked side
- **Endpoint 1**: The marked side
- **Interior (0, 1)**: All degrees of marking

This interval becomes the **domain of integration** in the Pure Integral.

### 2.4 Why Integration?

Of all operations, why does integration precede?

**Integration is measurement.** To integrate over [0, 1] is to:
1. Take the distinction (the interval)
2. Apply structure (the integrand)
3. Collapse to a value (the result)

This is the primordial act of **quantification** - turning quality (distinction) into quantity (number).

**Alternative operations considered:**

| Operation | Problem |
|-----------|---------|
| Addition | Requires pre-existing numbers |
| Differentiation | Requires pre-existing smooth function |
| Algebra | Requires symbols and rules |
| Integration | Only requires domain and integrand |

Integration is the most primitive quantitative operation. It bridges the discrete bounds {0, 1} and the continuous interior.

## Part III: Self-Reference Selects n = 4

### 3.1 The sLoop Requirement

The distinction {0, 1} is static. For anything to **happen**, the distinction must become dynamic. This requires **self-reference**: the distinction observing itself.

In FTD, this is called the **sLoop** (self-Loop):

```
Standard observation:    Observer --> System --> Measurement
sLoop:                   System   (Observer is PART OF System)
```

For the first distinction to observe itself, it must:
1. Be the observer
2. Be the observed
3. **Meet itself** at the origin

### 3.2 Topological Requirement: Self-Crossing

Self-reference requires a **topological signature**: the curve must **cross itself**.

A curve that crosses itself at a point P represents:
- Coming from one direction (observer approaching)
- Returning from another (observer departing)
- Meeting at P (self-observation)

The origin (0, 0) is the natural crossing point - it represents the void from which the distinction emerged.

### 3.3 The Integral Family I_n

Consider the family of integrals:

$$I_n = \int_0^1 \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^n}}$$

Each I_n corresponds to a curve. The question is: **Which n produces self-crossing?**

| n | Value | Curve | Topology |
|---|-------|-------|----------|
| 1 | 1.0 | Linear | No curve (degenerate) |
| 2 | pi/2 = 1.571 | Circle | No self-crossing |
| 3 | 1.402 | Tricuspoid | Cusps, no clean crossing |
| **4** | **1.311** | **Lemniscate** | **Self-crossing at origin** |
| 5 | 1.264 | Higher curve | Multiple lobes, not minimal |
| 6 | 1.233 | Sextic | Complex, not minimal |

### 3.4 Why n = 4 is Unique

The lemniscate (n = 4) is the **unique minimal self-crossing algebraic curve**:

**Criterion 1: Self-crossing**
- n = 2 (circle): Closed but not self-crossing
- n = 4 (lemniscate): Crosses itself at origin
- n = 4 is the FIRST n with self-crossing

**Criterion 2: Algebraic simplicity**
- The curve y^2 = x^2(1 - x^2) is degree 4 in (x, y)
- Degree-3 curves CAN self-cross (e.g., the nodal cubic y² = x²(x+1) has a node at the origin), but such crossings lack the symmetric figure-eight topology of the lemniscate

**Criterion 3: Minimality**
- n = 5, 6, ... also have self-crossing variants
- But they are more complex (higher degree)
- n = 4 is **minimal**

**Criterion 4: Complex Multiplication**
- The lemniscate corresponds to the elliptic curve y^2 = x^3 - x
- This curve has j-invariant j = 1728 = 12^3 = (N_base x N_c)^3
- It admits Complex Multiplication by Gaussian integers Z[i]
- This is a unique number-theoretic property

### 3.5 The Selection is Necessary, Not Contingent

The exponent n = 4 is not chosen arbitrarily. It is **the unique value** satisfying:

1. Self-crossing (self-reference requirement)
2. Algebraic minimal (simplicity)
3. CM property (number-theoretic uniqueness)
4. 4-fold symmetry (compatible with 3D cubic lattice)

These four requirements **uniquely determine** n = 4.

**Epistemic Status:** [SELECTION] - The selection of n = 4 is argued from necessity, not proven from axioms. The four requirements are each motivated but their conjunction could be questioned.

### 4.1 Crystallization

| Exponent 4 | Self-Reference | 0 |

| Integrand 1/sqrt(1-x^4) | Void structure | Encoding of all levels |

### 4.2 The Integrand as Void Structure

The form 1/sqrt(1-x^n) is not arbitrary:

- **1 in numerator**: Unity, the marked side of the distinction
- **x^n in denominator**: The power structure from self-reference
- **1 - x^n**: The complement, encoding the unmarked side
- **Square root**: The symmetric unfolding (halving of degree)
- **Divergence at x = 1**: The boundary is special (the distinction itself)

The integrand encodes the **entire structure of the void's self-observation**.

### 4.3 Why I_4 Converges

Despite the divergence at x = 1, the integral converges:

$$\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^4}} \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x}} \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x}}$$

This is integrable because the exponent in the denominator (1/2) is less than 1.

**Meaning:** The void's self-observation produces a **finite** result. The process does not diverge into infinity but crystallizes into a definite value.
