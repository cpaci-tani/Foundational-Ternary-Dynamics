\section*{The move this layer makes}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{The move this layer makes}

Every framework has to answer one question before it can say anything else:
\emph{what is there?} The ontology layer is FTD's answer, and the answer is
deliberately spare. There is a three-dimensional cubic lattice with no completed
boundary. On that lattice live two fields, and only two: a continuous vector
\emph{flux} that says what each site is \emph{disposed} to do, and a discrete
ternary \emph{state} that records what each site has \emph{actually} done. Time is
a separate primitive --- a global tick, not a fourth axis of space. Nothing else
is fundamental: charge, mass, particles, and even the metric of spacetime are
things the framework hopes to \emph{read out} of this substrate later, not things
it puts in by hand.

The intuition to carry forward is the split between \emph{disposition} and
\emph{actuality}. The flux $J$ is a field of tendencies; it is smooth, it carries
waves, and in isolation it runs forward and backward equally well. The state $s$
is the ledger of commitments the lattice has made; it takes only three values, and
once a commitment is made the bookkeeping that made it cannot in general be undone.
These two fields do not blend. They touch through exactly two doors --- one that
lets a built-up disposition \emph{become} a state (genesis), and one that lets a
committed state \emph{source} the disposition of its neighbours (Gauss). Holding
those two doors in mind is most of what it takes to follow the rest of the chain:
the mathematics of Chapter~3 is the arithmetic of the lattice's symmetry, and the
physics of Chapter~5 is the IR readout of these same two fields run forward in
time.

A reader should also note what this chapter does \emph{not} claim. It does not
claim that the two-field split is forced; that is a modelling choice, and it is
tagged as one. It does not claim that spacetime is built; the metric is promised
to Chapter~5, and at this layer space and time are simply two separate primitives.
The honesty of the chain lives in those tags, so they are stated inline as each
claim appears.

\section{The five postulates}\label{sec:postulates}

The substrate of FTD is fixed by five postulates, frozen and machine-checked, each
an \etag{ax} (the constitution, \ftdid{0254}, \S1.1):

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.6em,style=nextline,itemsep=2pt]
  \item[P1 --- Discrete space.] A three-dimensional cubic lattice of voxels with
    \emph{undefined boundary}: at every specified position the axis-adjacent sites
    exist, but the lattice is not a completed-infinity $\Z^3$ totality.
    Arbitrarily large finite computations are well posed; completed limits are not.
    \etag{ax}
  \item[P2 --- Discrete time.] Dynamics proceeds in global ticks. Simultaneity at
    the substrate level is absolute --- a privileged foliation, the neo-Lorentzian
    reading made explicit by FC-2 in Chapter~\ref{ch:logic}. \etag{ax}
  \item[P3 --- Ternary states, $J$-primary.] Each voxel carries a continuous flux
    vector $J\in\R^3$ (dispositional) and a ternary state $s\in\{-1,0,+1\}$
    (actual). The flux $J$ is \emph{primary}; the state $s$ is the manifestation
    projection of $J$ through the genesis threshold rule (\S\ref{sec:channels}).
    Treating $s$ as primary would double-count. \etag{ax}
  \item[P4 --- Local causality.] Updates depend only on the 26-neighbour Moore
    neighbourhood at the previous tick; information moves at most one voxel per
    tick. \etag{ax}
  \item[P5 --- Determinism.] The update map is a function. \emph{Determinism is not
    reversibility}: a deterministic map may be many-to-one (the point Chapter~2
    turns into the arrow of time). \etag{ax}
\end{description}

These five are the only generative inputs. Everything the framework derives is
quantified over exactly P1--P5; the machine-checked theorems of the corpus
(\ftdid{0243}, \ftdid{0253}) take precisely this set as their hypothesis.

\section{Graded monism and the \texorpdfstring{$\Zi$}{Z[i]} reading (FC-0)}\label{sec:gradedmonism}

The ontology is \emph{graded monism}: one substance --- the void --- whose
\emph{dispositions} are the flux $J$, whose \emph{manifestations} are the
actualized states $\pm1$, and whose \emph{properties} (charge, mass) are emergent
from manifestation patterns. The state field is not a second substance; it is the
actualized manifestation of the flux under the genesis rule. This is the
math-first ordering of \ftdid{0153}: \emph{ontology = finite mathematical
invariant structure; physics = operationally stable readout of that structure.}

One modelling choice is promoted to a framework commitment because the entire
algebraic spine of Chapter~\ref{ch:mathematics} rests on it:

\begin{forcedbox}[FC-0 --- the $\Zi$ reading \etag{ax}\,(class)]
The cubic lattice's order-4 planar symmetry is read as the arithmetic of the
Gaussian integers $\Zi$, with unit group $\Ziu$ of order $4$. \emph{This is a
declaration, not a derivation.} The algebraic spine is forced by P1--P5
\emph{together with} FC-0, not by the discrete ontology alone --- the honesty
correction installed by the construction monograph's red-team (\ftdid{0249}),
here canonized as the zeroth framework commitment (\ftdid{0254}). Declaring it
does not derive it. \hfill\etag{ax}
\end{forcedbox}

FC-0 belongs to the second of FTD's three registers --- the
\emph{framework commitments}, which sit between the generative postulates and the
unit-fixing calibrations. The pattern repeats for FC-1 and FC-2 in
Chapter~\ref{ch:logic}: where a theorem proves the postulates leave a fork open, a
commitment picks the branch FTD asserts the world is on. The theorem proves the
fork; the commitment picks the side.

\section{Two orthogonal fields, two coupling channels}\label{sec:channels}

FTD's field content is \emph{two orthogonal fields, nested} (the constitution
\S1.3, \ftdid{0257}). The decomposition is canonical but it is a \emph{choice} of
how to package the dynamics, not a new dynamical claim --- hence its tag is a
synthesis-and-selection, never a theorem:

\begin{claim}[The primary pair, $J\perp s$]\label{cl:primarypair}
The primary field content is the orthogonal pair
\[
  \fluxstate .
\]
$J$ is continuous, reversible in isolation, and carries the wave dynamics; $s$ is
discrete, finite, and irreversible (manifestation is many-to-one). They are
\emph{orthogonal} in the precise sense that they inhabit different ontological
grades --- disposition versus actuality --- and interact through exactly two
channels and no others. \etag{syn}\,$+$\,\etag{sel}~(\ftdid{0257})
\end{claim}

\noindent\textit{Why ``orthogonal.''} There is no state--state dynamics and no
flux--flux manifestation; the two fields meet only at the two doors below. The
word is structural, not metric: it names the absence of any third coupling
channel.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.6em,style=nextline,itemsep=3pt]
  \item[Channel 1 --- Genesis / evaporation (downward, $J\to s$).] At a void site,
    $\lvert J\rvert$ crossing the manifestation threshold projects a state (the
    $s=\operatorname{sign}$ rule); falling below the evaporation threshold reverts
    it. The kinetics scale is $K_{\mathrm{MANIFEST}}$ and the trigger is
    $K_{\mathrm{GENESIS}} = N_c\cdot K_{\mathrm{MANIFEST}}$. This is the engine's
    \texttt{phase\_write} step. It is the \emph{many-to-one} door: it is where
    irreversibility, and therefore the arrow of time of Chapter~\ref{ch:logic},
    enters.
  \item[Channel 2 --- Gauss sourcing (upward, $s\to J$).] Manifested states source
    the flux through the lattice Gauss constraint
    \[
      \gausssource ,
    \]
    where $\rho_s$ is the manifested charge density. This is the engine's
    \texttt{gauss\_project} step. It is the \emph{linear} door: it feeds committed
    states back into the dispositions of their neighbours, and it is the origin of
    the geometric Coulomb law of Chapter~\ref{ch:physics}.
\end{description}

Everything else in the framework is dynamics \emph{within} one field or the other.
The two channels are the engine's actual architecture; the constitution packages
them as the canonical ontology, and that packaging is the \etag{sel} above.

\subsection{The nested pair: symplectic quadratures \texorpdfstring{$(q,p)$}{(q,p)}}\label{sec:quadratures}

Inside the flux layer there is a second, nested orthogonality. The flux sector is
a second-order field, so its true arena is phase space: per mode, the amplitude
$q$ and its rate $p=\partial_t J$ form a symplectic pair $(q,p)$. The substrate's
only native dynamical angle is the quadrature phase $\arg(q+ip)$, winding at the
dispersion frequency derived in \S\ref{sec:causalcone}. This pair carries a
distinction that becomes load-bearing in Chapter~\ref{ch:logic}:

\begin{claim}[The Crucial Distinction]\label{cl:crucial}
The quadrature pair is symplectically non-trivial yet observably commutative:
\[
  \crucialdist
\]
--- a non-zero Poisson bracket but a vanishing observable commutator
(\ftdid{0243}). The quadrature \emph{clock} is the substrate's native time-keeper;
identifying it with ``the measurement angle'' is interpretive \etag{sel}, and the
incompatibility that would make choosing a quadrature a quantum measurement is
exactly what the framework declines to import (FC-1, Chapter~\ref{ch:logic}).
\end{claim}

\noindent The two orthogonalities live at different levels and do not compete: the
primary pair is orthogonal at the \emph{grade} level (two coupling channels only);
the nested pair is orthogonal in the \emph{symplectic} sense (conjugate
quadratures of one oscillation, internal to the flux member of the primary pair).
Chapter~\ref{ch:logic} reproduces the argument behind Claim~\ref{cl:crucial} in
full; here it is recorded as part of the field inventory.

\section{Space \texorpdfstring{$\perp$}{perp} time at the base}\label{sec:spaceperptime}

P1 (the lattice) and P2 (the tick) are \emph{separate primitives}. FTD's base
ontology has a 3-space and a 1-time, not a 4-geometry. Nothing at the postulate
level mixes them (the constitution \S1.5):

\begin{claim}[Separation at the base]\label{cl:spaceperptime}
At the postulate level, space and time are orthogonal primitives. The
\emph{causal cone} that relates them is a theorem of locality
(\S\ref{sec:causalcone}); the Lorentzian \emph{metric} that would weave them into
Minkowski spacetime is \emph{not} forced by P1--P5 (\ftdid{0253}). FTD's committed
reading --- the mixing is emergent and sector-scoped, the separation fundamental
--- is FC-2, declared in Chapter~\ref{ch:logic} and measured in
Chapter~\ref{ch:physics}. \etag{ax}\;(base structure) $+$ FC-2 forward-pointer
\end{claim}

This is the single most important orientation for an outsider: at the ground floor
of FTD there is \emph{no spacetime}. There is space, there is time, and there is a
maximum speed at which one can influence the other. That maximum speed is the one
thing about the space--time relation that the postulates \emph{do} force, and it
is derived next.

\section{Reproduced derivation I: the causal cone \texorpdfstring{$c=1/\sqrt3$}{c=1/sqrt3} and the lattice dispersion}\label{sec:causalcone}

The single number this layer forces about propagation is the causal speed. It
follows from the CFL (Courant--Friedrichs--Lewy) stability condition for a discrete
wave equation on the cubic lattice, with no appeal to the state space (the
constitution \S3.2 / \S5.1 row~1; \ftdid{0253}; \texttt{FOUND\_AXIOM\_ZERO.md}
\S2.2(d)).

\begin{result}[CFL causal cone]\label{res:cfl}
On a $D$-dimensional cubic lattice with unit voxel spacing and unit tick, the
maximum stable propagation speed of the flux wave is
\[
  c \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt D},
  \qquad\text{so at } D=3:\quad \cwave .
\]
\etag{thm}~(\ftdid{0253})
\end{result}

\paragraph{Why $1/\sqrt D$.} Take the second-order flux wave equation that governs
the dispositional field, discretized on the lattice by the standard
leapfrog/centred scheme. With voxel spacing $a$ and tick $\Delta t$ set to unity,
the update for a mode of wavevector $k=(k_1,\dots,k_D)$ replaces each second
derivative by a centred difference. The temporal difference contributes a factor
$-4\sin^2(\omega\,\Delta t/2)$ and each of the $D$ spatial differences contributes
$-4\sin^2(k_j/2)$. Matching the two sides of the discretized wave equation gives
the dispersion relation
\[
  \sin^2\!\Bigl(\frac{\omega}{2}\Bigr)
  \;=\; c^2 \sum_{j=1}^{D} \sin^2\!\Bigl(\frac{k_j}{2}\Bigr).
\]
For the scheme to be stable --- for $\omega$ to stay real for \emph{every} mode,
including the worst case where all $\sin^2(k_j/2)=1$ simultaneously (the lattice
corner $k_j=\pi$) --- the right-hand side must never exceed $1$. The worst case
sums to $D$, so stability requires
\[
  c^2 \cdot D \;\le\; 1
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  c \;\le\; \frac{1}{\sqrt D},
\]
and the maximum stable speed saturates the bound. At $D=3$ this is $\cwave$. This
is a theorem about discrete wave equations on the lattice; it does not depend on
the ternary state space at all --- it is purely the geometry of P1 plus the
one-voxel-per-tick locality of P4.

\paragraph{The lattice dispersion.} In the long-wavelength (small-$k$) regime where
a single spatial mode dominates, the same matching condition reduces to the FTD
flux-wave dispersion
\[
  \dispersion ,
\]
with $c=1/\sqrt3$. The frequency $\omega(k)$ is the rate at which the quadrature
phase $\arg(q+ip)$ of \S\ref{sec:quadratures} winds; it is the substrate's native
clock. Two features matter downstream. First, for small $k$ the relation linearizes
to $\omega \approx c\,\lvert k\rvert$, recovering an ordinary light-cone in the IR
--- the seed of the emergent metric promised to Chapter~\ref{ch:physics}. Second,
at large $k$ the $\sin$ bends the dispersion away from linearity: this is a
\emph{native} ultraviolet departure from exact Lorentz invariance, not a defect,
and it reappears as a falsifiable prediction in Chapter~\ref{ch:science}.

Note what has and has not been built. The cone --- the fact that there is a finite
maximum influence speed --- is \etag{thm} and holds for \emph{any} local lattice
dynamics. The Lorentzian \emph{metric} that would promote the cone to a full
spacetime geometry is not in hand here; per Claim~\ref{cl:spaceperptime} it is
emergent and sector-scoped, and the work of building it is Chapter~\ref{ch:physics}'s.

\section{Reproduced derivation II: the genesis / Gauss coupling structure}\label{sec:couplingderiv}

The two coupling channels of \S\ref{sec:channels} are the only places the two
fields meet, and their structure is fixed by the postulates. We state each as a
rule on the configuration $(s,J)$, making explicit why one is irreversible and one
is linear.

\paragraph{The genesis projection (downward, $J\to s$).} Genesis is a threshold
map applied at each void site $v$ ($s(v)=0$) once per tick, after the flux update:
\[
  s(v) \;\longmapsto\;
  \begin{cases}
    \operatorname{sign}\!\bigl(J(v)\cdot \hat n\bigr), &
      \lvert J(v)\rvert \,>\, K_{\mathrm{GENESIS}},\\[4pt]
    0, & \lvert J(v)\rvert \,<\, K_{\mathrm{MANIFEST}}\ \text{(evaporation)},\\[4pt]
    s(v), & \text{otherwise (hysteresis band).}
  \end{cases}
\]
This is the engine's \texttt{phase\_write} step, with trigger
$K_{\mathrm{GENESIS}} = N_c\cdot K_{\mathrm{MANIFEST}}$. Three properties are
load-bearing. (i) It is a \emph{function} of the local flux, so it preserves P5
(determinism). (ii) It is \emph{many-to-one}: distinct flux histories that cross
the same threshold with the same sign land on the same state, and the hysteresis
band makes the inverse genuinely undefined --- so the map is provably
\emph{non-invertible}. This many-to-oneness is the finite, irreversible content of
the substrate, and it is what Chapter~\ref{ch:logic} elevates into the native arrow
of time (FC-2). (iii) The threshold $K_{\mathrm{MANIFEST}}$ is the manifestation
quantum; it is fixed in the \emph{calibration} register
($M_{\mathrm{REST}}=m_e$), not derived here.

\paragraph{The Gauss sourcing (upward, $s\to J$).} The committed states source the
flux through the lattice Gauss constraint, imposed once per tick by projection:
\[
  \gausssource ,
  \qquad \rho_s(v) \;=\; \text{(manifested charge at $v$)} .
\]
Concretely, the projection step replaces $J$ by the nearest field whose lattice
divergence equals the manifested density $\rho_s$ --- a linear (Poisson) solve
against the manifestation pattern. Two properties matter. (i) It is \emph{linear}
in the source, so superposition holds and the resulting static potential is the
lattice Poisson Green's function --- exactly the geometric Coulomb law reproduced
in Chapter~\ref{ch:physics}. (ii) It runs \emph{upward} only: states set boundary
data for the flux, but the Gauss step itself never creates or destroys a state.
The asymmetry between the two channels --- one many-to-one and irreversible, one
linear and reversible --- is the ontological seed of every later asymmetry in the
chain, including the $\pi/G^{*}$ split of Chapter~\ref{ch:mathematics} and the
arrow of Chapter~\ref{ch:logic}.

Together, Results~\ref{res:cfl} and the two channel rules close the ontology: a
finite ternary lattice, two orthogonal fields, one maximum speed, and two doors
between disposition and actuality. Nothing else is fundamental.

\section{What this layer does not settle}\label{sec:ontology-open}

The ontology is deliberately spare, and its honesty is in naming what it leaves
unbuilt:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{The metric is not built here.} The causal cone is \etag{thm}
    (Result~\ref{res:cfl}), but the Lorentzian metric that would make space and
    time into one geometry is \emph{not} forced by P1--P5 (\ftdid{0253}). Whether
    the substrate carries an emergent IR metric is committed by FC-2
    (Chapter~\ref{ch:logic}) and measured, sector-scoped, in
    Chapter~\ref{ch:physics} --- it is not a result of this layer.
  \item \textbf{The two-field decomposition is a choice, not a theorem.} The
    primary pair $J\perp s$ and its two-channel coupling are packaged as canonical
    ontology, but that packaging is \etag{sel}\,$+$\,\etag{syn} (\ftdid{0257}), not
    a forced decomposition. A reader is entitled to ask whether a different
    bookkeeping of the same dynamics is possible; the framework's answer is that
    this one is canonical, not that it is unique.
  \item \textbf{FC-0 is declared, not derived.} The reading of the lattice
    symmetry as $\Zi$ (\S\ref{sec:gradedmonism}) is an \etag{ax}-class commitment.
    The whole spine of Chapter~\ref{ch:mathematics} is conditional on it, and the
    condition is stated, not proven. What would overturn FC-0 is set out in
    Chapter~\ref{ch:science}'s kill-conditions.
  \item \textbf{The native angle is a selection.} Identifying the quadrature clock
    of \S\ref{sec:quadratures} with a ``measurement angle'' is interpretive
    \etag{sel}; the framework records the clock as the native time-keeper and
    declines, at the next layer, the import that would make reading it a quantum
    measurement.
\end{itemize}

\noindent With the substrate fixed --- a finite ternary lattice, two orthogonal
fields, the causal cone, and the two coupling channels --- the chain proceeds to
ask what \emph{follows} from these commitments, and what the framework deliberately
declines to import. That is the work of Chapter~\ref{ch:logic}.
