\section*{The move this layer makes: run the lattice, read off the IR}

Up to here the chain has been a construction. The ontology fixed what exists
(Chapter~1), the logic fixed what follows and what is declined (Chapter~2), the
mathematics forced a single bridge constant and a single quadratic and then ran
into a wall (Chapter~3), and the philosophy asked who reads the lattice
(Chapter~4). This chapter asks the empirical question: \emph{when you actually
run the substrate, what infrared physics comes out?}

The answer has two registers, and keeping them apart is the whole honesty of the
project. In the first register the lattice, simulated as an instrument,
\emph{reproduces} a long list of infrared behaviour --- a static $1/r$ potential,
a relativistic causal cone, a mass gap, cluster masses that track particle
masses, a gravitational hierarchy of the right order, confinement signatures,
Ward identities. Several of these are not fitted; they are forced by lattice
geometry and fall out of the dynamics with zero free parameters. In the second
register sits the one quantity every outsider asks about first: the fine
structure constant $\alpha$. Here the framework can only \emph{match}, not
\emph{force}. The master quadratic of Chapter~3 has a root that agrees with
$\alpha^{-1}$ to roughly one part per million --- but Chapter~3's route-invariance
boundary (MC-T4.3) showed that no FTD-native route compels the operator assembly
behind that quadratic. So the identification stays a strongly-motivated
conjecture, and this chapter says so plainly.

The intuition to carry out of this chapter is therefore a discipline, not a
triumph: the computational effective field theory \emph{measures and derives}
infrared physics through the engine, each result wearing its own epistemic tag;
it reproduces a great deal; and at exactly the place where it would be most
spectacular to claim a derivation --- $\alpha$ from pure lattice geometry --- it
declines to overclaim. The accounting at the end of the chapter
(${\sim}23$ derived, ${\sim}129$ parametric, ${\sim}10$ imposed) is the
quantitative form of that discipline.

The reason the identification cannot be promoted is the boundary mapped in

\[

The dashed arrow in the chapter mini-map (Figure~\ref{fig:minimap-physics}), from

\subsection{Phase-G geometric Coulomb is the lattice Poisson Green's function}
\label{sec:geomcoulomb}

\begin{claim}[Geometric Coulomb, \etag{thm}, \ftdid{0004}]
The engine's emergent static pair potential has a closed-form prediction with
\emph{zero free parameters}: it is the periodic lattice Poisson Green's function,
carrying no fine-structure content whatsoever.
\end{claim}

\emph{Setup.} The engine's Gauss law sources the flux field from the ternary
state with no coupling constant in front: $\gausssource$, with $\rho_s = s \in
\{-1,0,+1\}$. The charge \emph{is} the state value. On the $L^3$ cubic torus the
seven-point Laplacian has the periodic Green's function
\[
  G_L(r) \;=\; \frac{1}{L^3}\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{e^{\,i\,k\cdot r}}{D(k)},
  \qquad D(k) \;=\; 2\bigl(3 - \cos k_x - \cos k_y - \cos k_z\bigr),
\]
with $k_i = 2\pi n_i / L$ and the zero mode removed (charge neutrality on the
torus).

\emph{Derivation.} Place a $+1$ charge at $n_1$ and a $-1$ charge at $n_2$. Each
solves a discrete Poisson equation,
\[
  \nabla^2 \phi_1 = -\,\delta(n - n_1), \qquad
  \nabla^2 \phi_2 = +\,\delta(n - n_2), \qquad
  J_a = -\nabla\phi_a,
\]
and by construction $\phi_1(n) = G_L(n - n_1)$. The pair flux is
$J_{\mathrm{pair}} = J_1 + J_2$, so the field energy contains the cross term
$2\,J_1\!\cdot\! J_2$. Integrating by parts on the torus (no boundary),
\[
  \sum_n J_1\!\cdot\! J_2
  \;=\; \sum_n \nabla\phi_1\!\cdot\!\nabla\phi_2
  \;=\; -\sum_n \phi_1\,\nabla^2\phi_2
  \;=\; \sum_n \phi_1\,\delta(n-n_2)\cdot s_2
  \;=\; -\,\phi_1(n_2)
  \;=\; -\,G_L(r),
\]
with $r = |n_1 - n_2|$. The interaction energy is therefore $V(r) = -2\,G_L(r)$,
and the engine's effective coupling readout is
\[
  \boxed{\;\geomcoulomb\;=\; -V\cdot r\;}
\]
in the $\sum|J|^2$ energy convention.\footnote{The current engine accumulates
field energy with a $\tfrac12$ prefactor (post-2026-04-27 refactor), which pairs
$V(r) = -G_L(r)$ and $\alpha_r = r\,G_L(r)$; the form $\geomcoulomb$ used here and
in the IR catalog is the canonical $\sum|J|^2$ convention of \ftdid{0004}, related
by the factor of two (\ftdid{0286}~v2). The physical content --- that the
readout is the lattice Green's function with no free parameter --- is
convention-independent.} For $r \ll L$ at large $L$ the continuum limit of the
lattice Green's function is $G_L(r) \approx 1/(4\pi r)$, so
\[
  \alpha_r(r) \;\approx\; \frac{1}{2\pi} \;\approx\; 0.1592,
\]
a universal lattice constant depending only on $r/L$, with no $\alpha$ anywhere
in it. A parameter-free FFT evaluation of $G_L(r)$ against the measured
$\alpha_r(r,L)$ over $L \in \{32,\dots,384\}$ reaches $R^2 = 1.0000$ with $0.07\%$
median residual in the $L=384$ Coulomb tail. \hfill$\qed$

\emph{Reading.} The lesson of this theorem is negative and important: the
emergent static potential the engine produces is \emph{geometric Coulomb} ---
pure lattice geometry, zero fine-structure content. The earlier ``plateau at
$3.6\times\alpha_{\mathrm{ref}}$'' headline was a category error (a slice of
$2\,r\,G_L(r)$ at fixed $r/L$, compared to an unrelated electroweak coupling).
Where $\alpha$ enters the engine's dynamics it does so by an explicit inserted
coupling $g_c = \sqrt{2\pi\,\alpha}$, under which the same theorem predicts
$\alpha_r(r,L;g_c) = g_c^2\cdot 2\,r\,G_L(r)$ and the engine reproduces QED
Coulomb to lattice-spacing precision --- a \etag{par} insertion, not an emergent
derivation of $\alpha$. This is the engine-side companion to
\S\ref{sec:alphabridge}'s boundary.

\subsection{The cluster-mass coefficient $\kquarter$ from $O_h$ representation theory}
\label{sec:clustermass}

\begin{claim}[Cluster-mass linear coefficient, \etag{der} at linear level, \ftdid{0110}]
The empirical cluster-size scaling $\clustermass$ has its coefficient
$\kquarter$ \emph{derived} from the representation theory of the cubic point
group $O_h$ on the $27$-voxel Moore block; the link to the full nonlinear engine
is a \etag{smc}, and a rigorous nonlinear bridge is \etag{opn}.
\end{claim}

\emph{The empirical fact.} Engine campaigns measure $N(A) \approx k\,A^2$ with
$k = 0.239 \pm 0.018$ across eleven amplitudes, five Standard-Model particles,
and two injection geometries. The structural question is why $k \approx \tfrac14$.

\emph{Step 1 --- the $27$-block decomposition (\etag{thm}).} The $3^3$ Moore block
of $27$ voxels carries the natural permutation representation $\rho_{27}$ of $O_h$
(order $48$), with four orbits: the centre ($1$), the six SC face neighbours, the
twelve FCC edge neighbours, and the eight BCC corner neighbours. It decomposes as
\[
  \rho_{27} \;\cong\; 4\,A_{1g} \oplus 2\,E_g \oplus 2\,T_{2g}
            \oplus A_{2u} \oplus 3\,T_{1u} \oplus T_{2u},
\]
which checks dimensionally ($4 + 4 + 6 + 1 + 9 + 3 = 27$). The multiplicity of
the trivial irrep follows from the character-table formula
\[
  \mult(A_{1g},\rho_{27})
  \;=\; \frac{1}{|O_h|}\sum_{\text{classes}}
        \bigl(\text{size}\bigr)\,\chi_{\rho_{27}}(g)\,\chi_{A_{1g}}(g)^{*}
  \;=\; \frac{192}{48} \;=\; 4 ,
\]
where $\chi_{\rho_{27}}(g)$ counts voxels fixed by $g$ and $\chi_{A_{1g}}\equiv 1$.
This is a theorem of group theory, independent of any FTD-specific machinery, and
$\mult(A_{1g}) = 4 = \Nbase$.

\emph{Step 2 --- the $A_{1g}$ subspace and the projected Laplacian (\etag{thm}).}
The four-dimensional $A_{1g}$ subspace is spanned by the orbit-averaged scalars
\[
  e_0 = \delta_{\text{centre}}, \quad
  e_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{SC}}, \quad
  e_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{12}}\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{FCC}}, \quad
  e_3 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt8}\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{BCC}}.
\]
The eighteen-point isotropic lattice Laplacian is $O_h$-equivariant, so by Schur's
lemma it preserves each irrep subspace; on $\mathrm{span}\{e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3\}$ it
acts as an explicit symmetric $4\times4$ matrix $M$ whose four eigenvalues
$\{-1.586,\,-3.862,\,-4.414,\,-4.805\}$ and eigenvectors are obtained by direct
diagonalisation (trace $= -44/3$, confirmed against the full $27\times27$
operator to machine precision).

\emph{Step 3 --- centre injection is $A_{1g}$-pure (geometric fact).} The centre
voxel is the unique fixed point of the $O_h$ action: every cubic symmetry maps
$(0,0,0)$ to itself. Hence $\delta_{\text{centre}} = e_0$ is $O_h$-invariant, so
$A_{1g}$-pure, with zero overlap on any non-trivial irrep.

\emph{Step 4 --- the mean energy fraction is $\tfrac14$ (\etag{der}).} In the
$M$-eigenbasis, $\delta_{\text{centre}}$ projects with squared coefficients
\[
  \Bigl(\tfrac38,\ \tfrac18,\ \tfrac38,\ \tfrac18\Bigr),
  \qquad \sum = 1,
  \qquad \text{mean} = \frac{1}{\Nbase} = \frac14 .
\]
The mean is not a coincidence: it follows from two identities. The
\emph{sum identity} gives $\|\delta_{\text{centre}}\|^2 = 1$, and because the
state is $A_{1g}$-pure that unit norm is carried entirely within the four
$A_{1g}$ eigenmodes. The \emph{counting identity} gives exactly $\Nbase = 4$ such
modes (Step~1). Therefore the mean per-mode energy fraction is $1/\Nbase = \tfrac14$,
\emph{independently} of the non-uniform per-mode split $(\tfrac38,\tfrac18,
\tfrac38,\tfrac18)$ --- which itself reflects the parity structure of the four
eigenvectors and is washed out to equipartition under Langevin thermalisation.

\emph{Step 5 --- from the mean to $N(A)$.} The injected energy
$E_{\mathrm{inj}} = A^2 K_{\mathrm{genesis}}^2$ distributes over the four $A_{1g}$
modes; the cluster's voxel count is the manifested energy divided by the per-voxel
cost $K_{\mathrm{genesis}}^2$, harvesting the mean fraction:
\[
  N(A) \;=\; \frac{1}{\Nbase}\,A^2 \;=\; \frac14\,A^2,
  \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \clustermass .
\]
The result is direction-invariant: an axial injection $J = (A,0,0)\,\delta_{\text{centre}}$
and a body-diagonal injection $J = (A/\sqrt3)(1,1,1)\,\delta_{\text{centre}}$ give
\emph{identical} $A_{1g}$ energy distributions $(\tfrac38,\tfrac18,\tfrac38,\tfrac18)\,A^2$
and the same mean $A^2/4$, because each Cartesian component evolves as an
independent scalar field under the same scalar Laplacian. \hfill$\qed$

\emph{Reading.} Steps~1--4 are \etag{thm}/\etag{der}: the character-table
multiplicity, the projected eigenstructure, the $A_{1g}$-purity of the centre, and
the mean-energy coefficient $\tfrac14$ are all rigorous at the linear level. The

\subsection{The gravity ratio $\alpha_G(e,e)$}
