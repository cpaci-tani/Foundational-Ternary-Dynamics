\section*{The move this layer makes: a forced chain, then a precisely located wall}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{The move this layer makes}

The ontology of Chapter~\ref{ch:ontology} hands mathematics one modelling
choice and a fistful of postulates; this chapter shows what those force. The
striking thing is how far the forcing reaches and how sharply it stops. From a
single reading of the cubic lattice's quarter-turn symmetry as the arithmetic
of the Gaussian integers $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, one integer is forced ($4=|\Ziu|$),
then one bridge constant ($G^{*}=\Gamma(1/4)/\Gamma(3/4)$, reachable four
independent ways), then one quadratic
($P(x)=x^{2}-16\,\Gss\,x+16\,\Gsss$) whose larger root sits $1.26$ parts per
million from $1/\alpha$. Each step is an ordinary, checkable piece of
mathematics built on the last.

And then the chain hits a wall --- not a failure of effort but a provable
boundary. To read the quadratic's root \emph{as} the physical coupling, the
substrate would have to assemble a particular $2\times 2$ operator. The
ontology forces the \emph{menu} of that operator's ingredients but not the
\emph{assembly} itself. That gap is the whole honesty of the project: it is why
$x_+ = 1/\alpha$ stays a strongly-motivated conjecture and not a theorem, and
it is the hinge between the forced mathematics of this chapter and the
\emph{matched} physics of Chapter~\ref{ch:physics}.

The reader's takeaway, fixed in advance, is the canonical accounting of the
spine: \textbf{nine numbered results, of which seven are theorem-grade and two
are honestly tiered below theorem grade}. This chapter states each at its
canonical tag, reproduces three load-bearing derivations in full, and maps the
boundary precisely. It introduces no new mathematics. Where it disagrees with
\texttt{SPEC\_ALGEBRAIC\_SPINE.md}, the \textsc{ledger}, or the construction
monograph, those documents are correct and this one has a bug.

\section{The seed: \texorpdfstring{$\mathbb{Z}[i]$}{Z[i]} and the forced 4}
\label{sec:math-seed}

The construction begins with one structural choice and one group-theory fact,
and the honesty of everything downstream depends on not confusing them.

The cubic lattice's coordinate planes carry an order-4 rotational symmetry: a
$90^{\circ}$ rotation of the $(x,y)$ plane cycles
$(1,0)\mapsto(0,1)\mapsto(-1,0)\mapsto(0,-1)\mapsto(1,0)$. FTD's seed move is to
\emph{read this planar quarter-turn as the arithmetic of the Gaussian integers}
$\Zi=\{a+bi : a,b\in\Z\}$, with the rotation realized as multiplication by $i$.
That the lattice's quarter-turn \emph{should be read through} $\Zi$ rather than
some other order-4 structure is a framework commitment --- the
discrete-ontology analogue of building $\C$ as $\R[i]$: natural, motivated, and
a \emph{choice}. It is the framework commitment FC-0 \etag{ax}
(\ftdid{0254}, canonized from \ftdid{0249}); no later theorem may silently
inherit the status ``forced'' from this step, which was ``chosen.''

Once $\Zi$ is the substrate's arithmetic, the rest is ordinary algebra. The
unit group is
\[
  \Ziu = \{1,\,i,\,-1,\,-i\} \;\cong\; \Z/4, \qquad |\Ziu| = 4,
\]
since $a+bi$ is a unit iff its norm $a^{2}+b^{2}=1$, whose only integer
solutions are $(\pm1,0)$ and $(0,\pm1)$ \etag{thm}. The single integer this seed
deposits into the construction is $4=|\Ziu|$. It reappears, squared, as the
coefficient $16$ of the master quadratic
(\S\ref{sec:math-masterquad}). The discipline to hold: $4$ is a \emph{theorem}
about $\Zi$; that the lattice is entitled to $\Zi$ in the first place is the
\etag{ax}.

\section{\texorpdfstring{$G^{*}$}{G*}: the bridge constant, four ways ---
  and why it is not \texorpdfstring{$\varpi$}{varpi}}
\label{sec:math-gstar}

The constant at the centre of the spine is
\[
  \Gstardef \etag{thm}
\]
(Theorem~1; \ftdid{0001}; \textsc{ot}-1.2). What earns $G^{*}$ its place is not
any one definition but a \emph{convergence}: four constructions of differing
character --- a $\Gamma$-ratio, a lattice Green's-function period, a
regularized determinant ratio, and a finite product --- all land on the same
real number. The four routes, each \etag{thm} (conditional on the cited classical
result where one is named):

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{The $\Gamma$-ratio.} Directly from the Euler reflection formula
    (reproduced in full in \S\ref{sec:math-deriv-gstar}); the canonical
    statement of \textsc{ot}-1.2.
  \item \textbf{The Watson BCC period.} The body-centred-cubic Watson integral
    satisfies $\watsonval$ (Theorem~5; \textsc{ot}-2.1), conditional on
    Watson's 1939 closed-form evaluation, consolidated by Glasser--Zucker
    (1980). This is $G^{*}$ arising from a \emph{lattice} quantity, of a wholly
    different character from the $\Gamma$-ratio.
  \item \textbf{The $\detz$ quarter-conjugacy bridge.} With $J$ the conjugacy
    operator ($J^{2}=-I$; the same $\Z/4$ seed), the quarter-twisted spectra
    $D_{1/4},D_{3/4}$ and Lerch's determinant formula $\lerch$ give the ratio
    $\detzratio$ (\textsc{ot}-1.7, \ftdid{0141}). The arithmetic content ties
    it to the seed: $4\cdot D_{1/4}$ and $4\cdot D_{3/4}$ are the two
    non-trivial residue classes $\bmod\,4$ --- the split and inert prime classes
    of $\Zi$.
  \item \textbf{The finite-$N$ attractor.} The finite product
    $\finiteN$ is computable in $O(N)$ rational operations, and
    $\finiteNrate$ (\textsc{ot}-1.8, \ftdid{0142}). This route discharges the
    undefined-boundary ontology's $\varepsilon$--$L$ obligation for $G^{*}$:
    it supplies a convergent \emph{finite} computation, no completed infinity
    required.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{boundarybox}[The $G^{*}\neq\varpi$ warning (\ftdid{0117})]
$G^{*}\approx\Gstarapprox$ is the lemniscatic \emph{ratio}
$\Gamma(1/4)/\Gamma(3/4)$. It is \textbf{not} the Bernoulli/Gauss lemniscate

$\Gstarclosed$ (equivalently $\varpidef$). The two are sometimes both called

audit (\ftdid{0117}) found and corrected exactly this conflation across five

\end{boundarybox}

\section{The master quadratic}
\label{sec:math-masterquad}

With $G^{*}$ in hand, the construction's central polynomial is purely
algebraic. Define
\[
  \masterquad. \etag{thm}
\]
Its discriminant factors cleanly,
\[
  \discriminant
\]
which is \emph{positive} because $G^{*}>1/4$ (indeed $G^{*}\approx\Gstarapprox$),
so both roots are real:
\[
  \rootsexplicit
\]
Numerically $x_+=\xplusnum$ and $x_-=\xminusnum$. This is the quadratic formula
applied to a polynomial whose coefficients are integer multiples of powers of
$G^{*}$; nothing more is involved, and the whole of it is \etag{thm}
(Theorem~2; \ftdid{0001}; \textsc{ot}-1.1). The full derivation is reproduced
in \S\ref{sec:math-deriv-masterquad}.

\paragraph{The coefficient-16 soft spot, flagged honestly.} The prefactor $16$
has a tempting arithmetic reading. For the lemniscatic CM elliptic curve
$E:y^{2}=x^{3}-x$, the automorphism group over $\overline{\Q}$ is
$\Aut(E)=\{\pm1,\pm i\}\cong\Z/4$ --- the unit group of $\Zi$ again --- so
$|\Aut(E)|=4$ and $|\Aut(E)|^{2}=16$. That $|\Aut(E)|^{2}=16$ is a \etag{thm}
(Theorem~4 arithmetic fact; \ftdid{0006}). But the claim that the master
quadratic's prefactor is \emph{forced} to equal $|\Aut(E)|^{2}$ --- that two
distinct objects both equal $16$ for a structural reason --- is
\textbf{not proven}; the structural necessity is \etag{cnj} (\textsc{ot}-4.1,
the lone Tier-4 entry: ``two distinct objects both equal $16$; the structural
reason for the coincidence is conjectured but not proved''). This is the
framework's softest mathematical spot. A partial unification exists (the
tower-level reading of \ftdid{0122} supplies a reason for $k=4$, hence
$2^{k}=16$, via $|\Ziu|^{2}=16$), but it is a partial unification, not a
forcing theorem.

\paragraph{No physics here, and the retired smaller root.} In this chapter,
$x_+=\xplusnum$ is a root of a polynomial --- nothing more. Its identification
with $1/\alpha$ is a \etag{smc} (\ftdid{0013}), deferred in full to
Chapter~\ref{ch:physics}; it is not asserted as forced anywhere in the
construction. The smaller root $x_-\approx\xminusapprox$ is a pure algebraic
artifact, fixed by the Vieta relation $x_- = 16\,\Gsss/x_+$ the instant $x_+$
is fixed, and it carries no physics: the historical identification
$x_-\!\leftrightarrow\! N_c$ is \textbf{retired} (the \textsc{ledger} row
FTD-0014 was removed in commit \texttt{ca7eb61}). FTD's $N_c=3$ is
\etag{thm} from topology, independently sourced --- the Moore Layer Theorem and
the four convergent routes of \texttt{DERIV\_NC\_FROM\_TOPOLOGY.md} --- and owes
nothing to the polynomial's smaller root.

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha} boundary: dynamical, not structural}
\label{sec:math-boundary}

The spine forces $G^{*}$, the coefficient $16$, and the polynomial's roots. To
turn the polynomial \emph{toward physics} one more thing is needed, and naming
it sharply is the entire content of this section. The argument structure is
summarized here; the exhaustive four-route case analysis is in the construction
monograph (Part~II), cited in full below.

\paragraph{The readout move.} Read the quadratic as the characteristic
polynomial of a $2\times2$ \emph{readout operator} $W$ whose dominant eigenvalue
is to be the measured inverse coupling. For its characteristic polynomial to be
$P$, the operator must have the \emph{operator assembly}
\[
  \assembly.
\]
The question that decides whether $\alpha$ is derived or merely matched is
exactly: does $\Ftheory$ --- the five postulates, the algebraic spine, and the
$O_h$ representation theory of the BCC corner module --- \emph{force} this
assembly, or is it an imposed selection? This is the central foundational
obstruction the canonical sources call \textbf{MC-T4.3}.

\paragraph{What is forced: the menu.} The canonical attack (\ftdid{0242})
ran four FTD-native routes of differing character (a $J$-twisted
$\detz$ operator, a BCC body-diagonal response operator, the lemniscatic-CM
arithmetic of $E:y^{2}=x^{3}-x$, and a forced-variational / period-ring route).
Every route agrees, with nothing smuggled, on what \emph{is} forward-forced
\etag{der}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item the \textbf{even trace} $16\,\Gss$ --- where $16=|\mu_4|^{2}$ for
    $E:y^{2}=x^{3}-x$ (\ftdid{0006}) and $G^{*2}=2\pi\!\cdot\!G_{\mathrm{BCC}}(0)$,
    the Watson BCC self-energy (\ftdid{0001});
  \item the \textbf{existence of a clean FTD-native odd source}: the $J$-twisted
    determinant ratio $\detz(D_{3/4})/\detz(D_{1/4})=\Gamma(1/4)/\Gamma(3/4)=G^{*}$
    (degree $1$; the $\sqrt{2\pi}$ cancels). This is strictly stronger than
    trace-only: it makes $16\,\Gsss=16\,\Gss\!\cdot\!G^{*}$ \emph{assemblable}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What is not forced: the assembly $W$.} What no route forces
\etag[ no-go]{fo} is the \emph{operator assembly itself} --- that the same
$2\times2$ readout carrying $\Tr W=16\,\Gss$ also has $\det W=16\,\Gsss$. For a
$2\times2$ operator, trace and determinant are \textbf{independent invariants};
fixing the trace leaves the determinant free. The odd scalar $G^{*}$ exists,
but nothing forces the \emph{gluing} --- neither that the two invariants belong
to one operator, nor that the odd scalar lands in the determinant slot. The
verdict across all four routes is therefore \textbf{0 of 4 force the assembly},
and (by the route-invariance theorem below) this is one structural fact reached
four ways, not four independent failures. The canonical phrasing:
\textbf{$\alpha$ is dynamical, not structural} --- the ontology forces the
\emph{menu} but not the \emph{dish}. This is a \etag[ no-go]{fo}, \textbf{not a
theorem and not a derivation of $\alpha$}.

\paragraph{The boundary is built from theorem-grade masonry.} The deeper attack
(\ftdid{0243}) established three genuinely theorem-grade facts that hem the
open kernel into a single, precisely located gap:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{The flip is ruled out \etag{thm}.} The only geometric candidate
    for an operator that forces $\det=16\,\Gsss$ --- the $C_3(\langle111\rangle)$
    three-plane $\detz$ product --- is excluded from the rank-2 readout: the
    complex structure $J$ ($J^{2}=-I$) required by the trace forces $O_h$ to
    break to one $C_4$ axis, so $C_3(\langle111\rangle)\notin\mathrm{Stab}$,
    while the three-plane product requires it; and $\langle C_4,C_3\rangle=O$
    (machine-checked) leaves no readout.
  \item \textbf{Leg~3b closes its own scope \etag{thm}.} No
    $C_3(\langle111\rangle)$-equivariant rank-2 restriction of the three-plane
    source carries $(\Tr,\Det)=(16\,\Gss,16\,\Gsss)$; the chain is
    reality $\Rightarrow$ scalar-$i$ $\Rightarrow$ $C_4$ $\Rightarrow$ $O$ ---
    the same wall reached from the restriction side.
  \item \textbf{The reduction is route-invariant \etag{thm}.} All four attacks
    reduce to the same obligation because $\Q(G^{*})$ is the Galois-fixed field
    of the master quadratic's $\Z/2$: the splitting field over $\Q(G^{*})$ is
    $\Q(G^{*})(\sqrtwall)$, and every forward-forced \emph{symmetric} datum
    (the Watson trace, the $\detz$ ratio, the Chowla--Selberg periods) lives in
    the $\sigma$-fixed subfield $\Q(G^{*})$ and is provably blind to which root
    is $1/\alpha$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The square-root wall and the conditional theorem.} In the field
dialect, the residual obligation is the cleanest form and gives the boundary its
name (\textbf{K-BIND}): to fix $\alpha$, the substrate must \emph{natively
realize} the squarefree generator $\sqrtwall$ of the quadratic extension
$\splitfield$ --- the one antisymmetric beable that breaks the $\Z/2$
root-swapping symmetry and selects the physical branch. Every symmetric datum
cannot tell the two roots apart; this square root is exactly the missing
ingredient. K-BIND is \etag{cn} (\ftdid{0244}): axiomatizing the admissible
operator calculus over $V_{\mathrm{complex}}\cong\Ziu$-module $\Zi^{2}$ forces
all invariants into $\Q(G^{*})$, and the degree-2 extension cannot be natively
realized (transcendence of $G^{*}$, Chudnovsky 1976), so no native operator
reaches $x_+$ without the external selection $W$. What \emph{is} provable
unconditionally is the conditional theorem \etag{thm}: $\Ftheory$ does not force
$\alpha$ --- the assembly $(\Tr,\Det)=(16\,\Gss,16\,\Gsss)$ is logically
independent of $\Ftheory$ --- unless $\Ftheory$ is extended by a substrate-native
binding law $W$ fixing the determinant's odd-$G^{*}$ exponent at exactly $1$.
The independence is witnessed on both sides: $\Ftheory\cup\{W\}$ is consistent
(the master quadratic is the witness model), and $\Ftheory\cup\{\neg W\}$ is
consistent ($\det=16\,\Gsf$ and $\det=G^{*}$ are explicit, $\Ftheory$-consistent
alternative assemblies). $W$ is therefore a 6th-postulate-class input
(\ftdid{0243}).

\paragraph{Net.} \textbf{MC-T4.3 remains a \etag[ no-go]{fo}}, and
\textbf{$x_+=1/\alpha$ (\ftdid{0013}) remains a \etag{smc}} --- nothing here
promotes either. The contrast with $N_c=3$ is exact: $N_c$ \emph{is} structural,
forced from $O_h$ and topology by four independent routes with no

\section{Reproduced derivation I: the \texorpdfstring{$G^{*}$}{G*} identity}
\label{sec:math-deriv-gstar}

The first load-bearing chain reproduced in full. \emph{Goal:} establish the two
standard closed forms of $G^{*}$ from the definition and the Euler reflection
formula alone.

\emph{Step 1 --- the reflection value.} The $\Gamma$-function reflection
formula $\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\pi/\sin(\pi z)$ at $z=1/4$ gives
\[
  \reflection
\]
since $\sin(\pi/4)=1/\sqrt2$.

\emph{Step 2 --- the first closed form.} By definition $G^{*}=\Gamma(1/4)/\Gamma(3/4)$.
Multiply numerator and denominator by $\Gamma(1/4)$:
\[
  G^{*} = \frac{\Gamma(1/4)}{\Gamma(3/4)}
        = \frac{\Gamma(1/4)^{2}}{\Gamma(1/4)\,\Gamma(3/4)}
        = \frac{\Gamma(1/4)^{2}}{\pi\sqrt2},
\]
substituting the Step-1 value $\Gamma(1/4)\Gamma(3/4)=\pi\sqrt2$ in the last
equality.

\emph{Step 3 --- the lemniscate form.} The Bernoulli/Gauss lemniscate constant
is $\varpidef$. Solving for $\Gamma(1/4)^{2}=2\sqrt{2\pi}\,\varpi$ and
substituting into Step~2,
\[
  G^{*} = \frac{2\sqrt{2\pi}\,\varpi}{\pi\sqrt2}
        = \frac{2\varpi}{\sqrt\pi}.
\]
Collecting Steps 2--3 gives the canonical closed-form chain
\[
  \Gstarclosed,
\]
numerically $G^{*}=\Gstarnum$ \etag{thm} (Theorem~1; \ftdid{0001}). This is a
three-line proof. \textbf{Note} that the lemniscate form makes the $G^{*}\neq\varpi$
distinction quantitative: $G^{*}$ and $\varpi$ differ by the factor
$2/\sqrt\pi\approx1.128$, so $G^{*}\approx\Gstarapprox$ while
$\varpi\approx\varpiapprox$. They are never interchangeable.

\section{Reproduced derivation II: the master-quadratic assembly}
\label{sec:math-deriv-masterquad}

The second chain, reproduced from the (trace, determinant) data to the explicit
roots. \emph{Goal:} given the assembly $\assembly$, recover $P(x)$, its
discriminant, and $x_\pm$ in closed form.

\emph{Step 1 --- the characteristic polynomial.} A $2\times2$ operator $W$ with
trace $\Tr W$ and determinant $\det W$ has characteristic polynomial
$x^{2}-(\Tr W)\,x+(\det W)$. Inserting the assembly $(\Tr W,\det W)=(16\,\Gss,16\,\Gsss)$
gives exactly
\[
  \masterquad.
