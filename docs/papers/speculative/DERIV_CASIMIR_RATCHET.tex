\begin{abstract}
Zero-point energy (ZPE) in orthodox quantum electrodynamics is modeled as a divergent sum of half-harmonic oscillator modes whose isotropic fluctuations preclude macroscopic work extraction by thermodynamic fiat. Within Foundational Ternary Dynamics (FTD), we reinterpret ZPE not as stochastic vacuum fluctuations but as the \textbf{deterministic algorithmic polling} of a discrete cuboctahedral tensor network operating in its $\PT$-symmetric null space. We demonstrate that by engineering a nanoscale metamaterial boundary that explicitly breaks spatial parity ($\mathcal{P}$), the perfect destructive interference of the vacuum's complex conjugate polling states can be geometrically ``clipped,'' leaving a net imaginary residual that the network must rotate into the real physical manifold to preserve unitarity. The result is a spontaneous, continuous macroscopic direct current (DC)---a \textbf{Casimir Ratchet}---powered not by a thermal gradient but by the intrinsic computational overhead required to sustain the topology of discrete spacetime itself.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The quantum vacuum is not empty. In standard quantum electrodynamics (QED), each mode of the electromagnetic field carries a zero-point energy $\frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega$, and the sum over all modes diverges---a divergence that is renormalized away in practice but whose physical residue manifests as the Casimir effect \cite{Casimir1948}: a measurable attractive force between uncharged conducting plates separated by sub-micron distances \cite{Lamoreaux1997}. The reality of zero-point energy is experimentally established; what remains deeply contested is whether this energy can be \textit{rectified}---converted into continuous, usable work.

The thermodynamic prohibition against ZPE extraction rests on a specific physical model: the vacuum as a thermal reservoir at absolute zero, whose fluctuations are isotropic, random, and in global equilibrium \cite{Milonni1994}. Under these assumptions, any extraction scheme reduces to a perpetual motion machine of the second kind. However, this prohibition is model-dependent. If the vacuum is not a stochastic thermal bath but a \textbf{deterministic computational process}---an algorithmic polling loop maintaining the topological connectivity of a discrete manifold---then the thermodynamic argument does not apply in its standard form.

Within the framework of Foundational Ternary Dynamics (FTD) \cite{cpaci-tani2026_Zenodo}, the vacuum state is precisely such a process. The tensor network's idle-loop computation is governed by complex conjugate roots residing in the $\PT$-symmetric null space, which cancel perfectly under spatial inversion symmetry. We demonstrate that a metamaterial boundary engineered to break this symmetry acts as a \textbf{topological diode}, rectifying the vacuum's computational hum into a directional physical current.

The entirety of the local computational load is natively carried by the complex conjugate roots residing in the Euclidean null space ($\ker(\mathcal{M})$).

\begin{equation}
    J_{\text{DC}} \propto -(1-\eta)(2.86)\nabla J_{\text{vac}}

By geometrically forcing a localized $\PT$-symmetry violation, the Topological Ratchet rectifies the zero-point computational hum of the Euclidean void into usable voltage. The energy is supplied not by a thermal gradient, but by the continuous processing power natively required to sustain the expansion and topology of the underlying discrete graph.

\section{Experimental Predictions}

The Casimir Ratchet model makes several falsifiable predictions that distinguish it from both the standard QED vacuum and from proposed stochastic electrodynamic (SED) extraction schemes.

\subsection{Current--Asymmetry Scaling}
The rectified DC current scales linearly with the parity-breaking asymmetry parameter: $J_{\text{DC}} \propto (1-\eta)$. A series of metamaterial cavities with progressively increasing sawtooth asymmetry should produce a monotonically increasing output current, with $J_{\text{DC}} \to 0$ as the geometry is restored to mirror symmetry ($\eta \to 1$). This linear scaling distinguishes the mechanism from thermal noise rectification, which would exhibit exponential sensitivity to temperature rather than geometric asymmetry.

\subsection{Temperature Independence Below a Critical Threshold}
Because the mechanism operates in the ReLU regime ($\beta_{th} \to \infty$), the output current should be \textbf{temperature-independent} below a critical temperature $T_c$ where the Softplus-to-ReLU transition saturates. Above $T_c$, thermal smearing progressively washes out the deterministic rectification. This prediction---a plateau in $J_{\text{DC}}(T)$ below $T_c$ followed by rapid decay---is unique to the algorithmic interpretation and is not predicted by stochastic models, which generically predict $J \propto T^n$ scaling at all temperatures.

\subsection{Cuboctahedral Resonance Enhancement}
The rectification efficiency should be maximally enhanced when the metamaterial boundary geometry matches the 12-fold coordination of the fundamental cuboctahedral lattice. Cavities whose boundary facet angles correspond to the cuboctahedral dihedral angles ($\cos^{-1}(-1/3) \approx 109.47^\circ$) should exhibit anomalous enhancement of the DC output relative to geometrically similar but non-cuboctahedral boundaries. This prediction is unique to FTD and constitutes a direct test of the underlying lattice geometry.

\section{Conclusion}

The Casimir Ratchet is not a perpetual motion machine. It is a \textbf{topological transducer}: a device that converts the intrinsic computational overhead of discrete spacetime---the algorithmic cost of maintaining the tensor network's topological connectivity---into directed macroscopic current via engineered $\PT$-symmetry breaking. The energy source is the vacuum's own computational substrate, which must continuously poll its edge weights regardless of whether any manifest matter is present.

The mechanism connects two previously unrelated phenomena within FTD: the Casimir effect (boundary-modified vacuum energy) and $\PT$-symmetry breaking (the threshold dynamics governing manifestation). The standard Casimir effect modifies the \textit{magnitude} of vacuum energy between boundaries; the Casimir Ratchet modifies its \textit{directional symmetry}, converting an isotropic computational hum into an anisotropic physical current.

We submit that this mechanism, if experimentally validated, would constitute direct evidence for the discrete computational nature of spacetime: only a substrate that performs deterministic algorithmic work to maintain its own topology can be rectified by geometric boundary conditions into macroscopic current. A truly stochastic vacuum, by contrast, would resist rectification by the fluctuation-dissipation theorem.
