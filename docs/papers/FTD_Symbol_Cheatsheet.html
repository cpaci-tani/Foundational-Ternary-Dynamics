Calculus & Analysis

d or dx
Differential element
"An infinitesimally small piece of x." In ∫f(x)dx, the dx means "add up f(x) times a tiny slice of x." In FTD papers written as \dd for upright d (typographic convention). On the lattice, differentials become finite differences: dx → Δx = 1 (one lattice spacing).

lim
Limit
"What does this approach as the input grows?" limn→∞(1+1/n)n = e means "as n gets larger and larger, (1+1/n)n gets closer and closer to e." A limit never reaches ∞ — it describes the trend. Infinity is epistemic: the limit is a statement about finite approximations, not about reaching infinity.
Calculus
sup, inf
Supremum / Infimum
sup = "the smallest number that's ≥ everything in the set" (least upper bound). inf = "the largest number that's ≤ everything in the set" (greatest lower bound). Like max and min, but they work even when the max/min isn't attained. ||f||∞ = sup|f(x)| is the largest value f takes.
Calculus
O(·)
Big-O notation
"Grows no faster than." f(n) = O(n²) means f grows at most like n² for large n. Hides constant factors. Used for asymptotic analysis: "the correction terms are O(k−1)" means they shrink like 1/k.
Calculus
~
Asymptotic equivalence
f(n) ~ g(n) means f(n)/g(n) → 1 as n → ∞. "f grows at the same rate as g." Example: (n+1)n+1/nn ~ e(n+1) means the ratio approaches e(n+1) for large n.
Calculus
L², L∞, Hs
Function spaces
L²: functions whose square is integrable (∫|f|² < ∞). L∞: bounded functions (sup|f| < ∞). Hs: Sobolev space (s derivatives are in L²). The regularity functional 𝒩m is FTD's native Sobolev analogue for dyadic shell sums.

∑|ak| < ∞
Absolute convergence
"The sum converges even when you take absolute values." Guarantees the sum doesn't depend on the order of terms. The Weierstrass M-test uses this to prove uniform convergence: if |akψk(t)| ≤ 2|ak| and ∑|ak| < ∞, then the series converges uniformly to a continuous function.
