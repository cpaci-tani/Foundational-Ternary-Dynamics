# Fabrication Protocol: Al-Cu-Fe-Bi Metamaterial

**Target Phase**: Icosahedral Quasicrystal (i-phase) with Interstitial Bismuth
**Stoichiometry**: $Al_{62} Cu_{25} Fe_{12} Bi_{1}$ (approximate atomic %)

## 1. Experimental Safety
*   **Hazards**: Bismuth fumes are toxic. Aluminum powder is explosive.
*   **Requirement**: Inert atmosphere glovebox (Argon) and fume hood.

## 2. Precursors (High Purity > 99.99%)
*   **Aluminum (Al)**: Shot or rod.
*   **Copper (Cu)**: Oxygen-free shot.
*   **Iron (Fe)**: Iron powder or small shards.
*   **Bismuth (Bi)**: Shot.

## 3. Melting Procedure (Arc Melting or Induction)
*   **Step A: The Base Master Alloy (Al-Cu-Fe)**
    1.  Weigh Al, Cu, Fe to ratio $Al_{63}Cu_{25}Fe_{12}$.
    2.  Place in water-cooled copper hearth (Arc Melter).
    3.  Purge chamber with Argon (3x vacuum/fill cycles).
    4.  Melt and flip sample 4-5 times to ensure homogeneity.
    5.  Result: A crystalline "beta-phase" ingot.

*   **Step B: The Bismuth Doping**
    1.  Add the Bismuth shot to the ingot.
    2.  **CRITICAL**: Bismuth boils at roughly the melting point of Iron.
    3.  **Technique**: Use a "Suction Casting" or rapid Induction melt to incorporate the Bi without boiling it off.
    4.  Quench immediately onto a chilled copper wheel (**Melt Spinning**).
    5.  **Result**: Rapidly solidified ribbons. This freezes the atoms in a disordered state, preventing Bismuth segregation.

## 4. The Annealing (Creating the Quasicrystal)
The amorphous/crystalline ribbons must be transformed into the Quasicrystal.
1.  Seal ribbons in a quartz ampoule under vacuum.
2.  **Heat Treatment**:
    *   Ramp to **700°C** over 2 hours.
    *   Hold at **700°C** for **12 hours**. (This forms the i-phase).
    *   Ramp down to **600°C** and hold for **12 hours**. (This orders the Phason defects).
    *   Quench in water.

## 5. Verification (XRD)
*   Perform X-Ray Diffraction.
*   **Success Signature**: You should NOT see the periodic peaks of simple crystals. You should see the sharp, non-periodic peaks arranged by the **Golden Mean ($\tau$)**.
*   If you see sharp Bragg peaks at non-integer indices: **You have the Hull Material.**
